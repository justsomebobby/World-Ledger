King Edric and Queen Lyra stand at the head of a long, polished table. But they are not alone.

Three other figures are seated around it.

To the Queen's right sits a man in dark, formal robes embroidered with silver thread. He is perhaps fifty, with sharp, intelligent eyes and a neatly trimmed grey beard.



**You say**

> You say, "Pardon my interruption and directness but who might these 3 be?"


Queen Lyra meets your gaze, her expression unreadable. "You have returned swiftly," she says, her voice cool. "And you have brought a guest." Her eyes flick to Finn, taking in his ink-stained hands and worn clothes.

The man in the silver-embroidered robes stands. He is tall, with an air of quiet authority. "I am Lord Alistair Vance," he says, his voice smooth and measured. "Master of Coin for the Royal Treasury." He gives a shallow, precise bow.



**You say**

> You say, "Ah so that's the situation, how far into this discussion are we? Should I take place in this conversation or would you prefer I simply wait a moment for this business to be finished {the king before you left said he'd be replacing this man with Lord Garvin, the old treasurer before this man took over and began taking bribes}"


King Edric's jaw tightens. He shares a quick, loaded glance with Lyra. The Queen's expression is one of sharp, controlled focus.

Lord Vance's eyes narrow almost imperceptibly. He does not sit back down.

"We are discussing the state of the royal coffers," the King says, his voice carefully neutral. "And the... irregularities... discovered in recent transactions."



Lord Vance's gaze shifts from Edric to you. There is a cold calculation in his eyes, the kind of look a banker gives a ledger that doesn't balance.

"Irregularities," he repeats, the word smooth as oiled silk. "A curious term for a matter of state finance. I was just explaining to Their Majesties that the... sudden cessation of tribute payments to Lord Thorne's interests has created a significant... gap in the kingdom's projected outlays."



This man is discussing the slave trade like it was standard business or taxes for the kingdom, and he's ignoring the direct small fortune amassed by seizing Thorne's assets, you're genuinely curious as to how this will play out.

 The Queen's gaze is ice. "A gap, Lord Vance? You speak as if we misplaced a purse. These were bribes. Payments to a slaver."

Lord Vance spreads his hands, a gesture of practiced reason. "Your Majesty, I speak only of the ledgers. The Crown's commitments—treaties, garrisons, supply lines—were balanced against certain... predictable inflows. Removing them without a phased plan risks instability."



He's genuinely proposing that the lack of slavery and bribes is a problem. Very directly, it's almost funny if it wasn't so corrupt and disgusting.

 The Master of Coin's words hang in the air, a testament to the rot you've been ripping out by the roots. He's not defending Thorne, not exactly. He's defending the system that allowed Thorne to exist.

You step further into the room, letting the heavy door swing shut behind Finn, Eira, and Rylan.



**You say**

> You say, "Your majesties may I cut this discussion short? It seems rather counter productive when we have important matters to discuss."


Queen Lyra's eyes cut to you, sharp as a blade. "Speak."

Lord Vance stiffens. "The financial stability of the kingdom is hardly 'counter-productive,' necromancer. It is the foundation upon which all else rests."

**You say**

> You say, "Lord Vance you've built the nations finances around slavery and brides, you've come to plead your case that these are essential, but you fail to realize Thorne will be executed for his acts of treason that you're actively defending in front of the crown. You are committing high treason in front of your King and Queen and doing it shamelessly. I'd ask that you leave before I escort you to a cell myself."

Lord Vance's face goes very still. The polished, bureaucratic mask cracks for an instant, revealing a cold, furious intelligence beneath. He doesn't look at the King or Queen. His eyes are locked on you.

"You accuse me of treason," he says, his voice dropping to a soft, dangerous register. "Based on what? The rantings of a criminal lord? The interpretation of a... foreign sorcerer?" He gestures dismissively toward you. "The ledgers I keep are clean."



**You say**

> You say, "Yes yes those iron shipments were very clean ledgers weren't they? The whole operation was very clean on your part. It was also selling the citizens of this kingdom. Something you're implying is essential for the safety of the nation. You're pleading with the crown to sell their own people. You have stones I'll give you that but otherwise I have very little to say to you. I had a summons to be here at this time, I honestly don't remember any plans to summon you though? Did you come just because you missed a bribe you were expecting this morning?"


Lord Vance's nostrils flare. The calm, oily demeanor is gone, replaced by a rigid, simmering anger. "You speak of things you do not understand. The stability of a kingdom is not built on sentiment. It is built on coin, on treaties, on strength. Thorne provided a service. He kept the peace with powers you cannot fathom."

Queen Lyra stands, her hands flat on the table. "Enough." Her voice is a whip-crack. "Lord Vance, you will leave this chamber."



Lord Vance's eyes dart from Lyra to Edric, then back to you. For a moment, it looks like he might argue further. Then his shoulders drop a fraction, the fight draining out of him, replaced by a cold, resigned calculation. He gives a stiff, shallow bow.

"As Your Majesty commands," he says, his voice flat. He turns on his heel and walks toward the doors without another glance at you.



The doors close behind Lord Vance with a soft, definitive thud. The silence in the council chamber is thick and heavy.

King Edric lets out a long, weary breath, sinking back into his chair. He looks older than he did this morning. "I've already summoned Lord Garvin to resume his post," he says, his voice gravelly. "He's an old man, but his ledgers were honest."

Queen Lyra remains standing, her gaze fixed on the door Vance just exited.
