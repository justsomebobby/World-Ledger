AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 19: Level 1–1000 Scaling Matrix, Freeform Gameplay Scaling, Player Power Bands, and Threat Alignment

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 19 defines what player level actually means in AIDRPG.

Earlier chunks defined the architecture, safety systems, combat validation, cards, Context, commands, inventory, quests, abilities, and world memory.

This chunk begins the mechanical calibration layer.

This chunk answers:

How strong is a Level 1 character?

How strong is a Level 100 character?

How strong is a Level 1000 character?

How does AIDRPG scale freeform AI Dungeon gameplay?

How does the system judge a creature, monster, NPC, boss, slime, wolf, goblin, dragon, demon lord, god, or unknown entity?

How does player level connect to enemy threat tier?

How does level affect social situations, travel, crafting, investigation, domain work, and combat?

How should AIDRPG handle real AI Dungeon inputs where the player does not say “attack,” “use skill,” or “roll,” but instead writes naturally?

The central rule is:

AIDRPG level is a story-scale and capability-scale marker, not a rigid turn-based combat value.

Level helps judge what is plausible.

It does not replace context.

A Level 1000 player can still be restrained, tricked, poisoned, socially trapped, cursed, outmaneuvered, emotionally compromised, or countered.

A Level 5 player can still survive a massive threat through luck, stealth, allies, terrain, negotiation, divine intervention, or escape.

⸻

2. What the uploaded gameplay examples show

The example stories make one thing clear:

AIDRPG must be designed for freeform narrative play, not menu-only gameplay.

The player often writes things like:

* asking an interface or spirit for information
* checking stats naturally
* asking what the world is like
* trying to help an NPC stand
* stepping into a social conflict
* using real-world knowledge in a fantasy world
* improvising brawling tactics
* probing an enemy for openings
* trying to interact with strange creatures instead of instantly attacking
* reacting to slimes, monsters, hostile humans, beastfolk, social rumors, and unfamiliar systems
* asking questions instead of issuing formal commands
* making plans in prose
* using unusual ideas from stories or anime logic

So the scaling system cannot assume every gameplay moment is:

Player attacks monster.
Monster takes damage.
Player gains XP.

Real play is more like:

Player notices a strange situation.
Player asks questions.
AI reveals social or physical context.
Player improvises.
AI responds with partial success, danger, or new information.
AIDRPG decides what consequences are real.

This means level scaling must support:

* social scenes
* ambiguous danger
* nonlethal conflict
* improvised combat
* training through action
* in-world stat interfaces
* unknown creatures
* strange powers
* monster biology
* party/NPC involvement
* player creativity
* AI narration that may exaggerate or contradict itself

The level system must be flexible enough for all of that.

⸻

3. What level represents

Level represents the player’s accumulated overall growth.

It includes:

* survival experience
* combat experience
* physical conditioning
* magical growth
* skill development
* domain growth
* danger exposure
* quest accomplishments
* major story milestones
* adaptation to the world
* practical knowledge
* body/soul/system progression
* increasing ability to survive higher-scale situations

Level does not directly mean:

* the player is better at every specific action
* the player automatically knows every skill
* the player can ignore injury
* the player can ignore poison
* the player can defeat any lower-level enemy instantly
* the player cannot be socially manipulated
* the player cannot be trapped
* the player cannot fail

Level is the broadest measure.

Stats, abilities, resources, race/species, items, and context decide the details.

⸻

4. Level is not the same as skill

A Level 30 brawler may be physically stronger than a Level 10 duelist, but the duelist may still have better sword technique.

A Level 80 mage may have enormous MP but poor hand-to-hand ability.

A Level 200 noble strategist may have high Leadership and INT but low direct ATK.

A Level 500 slime-like character may be almost impossible to stab normally but vulnerable to fire, salt, purification, or containment.

A Level 1000 god-touched warrior may be terrifying in open battle but still vulnerable to oaths, cosmic law, sealing rituals, or emotional leverage.

So AIDRPG must not judge by level alone.

Level creates the expected scale band.

Stats and context decide the actual scene result.

AIDRPG should treat Level 1–1000 as a long campaign scale.

The level bands are:

Level Band	Scale Name	Story Meaning
1–5	Ordinary / Newly Awakened	barely above normal person, survival-focused
6–10	Novice	beginning adventurer, can handle minor threats
11–20	Apprentice	trained enough to survive low danger
21–35	Early Adventurer	can handle real danger with care
36–50	Adventurer	competent in dangerous work
51–75	Seasoned Adventurer	reliable against standard threats
76–100	Veteran	strong regional combatant
101–150	Elite	above ordinary warriors and monsters
151–200	Heroic	major local hero / boss-fighting range
201–300	Legendary Mortal	can challenge legendary threats with preparation
301–400	Mythic Hero	can influence regions and major factions
401–500	Epic / National Scale	can face threats that end towns or armies
501–650	Ascendant	beyond normal mortal limits

Important:

AIDRPG should not rush players through these bands.

Most normal campaigns may never need Level 1000.

Level 1000 exists for extreme isekai, cultivation, divine, cosmic, anime, or long-form power fantasy campaigns.

Definitions:

Trivial Threats
Usually not dangerous unless there are special conditions, swarms, poison, surprise, curses, environment, or story constraints.

Fair Threats
Can be fought normally. Outcome depends on stats, resources, equipment, tactics, and narration.

Dangerous Threats
Victory should require cost, leverage, skill, party support, terrain, or preparation.

Overwhelming Threats
Clean victory should usually be rejected or softened unless strong leverage exists.

8. The freeform scaling rule

AI Dungeon does not always present enemies with clear levels.

The system must infer threat from context.

AIDRPG should estimate threat using:

* creature type
* size
* behavior
* intelligence
* number
* environment
* rarity
* narration tone
* local world logic
* known lore
* previous encounters
* creature card if one exists
* race/species traits
* magical properties
* durability
* attack method
* social authority
* faction support
* whether NPCs fear it
* whether it has defeated others
* whether it is named
* whether it is a boss
* whether it is story-critical

Example:

A “slime” is not always Tier 1.

Possible slime tiers:

Slime Type	Likely Tier
tiny harmless slime	0–1
basic wild slime	1–2
acidic slime	2–3
tainted slime swarm	3–5 depending count
intelligent slime symbiote	4–7 depending abilities
dungeon core slime	5–8
ancient devouring slime	8–10
godlike primordial slime	10+

So the system cannot say:

“Slime = weak.”

It must ask:

What kind of slime?

How many?

What does the scene say it can do?

Is it dormant, hostile, acidic, magical, intelligent, bonded, corrupted, or boss-like?

⸻

10. Threat estimate confidence

Threat estimates should have confidence.

Confidence	Meaning
Unknown	almost no information
Low	creature type known but traits unclear
Moderate	enough context to estimate danger
High	repeated observation or known lore
Confirmed	stats/lore/card/direct evidence confirms threat

Example:

The player sees “a strange wolf with burning eyes.”

Confidence:

Low to moderate.

The system should not fully know its exact tier yet.

If the wolf later breathes fire and resists arrows, threat estimate rises.

11. Level and freeform player input

The level system must interpret natural actions, not only commands.

Examples of freeform input:

I try to calm him down.

Level relevance:

* level usually matters little
* Social Domain, INT, reputation, species, and context matter more

I throw a quick jab to test his guard.

Level relevance:

* ATK, SPD, EP, combat domain, enemy skill, and level matter
* this is a probing action, not full lethal attack

I ask the spirit what my stats mean.

Level relevance:

* no combat effect
* may reveal player sheet/interface
* may update knowledge/context

I use what I remember from boxing to throw him off.

Level relevance:

* real-world skill may create a talent/candidate
* actual success depends on enemy style and context
* may grant Brawling/Martial Domain evidence

I try to turn the slime into armor because I remember stories from my world.

Level relevance:

* very unusual ability/crafting/creature interaction
* success depends on slime traits, magic, risk, control, compatibility
* not automatically valid just because creative
* may create ability/crafting candidate or dangerous symbiosis event

The matrix must allow creative attempts without making every creative attempt automatically work.

15. Why resources must scale high

If HP, MP, and EP stay low while stats scale high, high-level play becomes unstable.

Every hit becomes instant death or every ability becomes free.

Resources must scale so that:

* minor actions are cheap
* meaningful abilities cost noticeable resources
* high-tier enemies can still threaten
* long fights matter
* overuse has consequences
* recovery matters
* attrition matters
* resource exhaustion can create danger even at high level

Example:

A Level 1000 mage with 40,000 MP can cast many minor spells.

But a mythic ritual may cost 15,000 MP.

A failed divine counterspell may burn 25,000 MP and cause backlash.

So high numbers still matter.

16. Level and social power

Level should not only affect combat.

At higher levels, the player’s presence may affect social scenes.

But social effects depend on reputation, titles, faction knowledge, and witnesses.

Level Band	Social Meaning
1–10	unknown nobody
11–35	minor capable stranger
36–75	locally competent adventurer
76–150	respected or feared if deeds are known
151–300	heroic reputation possible
301–500	regional legend if public
501–800	mythic figure if known
801–1000	world-scale presence, divine or cosmic recognition possible

Important:

Level alone should not make everyone know the player.

A hidden Level 500 hermit may be unknown.

A Level 30 hero who publicly saved a town may be famous locally.

Reputation systems decide social recognition.

Level only affects what kind of reputation is plausible.

* resistance to deception
* domain knowledge
* world-scale pattern recognition

21. Level and class/background

The uploaded examples include class-like identity such as Brawler, Monk, and special abilities like quick learning or a localized spirit/interface.

AIDRPG should support class/background as build bias, not a hard cage.

Class/background may affect:

* starting stat spread
* starting domain familiarity
* ability candidates
* social interpretation
* training speed
* default equipment
* resource emphasis
* story expectations

Example:

Human Brawler

Likely starting bias:

* higher ATK
* higher EP
* moderate DEF
* moderate SPD
* lower MP
* early Brawling/Martial Domain
* may develop talents from real-world fighting experience

Human Monk

Likely starting bias:

* balanced ATK/DEF/SPD
* better EP
* possible spiritual/discipline skills
* may have higher MP if setting treats monks as spiritual

Quick Learner ability

Likely effect:

* faster skill/talent/domain growth
* more ability evidence from practice
* does not grant instant mastery
* anti-farm still applies
* repeated trivial action still reduces rewards

Localized Spirit / Interface

Likely effect:

* improved information access
* status display
* world explanations
* maybe analysis support
* does not automatically solve combat or grant stats

These should be formalized later in ability and class chunks.

22. Level and race/species hook

Chunk 20 will define race/species in detail.

Chunk 19 must define the hook:

The level matrix assumes base human-like scaling.

Race/species modifies:

* base stat floor
* stat growth bias
* resource scaling
* injury logic
* recovery logic
* equipment compatibility
* vulnerabilities
* natural abilities
* body shape
* lifespan/age logic
* size
* senses
* movement
* environment tolerance

Examples:

A fairy Level 20 and a human Level 20 should not have identical HP assumptions.

A giant Level 20 and human Level 20 should not have identical ATK/DEF assumptions.

An undead Level 20 and human Level 20 should not handle poison, fatigue, bleeding, or healing the same way.

A slime Level 20 and human Level 20 should not handle stabbing, armor, digestion, or body damage the same way.

However:

Race/species should not become infinite free power.

A dragonkin may have higher base durability, but also:

* equipment issues
* social consequences
* hunger/heat/cold constraints
* targeted weaknesses
* slower growth in some stats
* instinct risks
* faction danger

Race modifies the matrix.

It does not erase the matrix.

Tier:

group tier 3–6 depending count

Traits:

* individual weak
* group can block path
* can surround
* area effects matter
* fire or freezing may be valuable leverage

Symbiotic slime

Tier:

4–8 depending intelligence and bond

Traits:

* can become armor, weapon, parasite, companion, curse, or body modification
* requires validation
* may grant abilities
* may impose risks
* may need actor/item/body hybrid handling

Ancient/devouring slime

Tier:

8–10

Traits:

* absorbs matter
* adapts
* resists normal weapons
* may require special weakness

Important:

If the player attempts to wear or bond with a slime, AIDRPG should not treat that as simple item pickup.

It may involve:

* body condition
* creature interaction
* ability candidate
* item/equipment transformation
* symbiosis contract
* infection risk
* corruption risk
* new passive candidate
* actor/entity record if intelligent

This is exactly why freeform scaling matters.

26. Human or humanoid enemy scaling

Human-like enemies are common in AI Dungeon.

They must scale by role, not just species.

Humanoid Type	Default Tier
civilian	0–1
drunk thug	1–2
novice bandit	2

veteran guard	4
elite soldier	5
assassin	4–6
battle mage	4–7
named champion	6–8
legendary swordsman	8–9
divine hero	9–10

A normal human can be more dangerous than a monster if they have:

* training
* weapons
* armor
* magic
* poison
* tactics
* authority
* allies
* terrain
* surprise
* faction backing

Example:

A player may fight a physically ordinary human in a back alley.

If the human is trained, precise, and tactically superior, the system should not treat him as Tier 1 just because he is human.

A skilled human opponent may be Tier 3–5 even without magic.

27. Social conflict scaling

The apartment/social story example shows another important point:

Not every danger is a monster.

Social scenes can include:

* rumors
* reputation pressure
* neighbor gossip
* suspicion
* social exclusion
* authority complaints
* public perception
* legal/HOA/faction pressure
* misunderstanding
* manipulation
* kindness or trust-building

AIDRPG should scale social conflict separately from combat.

Social difficulty factors:

* number of witnesses
* public/private setting
* existing reputation
* social power of target
* faction/authority involvement
* evidence
* player charisma/social domain if used
* player level only if reputation or aura is known
* truth versus rumor
* stakes
* social norms

A Level 100 warrior does not automatically win an apartment rumor conflict.

They may intimidate people, but that could worsen reputation.

A low-level player can succeed socially through kindness, evidence, timing, or empathy.

So level is secondary in social scenes.

28. Level and relationship scenes

AI Dungeon often responds strongly to tone and interaction.

AIDRPG should recognize:

* respectful assistance
* threats
* kindness
* deception
* interrogation
* flirting
* intimidation
* comfort
* apology
* bargain
* refusal
* promise

Level may affect confidence or aura, but actor relationship changes should be based on:

* what the player did
* what the actor saw
* the actor’s values
* the situation
* prior relationship
* reputation
* social context

Example:

Helping an injured beastfolk stand respectfully should not be judged mainly by level.

It may affect:

* actor trust
* party possibility
* quest link
* reputation if witnessed

This is why level cannot become the only universal mechanic.

29. Level and interface/status display

In isekai-style games, the player may have a visible status panel.

AIDRPG should support that.

If the story has an interface:

* /sheet may be represented as in-world status display
* AI output may describe stats
* player may ask a spirit/interface for explanations
* system can show level/EXP/resources naturally

But:

AI-generated status panels should not override state unless validated or command/manual route allows it.

Example:

AI says:

LVL: 4
HP: 38/38
MP: 107.5/107.5
EP: 77.15/98
ATK: 9
DEF: 9
SPD: 9
LCK: 9
INT: 9

If this is initial setup and accepted by scenario, it may initialize state.

If later AI randomly outputs impossible stats, validator should check against state.

If command /sheet outputs stats, parser must ignore that output as gameplay.

Status displays are mirrors unless used during controlled initialization/manual override.

30. Level initialization rules

When a new game starts, AIDRPG may initialize level in several ways.

Default start

If no story setup says otherwise:

Level: 1
Stats: base human average
Resources: base Level 1 values
XP: 0

Scenario-defined start

If the scenario says the player starts at Level 4 as a Human Monk, this may be accepted during initialization.

Reincarnation / system start

If an in-world system grants class, level, race, or ability, it may initialize state if:

* source is setup narration
* not dream-only
* not quoted
* not contradicted
* player accepts it as start
* migration/manual state does not override it

Manual start

Player may use commands or setup fields.

Example:

/setlevel 4
/setrace Human
/setclass Monk

These commands should log manual initialization.

High-level start

If player starts at Level 100 or 1000, this must be explicit.

AIDRPG should not infer high level from vague phrases like “I am powerful.”

32. Level scaling with player creativity

AIDRPG should reward creative play, but safely.

Creative action can improve outcome if:

* it fits the scene
* it uses real leverage
* it uses known abilities/items
* it exploits enemy weakness
* it changes terrain
* it creates social pressure
* it creates surprise
* it accepts cost/risk
* it is supported by narration

Creative action should not automatically bypass level.

Example:

Level 5 player says:

I use anime logic to turn the slime into armor.

Possible outcomes:

* failed attempt
* slime burns/irritates skin
* slime partially coats arm
* ability candidate appears
* symbiosis risk begins
* slime armor works briefly but unstable
* success if story strongly supports it

Not automatic:

* permanent legendary living armor
* huge stat gain
* stable passive immunity
* full monster companion card

Creativity creates proposals.

Validation decides permanence.

Leverage types:

* terrain

* poison
* ritual
* artifact
* enemy weakness
* party support
* faction support
* surprise
* hostage/social leverage
* environmental hazard
* previous enemy injury
* research
* divine blessing
* story prophecy
* sacrifice
* prepared counter

Leverage can shift matchup.

Suggested leverage shifts:

Leverage Strength	Matchup Shift

story-arc leverage	can allow boss victory

Level 30 player versus adult dragon:

Base:

Impossible without leverage.

With dragon-slaying spear, ritual trap, wounded dragon, and party support:

Could become dangerous but possible.

36. Effective level

AIDRPG should use effective level for matchup, not raw level.

Effective level is raw level modified by current conditions.

Factors that lower effective level:

* low HP
* low MP
* low EP
* injuries
* fatigue
* poison
* disease
* fear
* broken equipment
* missing weapon
* bad terrain
* restrained
* surprise
* poor matchup
* unavailable ability
* wrong damage type

Factors that raise effective level:

* strong equipment
* relevant ability
* terrain advantage
* preparation
* party support
* enemy weakness
* high morale
* surprise
* favorable weather
* divine blessing
* buff
* domain expertise

Example:

Level 100 warrior with 0 EP, broken shield, and poisoned body may fight like Level 50 or worse.

Level 30 hunter with traps, high ground, silver arrows, and knowledge of a werewolf’s weakness may threaten a Tier 5 werewolf above normal level.

38. High-level play design

Level 500–1000 should not become:

I swing. Everything dies.

High-level play should shift from ordinary survival to scale management.

High-level threats should include:

* counters
* resource pressure
* divine law
* cosmic rules
* sealing
* social consequences
* collateral damage
* protecting others
* multiple simultaneous objectives
* corruption
* hostile domains
* reality instability
* enemies that cannot be solved by damage alone
* moral choices
* faction consequences
* world entity interactions

Example:

A Level 900 player can destroy a demon army.

But can they do it without:

* destroying the city?
* killing civilians?
* triggering divine punishment?
* exhausting MP before the demon lord arrives?
* letting the cult complete the ritual?
* corrupting their own soul?

This keeps high-level play meaningful.

39. Low-level play design

Level 1–20 should feel grounded.

Low-level play should involve:

* survival
* learning systems
* basic resources
* cautious combat
* social orientation
* city navigation
* first income
* shelter
* simple quests
* weak monsters
* training
* first ability attempts
* first injury consequences
* first card creation only when important

A Level 1 player should not casually defeat demon lords.

But they should be able to:

* ask questions
* avoid danger
* help people
* train
* discover abilities
* make allies
* use clever leverage
* survive lucky encounters
* gain meaningful early XP

This matches real AI Dungeon starts where the player asks what is happening and learns the world.

43. Level and XP expectation by band

Chunk 22 will define exact XP.

Chunk 19 defines pacing expectations.

51–100	slower, requires real danger or objectives

44. Level-up validation

A level-up should happen only when:

* XP reaches threshold
* operation ledger confirms no duplicate
* ProgressionReducer commits level-up
* stats/resources update according to rules
* dirty targets are marked
* player sheet updates
* protected cards update
* /debuglast can explain it

Level-up should not happen from:

* AI saying “you feel stronger” alone
* dream sequence
* quote
* card text
* rumor
* duplicate reward
* command output
* old migration unless explicit

AI may narrate level-up flavor, but state decides level.

⸻

45. Level and story card behavior

Level changes should update protected cards.

Cards affected:

* Player Sheet
* Body & Status if max resources change
* Abilities if level unlocks ability slot/rank
* Quest Log if level-up came from quest
* maybe generated ability cards if evolution occurs

Generated cards should not be created merely because level increased.

Example:

Level 10 reached.

Do not create:

* “Level 10 Card”
* “Power Card”
* “Stats Card”

Instead update Player Sheet.

If Level 10 unlocks Mana Slash stabilization, AbilityReducer/CardSync may create Mana Slash card if it is stable and important.

⸻

46. Level and Context behavior

Context should include level only when relevant.

Good Context:

You are Level 4, still an early-stage adventurer. The demon lord is far beyond your current capabilities.

Good Context:

You are Level 120 and exhausted; despite your experience, your EP is nearly gone.

Bad Context:

Level 4, XP 0/100, Total XP 0, ATK 9, DEF 9, SPD 9, INT 9, LCK 9...

Unless the player asks for status or debug.

Normal Context should use level to guide plausibility, not dump numbers every turn.

⸻

47. Level and AI Dungeon response correction

If AI Dungeon exaggerates beyond level, AIDRPG should correct gently.

Example:

Level 4 player output:

You instantly annihilate the demon lord with one punch.

Validation:

* reject or soften victory
* possible dream/vision/flavor
* no demon lord defeat
* Context correction:

The King of Demons is far beyond your current level; direct victory is not plausible without major leverage.

Example:

Level 300 player output:

A common rat kills you instantly.

Validation:

* reject or soften unless poison, curse, surprise, or special rat context supports it

Correction:

A normal rat is not a lethal threat to you unless unusual conditions apply.

This keeps AI Dungeon freeform but grounded.

51. Final Chunk 19 doctrine

AIDRPG level is a scale marker.

It tells the system what kind of story the player has grown into.

Level 1 is fragile.

Level 10 is novice.

Level 50 is a real adventurer.

Level 100 is veteran.

Level 200 is heroic.

Level 500 is epic.

Level 1000 is world-scale.

But level is never the only answer.

Freeform AI Dungeon play requires context.

A slime may be harmless, acidic, tainted, symbiotic, or godlike.

A human may be civilian, trained killer, archmage, or divine champion.

A social rumor may threaten a high-level player more than a sword.

A low-level player may survive a high-tier threat through leverage.

A high-level player may still fail if exhausted, countered, trapped, cursed, or socially outmaneuvered.

AIDRPG should scale the world through:

* level
* stats
* resources
* race/species
* abilities
* equipment
* creature traits
* numbers
* terrain
* preparation
* story leverage
* reputation
* current state
* validation evidence

The goal is not to make a rigid table decide the story.

The goal is to give the validator enough structure to keep freeform gameplay believable, fair, scalable, and safe.
