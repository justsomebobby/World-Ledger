AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 23: Creature Tier Matrix, Enemy Stat Bands, Boss Scaling, Group Threat, and Encounter Difficulty

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 23 defines how AIDRPG scales enemies, monsters, NPC opponents, bosses, swarms, creatures, and unknown threats.

Chunk 19 defined player level scale.

Chunk 20 defined race/species scaling.

Chunk 21 defined player stats and resources.

Chunk 22 defined XP and progression.

Chunk 23 answers:

How strong is a wolf?

How strong is a goblin?

How strong is a guard?

How strong is a slime?

How strong is an ogre, troll, demon, dragon, lich, god avatar, or cosmic entity?

How does AIDRPG estimate an enemy when AI Dungeon invents something new?

How does enemy tier relate to player level?

How do groups scale?

How do bosses scale?

How do enemy stats, HP, MP, EP, abilities, resistances, and weaknesses work?

How does AIDRPG avoid treating every creature name as a fixed power level?

The central rule is:

Enemy scaling is based on threat context, not name alone.

A “slime” can be harmless, acidic, intelligent, corrupted, symbiotic, dungeon-core, ancient, or godlike.

A “human” can be a civilian, guard, assassin, archmage, champion, avatar, or divine vessel.

AIDRPG must estimate threat from the full scene.

⸻

2. Core philosophy

AI Dungeon creates enemies freely.

It may say:

* a wolf appears
* thirteen goblins attack
* a strange slime pulses near the tower
* a guard blocks the way
* an old man draws a sword
* a dragon descends
* the demon king smiles
* a goddess tests you
* a cosmic shadow watches from the sky
* a swarm of insects fills the room
* a bandit captain calls for reinforcements
* the slime tries to bond with your arm
* a child-sized creature emits killing intent

AIDRPG cannot rely on names only.

It must consider:

* enemy species
* enemy size
* enemy number
* enemy equipment
* enemy intelligence
* enemy training
* enemy abilities
* enemy behavior
* enemy environment
* enemy story role
* enemy condition
* enemy resistance
* enemy weakness
* player level
* player resources
* party support
* leverage
* whether the enemy is named
* whether locals fear it
* whether it has already shown power

The enemy system should produce a Threat Estimate first.

Hard enemy stats can be generated only when enough information exists or when the story/card explicitly defines them.

⸻

3. Enemy tier overview

AIDRPG uses enemy threat tiers.

Recommended enemy tiers:

Tier	Name	Meaning
0	Harmless / Noncombatant	not a real combat threat unless context changes
1	Trivial Threat	dangerous only to fragile or careless characters
2	Minor Threat	weak monster or ordinary low-trained attacker
3	Standard Threat	dangerous to novices, ordinary trained combatant or beast
4	Serious Threat	skilled fighter, dangerous monster, strong pack
5	Elite Threat	high danger, ogre-class, elite soldier, strong monster
6	Boss Threat	major monster, named champion, troll, lesser demon boss
7	Legendary Threat	dragonling, ancient knight, major demon, large boss
8	Mythic Threat	adult dragon, lich, archmage, ancient monster
9	Worldly Mythic Threat	ancient dragon, demon lord lieutenant, demigod beast
10	World-Scale Threat	god avatar, sealed titan, world-ending beast
10+	Cosmic / Divine / Absolute	full gods, cosmic entities, reality-scale threats

Tiers are not exact.

They are scale categories.

An enemy can have:

* individual tier
* group tier
* boss tier
* environmental tier
* social/legal tier
* unknown/pending tier

Example:

A plague rat may be Tier 1 physically but Tier 5 as a disease hazard.

A noble magistrate may be Tier 0 physically but Tier 5 socially.

A slime swarm may be Tier 1 individually but Tier 5 as a group.

4. Enemy effective level range by tier

Each tier maps roughly to a player-level equivalent.

Enemy Tier	Effective Level Range	Typical Meaning
0	0–1	harmless civilian, noncombatant, ordinary animal
1	1–3	rat, weak pest, fragile enemy
2	3–8	goblin, stray dog, weak bandit, basic slime
3	8–18	wolf, trained thug, guard, small pack
4	18–35	trained soldier, large wolf, dangerous beast, bandit captain
5	35–75	ogre, elite soldier, strong monster, serious magical beast
6	75–150	troll, named champion, lesser demon boss, powerful mage
7	150–300	dragonling, ancient warrior, major demon, young giant boss
8	300–500	adult dragon, lich, archmage, legendary monster
9	500–800	ancient dragon, demigod beast, demon lord lieutenant
10	800–1000+	god avatar, sealed titan, world-scale entity
10+	1000+	full deity, cosmic entity, reality-scale being

This effective level helps XP and matchup estimation.

It is not always literal character level.

A Tier 6 monster might not have a “level” in-world.

It has an equivalent scale for validation.

5. Enemy stat band by tier

Enemy stats should scale similarly to player stats, but enemies can be more focused.

Enemy stats:

* ATK
* DEF
* SPD
* INT
* LCK

Suggested individual enemy stat ranges:

Tier	Weak Stat	Average Stat	Strong Stat	Extreme Stat
0	1–3	3–5	5–8	8–10
1	2–5	5–8	8–12	12–16
2	5–10	10–18	18–28	28–40
3	10–20	20–40	40–65	65–90
4	25–45	45–85	85–140	140–200
5	50–90	90–170	170–280	280–400
6	100–180	180–350	350–600	600–850
7	180–325	325–650	650–1100	1100–1600
8	325–600	600–1200	1200–2000	2000–3000
9	600–1000	1000–2000	2000–3500	3500–5000
10	1000–1800	1800–3500	3500–6000	6000–9000
10+	1800+	3500+	6000+	special/cosmic

Important:

Enemies should not always use balanced stats.

A troll may have:

* high ATK
* high DEF
* low SPD
* low INT
* average LCK

A dragon may have:

* high ATK
* high DEF
* high SPD through flight
* high INT
* high MP

A ghost may have:

* low physical ATK
* unusual DEF against physical
* high INT/MP
* strong spirit attacks

6. Enemy resource bands by tier

Enemies should have HP, MP, and EP or equivalent resources.

Some enemies use alternative meanings:

* HP = body integrity, mass, core, structure, essence
* MP = mana, spiritual power, divine power, infernal force
* EP = stamina, action endurance, cohesion, battery, animating force

Suggested individual enemy resource ranges:

Tier	HP Range	MP Range	EP Range
0	5–50	0–30	10–50
1	30–120	0–80	40–150
2	100–350	0–250	100–400
3	250–800	0–600	300–900
4	700–1,800	100–1,500	800–2,000
5	1,500–4,000	300–3,500	1,500–4,500
6	3,500–9,000	800–8,000	3,000–9,000
7	8,000–20,000	2,000–18,000	7,000–20,000
8	18,000–45,000	5,000–40,000	15,000–45,000
9	40,000–100,000	15,000–90,000	35,000–100,000
10	90,000–250,000	40,000–250,000	80,000–250,000
10+	special	special	special

These are for major individual enemies.

Ordinary humanoids at a tier may have lower HP than monsters but higher equipment, skill, intelligence, or tactics.

Boss modifiers can raise resources.

Group scaling is separate.

7. Tier is not only combat

An entity can have different threat dimensions.

Threat dimensions:

* physical threat
* magical threat
* social threat
* legal threat
* environmental threat
* disease/poison threat
* spiritual threat
* economic threat
* domain threat
* investigation threat
* narrative/boss threat

Example:

A plague rat:

Physical Tier: 1
Disease Tier: 5
Overall Scene Threat: depends on exposure

A noble judge:

Physical Tier: 0
Social/Legal Tier: 6
Overall Scene Threat: high if trial/reputation scene

A sealed demon statue:

Physical Tier: 0 while sealed
Spiritual/Corruption Tier: 7
Combat Tier: pending if awakened

AIDRPG should not convert every threat into sword-fight numbers.

⸻

9. Threat estimate confidence

Threat estimate confidence levels:

Confidence	Meaning
Unknown	very little information
Low	creature label known, traits unclear
Moderate	enough context for usable estimate
High	observed abilities/behavior support tier
Confirmed	card, repeated evidence, or direct stat/lore support

Confidence matters.

Low-confidence enemies should not be overdefined.

Example:

“The black wolf watches you with glowing eyes.”

Initial:

* wolf-like
* possible magical
* confidence low/moderate

After it breathes shadow fire:

* threat increases
* magical trait confirmed
* confidence high

After locals call it the Moon-Eater:

* possible boss/legendary status
* story role changes

10. Enemy stat generation from tier

If exact stats are needed, generate them from tier and archetype.

Process:

1. Determine enemy tier.
2. Determine enemy archetype.
3. Select effective level within tier.
4. Assign stat spread.
5. Apply race/species/body modifiers.
6. Apply equipment/ability modifiers.
7. Apply boss/group modifiers.
8. Apply current condition modifiers.
9. Record confidence.

Enemy archetypes:

* brute
* skirmisher
* tank
* caster
* assassin
* beast
* swarm
* guardian
* boss
* elite humanoid
* undead
* spirit
* construct
* slime/ooze
* dragon
* divine/cosmic
* social/legal threat

Example:

Tier 5 ogre brute:

* effective level 50
* high ATK
* high DEF
* low INT
* low SPD
* high HP/EP
* little MP

Tier 5 assassin:

* effective level 50
* high SPD
* high ATK
* high LCK
* low HP/DEF
* poison/equipment threat

Same tier, different danger.

13. Slime threat matrix

Slimes need special treatment because AI Dungeon often uses them in creative ways.

Slime Type	Individual Tier	Group Tier	Main Threat
harmless tiny slime	0–1	1–2	nuisance, mess, weak acid maybe
basic wild slime	1–2	2–3	engulfing, weak acid, physical weirdness
acid slime	2–4	3–5	burns, equipment damage
sticky slime	2–3	3–5	restraint, suffocation risk
poison slime	3–5	4–6	toxin/condition
tainted slime	3–5	4–7	corruption, disease, mutation
intelligent slime	4–7	varies	tactics, negotiation, symbiosis
mimic slime	4–8	varies	copying, disguise, ambush
dungeon core slime	6–8	7–9	dungeon control, spawning
ancient devouring slime	8–10	9–10	absorption, adaptation
primordial slime	10+	special	world/cosmic body logic

Slime validation rules:

* do not assume every slime is weak
* do not assume every slime is acid
* do not assume stabbing works normally
* do not assume a slime has a core unless established
* do not allow slime symbiosis as simple equipment pickup
* bonding with slime may affect body, item, actor, ability, condition, or curse systems

25. Boss scaling

Bosses are not just stronger enemies.

A boss may have:

* higher resources
* multiple phases
* special abilities
* minions
* lair effects
* resistances
* weaknesses
* named identity
* quest/case/domain links
* special defeat condition
* escape/routesecond phase
* story consequences

Boss types:

Boss Type	Resource Multiplier	Ability Complexity
elite	×1.25–1.5	one special trait
named enemy	×1.5–2	one or two special traits
minor boss	×2–3	defined ability set
major boss	×3–5	phases/minions/lair
arc boss	×5–8	major story mechanics
mythic boss	×8–15	special victory conditions
world boss	special	may require non-combat resolution

Bosses should not always die when HP hits zero if story supports alternate defeat rules.

Examples:

* lich requires phylactery
* demon requires banishment circle
* spirit requires anchor resolution
* god avatar can be banished but not killed
* troll regenerates unless countered
* slime core must be damaged
* dragon may flee if wounded

Boss victory must be validated by the boss’s defeat condition.

⸻

26. Boss phase logic

Some bosses should have phases.

Phase examples:

Physical phase

Boss fights normally.

Enraged phase

ATK/SPD increase, defense drops or risk rises.

Defensive phase

Boss shields, summons, or retreats.

Ritual phase

Boss channels spell/objective.

Desperation phase

Boss uses dangerous final ability.

Escape phase

Boss flees, transforms, or collapses lair.

Non-combat phase

Negotiation, oath, puzzle, exorcism, seal, or moral decision.

Phases should be used for major bosses, not every enemy.

AIDRPG should not invent phases unless boss scale/story supports them.

55. Enemy difficulty and validation outcomes

Enemy-related events use five outcomes.

Accept

Use when enemy fact or consequence is confirmed.

Examples:

* wolf bites player
* goblin group has thirteen members
* dragon breathes fire
* troll regenerates
* boss flees wounded

Reject

Use when unsupported.

Examples:

* normal rat kills Level 500 player instantly
* Tier 1 slime suddenly has godlike power with no setup
* player kills dragon with no leverage at Level 5
* absent enemy attacks

Pending

Use when possible but unconfirmed.

Examples:

* strange slime may be acidic
* wolf may be magical
* old man may be dangerous
* rumor of dragon nearby

Soft_fail

Use when attempt partially works.

Examples:

* player wounds troll but it regenerates
* player damages slime but does not kill core
* player scares goblins but leader holds them
* player identifies possible weakness but not enough proof

Hard_deny

Use when unsafe/non-canon.

Examples:

* card text creates enemy
* debug text creates monster
* dream enemy changes real scene
* duplicate enemy defeat reward
* junk enemy name becomes card
* unsupported instant god kill

58. Final Chunk 23 doctrine

Enemy scaling is contextual.

A creature name is not enough.

A wolf can be ordinary, cursed, divine, diseased, or boss-level.

A slime can be weak, acidic, symbiotic, corrupted, dungeon-core, or primordial.

A human can be harmless, trained, magically armed, socially powerful, or divine.

A dragon can be hatchling, adult, ancient, avatar, or god.

AIDRPG should estimate enemy threat from:

* tier
* effective level
* stats
* resources
* body type
* abilities
* resistances
* weaknesses
* number
* equipment
* intelligence
* morale
* environment
* story role
* current condition
* player level
* party
* leverage

Bosses need defeat conditions.

Groups need counts.

Swarms need mass behavior.

Spirits, slimes, undead, and constructs need body-compatible damage logic.

Social/legal threats are not solved by ATK alone.

Enemy cards should be selective and specifically triggered.

XP should depend on real challenge, not enemy name.

The goal is not to create a rigid monster manual.

The goal is to give AIDRPG enough structure to interpret freeform AI Dungeon creatures consistently, fairly, and safely.
