Name: Zack
Race: Orc

Starting Location: Large city

These are starting truths. They are real now, but the story may change them through events, injury, disguise, transformation, aging, death, or other consequences.

---

If there was a dignified way for your old life to end, this was not it.

You remember wet pavement, a horn, headlights, and the deeply unfair thought that this is a stupid way to die.

Then everything goes white.

Not heavenly white. Not peaceful white. More like someone forgot to finish drawing the room.

You are standing anyway.

You have weight. Breath. Hands. A heartbeat doing its best under very confusing circumstances.

You are Zack, a Orc, 24 years old.

Standard looking

Two people are already waiting.

The man is tall, composed, and plainly dressed, with the tired patience of someone who has been right so often that it has become a burden.

You know his name before he says it.

Serathis Vale.

The woman beside him looks warmer, brighter, and considerably more entertained. She has the pleasant expression of someone watching a vase wobble near the edge of a table, curious whether it will fall.

Elyndra Vaelis.

She smiles first.

"Well," she says, looking you over, "that could have gone worse."

Serathis does not look at her. "It still might."

"That's the spirit."

"It is not."

Their exchange is too casual to be a ceremony. Too practiced to be accidental.

Serathis turns his attention to you.

"You were not summoned by a kingdom," he says. "Not chosen by prophecy. Not bought by ritual. Not handed a clean destiny."

Elyndra tilts her head. "Which is not the same as being irrelevant."

"It is," Serathis says, "a warning against assuming importance too early."

She laughs softly. "He means welcome."

"I do not."

"You do a little."

For the first time, Serathis looks genuinely tired.

"The world you are entering was unstable before you arrived," he says. "It will not make special room for you."

Elyndra's smile softens.

"But it will notice you," she says. "Everything does, eventually."

No blessing follows. No shining sword. No promise that this will be fair.

Only a place in a living world.

Serathis steps aside as if opening a door you cannot see.

"That is enough delay."

Elyndra gives you one last look.

"Try to make something of it."

Then the white place vanishes.

Your next breath is heavier.

Large city.

Air fills your lungs with the smell of somewhere real. The ground beneath you holds. Nearby, life is already happening: voices, movement, weather, work, animals, routine.

Your old world is gone.

This one was already in motion before you arrived, and it does not pause to explain itself.

 You are standing on the cobbled edge of a wide street in what is unmistakably a city—a large one, by the noise and scale. The air is thick with the scent of wet stone, woodsmoke, baking bread, and the less pleasant undertone of crowded life. Buildings rise several stories on either side, timber-framed and stone-bottomed, their steep roofs shingled with slate or thatch. Signs creak overhead, painted with symbols for inns, smithies, chandlers, and clothiers.

 You stand on the cobbled edge of a wide street, the weight of your new reality settling into your bones. The city breathes around you—a living, noisy, smelly thing. You are an orc here, a fact made plain by your own reflection in a rain barrel nearby: broad shoulders, greenish skin, prominent tusks, a face that would look brutish to a human. You wear simple, rough-spun trousers and a tunic, no armor, no weapon, no coin. Your pockets are empty.

**You do**

> You jump until you are tired.


You blink, shake your head, and decide to test the limits of this new body.

You start jumping.

At first, it's easy. Your orcish legs are strong, propelling you a good foot or two off the cobbles with each hop. You jump up and down, ignoring the odd looks from passing humans and a dwarf who mutters something about "daft greenskins."

After a dozen jumps, your breathing quickens. After thirty, a dull burn begins in your calves and thighs. Your lungs pull in the thick city air.

 You keep going, jumping higher, pushing until your legs ache and your breath comes in ragged gasps. You're sweating now, your tunic sticking to your back. The wound in your calves—some old injury that came with this body—begins to throb sharply with each impact. It feels like a deep, persistent ache, a reminder that this body isn't pristine.

After maybe fifty jumps, your energy is flagging. Your last jump barely clears the ground.

 You stop, panting, hands on your knees. The calves wound throbs angrily now, a hot, persistent ache that digs deeper with each heartbeat. You're winded, sweat-damp, and standing in the middle of a city street while people flow around you, some giving you a wide berth, others staring openly.

Your energy is low—not dangerously so, but you've burned through a good chunk of it. The wound isn't helping.

A sharp whistle cuts through the street noise. You look up.

**You do**

> You suddenly develop Fireball and shoot a fireball into the sky.


You have no magical training, no affinity for fire, no knowledge of spellcraft, no innate ability to conjure flames. You are an orc—a being of flesh, muscle, and instinct, not a mage. You feel nothing stirring within you, no surge of power, no gathering heat in your palms. You just stand there, panting, sweating, with two aching wounds, and will a fireball into existence.

Nothing happens.
