AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 31: Named Actors, NPC Importance, Actor Memory, Relationships, Reputation, Titles, Factions, and Social Consequence Tracking

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 31 defines how AIDRPG handles people, NPCs, named actors, companions, major actors, factions, reputation, titles, relationship memory, social consequences, and actor truth.

Earlier chunks defined:

* player stats and resources
* progression
* enemies and combat
* abilities
* absorption
* crafting
* economy
* quests
* investigation and evidence

Chunk 31 answers:

How does an NPC become important?

When should a random guard become a named actor?

How does AIDRPG remember what an NPC knows, believes, witnessed, owns, promised, or did?

How does it prevent random one-line NPCs from flooding memory?

How does reputation work?

How do titles work?

How do factions remember the player?

How does the system distinguish public reputation from private relationship?

How does it track trust, fear, hostility, debts, promises, crimes, favors, betrayal, and loyalty?

How does it stop rumors, accusations, and AI hallucinations from corrupting actor memory?

The central rule is:

Actor memory should preserve confirmed social truth without turning every temporary NPC into permanent clutter.

2. Core philosophy

AI Dungeon creates people constantly.

The story may introduce:

* a guard
* a merchant
* an innkeeper
* a wounded traveler
* a suspicious noble
* a stable boy
* a village elder
* a bandit captain
* a companion
* a summoned spirit
* a worker
* a foreman
* a quest giver
* a witness
* a suspect
* a faction leader
* a rival
* a lover
* a prisoner
* a shopkeeper
* a god avatar
* a demon patron
* a talking slime
* a living sword
* a dragon
* a ghost
* a king

AIDRPG must remember important people.

But it must not permanently store every:

* unnamed guard
* random villager
* one-line shopkeeper
* background worker
* temporary enemy
* generic bandit
* generic wolf
* random witness with no future relevance
* hallucinated name
* junk actor name
* duplicate version of the same NPC

The system needs an importance filter.

The goal is to remember people who matter, while letting background actors stay lightweight.

4. Actor categories

AIDRPG should classify actors.

Background actor

A temporary person with little memory.

Examples:

* random villager
* unnamed guard
* passerby
* generic shop customer

Usually no permanent record.

Minor actor

Named or lightly relevant, but not central.

Examples:

* innkeeper who sells room
* guard who gives directions
* local merchant
* one-time witness

Can have lightweight memory.

Usually no generated card.

Supporting actor

Recurring or useful NPC.

Examples:

* friendly smith
* repeated shopkeeper
* local healer
* recurring witness
* foreman
* small quest giver

May appear in actor memory summaries.

Card only if important.

Major actor

Important recurring person.

Examples:

* companion
* rival
* patron
* major villain
* faction leader
* lover
* mentor
* noble
* recurring quest giver
* named boss
* important witness
* major suspect

Should have durable memory.

Often card eligible.

Party actor

Travels with or directly accompanies the player.

Examples:

* companion
* follower
* summon with persistent identity
* pet
* bonded spirit

Needs presence and status tracking.

Faction actor

An actor representing a faction.

Examples:

* captain of guard
* guildmaster
* priest of church
* rebel leader

Links to faction reputation.

World actor

A powerful entity whose existence affects the setting.

Examples:

* dragon sovereign
* demon lord
* god avatar
* king
* ancient lich
* world tree spirit

Usually card eligible if active.

5. NPC importance scale

AIDRPG should use an importance scale.

Importance	Meaning	Memory Behavior
0	background	no durable memory unless promoted
1	minor temporary	lightweight scene memory only
2	minor named	small actor record, no card
3	recurring/supporting	durable actor memory
4	major actor	full actor memory, card eligible
5	central actor	protected/carded, high Context priority
6	world actor	major persistent world memory

Importance should change over time.

A random guard can become important if:

* the player befriends them
* they become a witness
* they join the party
* they betray the player
* they become a recurring rival
* they are linked to a major quest
* they hold a key item
* they become a faction representative
* they are named and recur

A named actor can also become less active, but should not be forgotten if important history exists.

⸻

6. Actor promotion rules

An NPC should be promoted when one or more of these happens:

* receives a proper name
* appears repeatedly
* gives or receives a quest
* joins party
* becomes companion/follower
* becomes enemy/rival
* holds key item
* makes a promise/contract
* witnesses major event
* is suspect/witness in major case
* is emotionally important
* belongs to major faction
* controls resources/services
* has unique abilities
* survives major event with player
* receives injury/death/capture that matters
* gets a generated card candidate
* is explicitly marked important by player or system

Do not promote solely because:

* AI gives a junk name
* a random NPC says one line
* a generic enemy appears
* a background crowd member exists
* a temporary illusion speaks
* Story Card text mentions a person without scene confirmation

⸻

7. Actor demotion / archiving

Actors can be archived or lowered in active priority.

Demote/archive when:

* actor leaves story for long time
* minor actor’s role is complete
* actor is dead and no longer relevant
* actor’s quest is archived
* actor was created by mistake and invalidated
* duplicate actor merged
* background NPC no longer needed

Do not delete important history.

Archive should preserve:

* who they were
* final status
* relationship outcome
* major promises/debts
* quest/case links
* death/location if known
* unresolved threads

Example:

A minor innkeeper who gave directions may vanish from active memory.

A companion who died should remain archived and may still affect reputation, grief, or story.

9. Actor name hygiene

Actor names need validation.

Reject or avoid actor names that are:

* generic role only
* full sentence
* parser fragment
* debug text
* command output
* pronoun
* title with no identity unless intentionally title-only
* duplicate with minor spelling drift
* absurd junk unless story tone supports it
* too long
* copied from Context/card label
* “the man”
* “the guard”
* “a villager”
* “Unknown”
* “NPC”
* “Quest Giver”

Good actor names:

* Mara
* Old Bren
* Captain Mora
* Lord Veyr
* Stable Boy Tovin
* Frostmaw
* Sister Elian
* Korr the Blacksmith
* The Bone Knight
* Ashen Foreman Durn

Generic roles can exist as scene actors but should not become durable named actor records unless needed.

10. Alias and duplicate actor prevention

AI may rename the same actor.

Examples:

* Captain Mora
* Captain Morra
* Mora
* Captain Mira
* Guard Captain Mora

Duplicate detection should compare:

* name similarity
* title/role
* location
* faction
* quest link
* relationship
* recent scene
* unique facts
* actor card key
* aliases

If same actor:

* merge aliases
* preserve canonical name
* avoid duplicate card

If separate:

* keep separate, but clarify.

Example:

Mara and Mira may be different if both exist.

If AI alternates Mara/Mira for same companion, merge or flag typo depending context.

⸻

11. Actor truth status

Actor facts need truth status like investigation facts.

Truth labels:

* confirmed
* observed
* claimed by actor
* claimed by other actor
* rumored
* suspected
* disputed
* contradicted
* false
* unknown

Example:

Confirmed:

“Mara is traveling with the player.”

Claimed:

“Mara says she once served in the royal guard.”

Rumored:

“Villagers say Mara betrayed a guild.”

Suspected:

“Mara may be hiding something about the tower.”

Do not write rumors into actor memory as confirmed biography.

This is critical.

⸻

12. Actor memory layers

Actor memory should have layers.

Identity memory

Stable who/what they are.

Example:

Mara is a human swordswoman and companion.

Relationship memory

How they relate to player.

Example:

Mara trusts the player after they rescued her brother.

Event memory

Major things that happened.

Example:

Mara was wounded fighting Frostmaw.

Knowledge memory

What they know, claim, or witnessed.

Example:

Mara saw blue wax in Lord Veyr’s study.

Obligation memory

Promises, debts, contracts, favors.

Example:

The player owes Mara 6 silver for sword repair.

State memory

Current status.

Example:

Mara is injured, resting at Greyford inn.

Secret/uncertain memory

Unverified or hidden facts.

Example:

Mara may be connected to the thieves’ guild, but this is only rumored.

Context should usually include only relevant layers.

⸻

13. Major actor memory

Major actors need more durable summaries.

Major actor memory should track:

* identity
* relationship
* current status
* current location
* loyalty/hostility
* key history with player
* secrets known to player
* claims/rumors separately
* important promises
* injuries/conditions
* abilities/equipment if relevant
* faction position
* active quest/case links
* unresolved tensions
* last major interaction

Example:

Mara major memory:

* companion and skilled swordswoman
* trusts player moderately
* was present during Old Mill wolf fight
* currently wounded in left shoulder
* dislikes needless cruelty
* player promised to help find her brother
* rumored to have thieves’ guild history, unconfirmed

This is more useful than raw chat logs.

14. Relationship model

Relationships should be tracked separately from reputation.

Relationship is personal.

Reputation is public/social.

Relationship dimensions:

* trust
* affection
* respect
* fear
* hostility
* loyalty
* gratitude
* resentment
* debt
* obligation
* romance
* rivalry
* suspicion
* dependence
* authority
* intimidation

Do not reduce everything to one number.

A character can respect the player but not trust them.

A character can fear the player and obey, while secretly hating them.

A character can love the player and disagree morally.

16. Relationship score optional model

If numerical tracking is desired, use hidden or debug-facing scores.

Example dimensions from -100 to +100:

* Trust
* Affection
* Respect
* Fear
* Hostility
* Obligation

But player-facing summaries should usually be descriptive.

Example:

Instead of:

“Mara Trust +42”

Use:

“Mara trusts you somewhat, but she is still uneasy about your use of blood magic.”

Numbers can exist internally for consistency but should not dominate narrative.

17. Relationship change validation

Relationship changes require cause.

Valid causes:

* rescue
* betrayal
* payment
* unpaid debt
* kindness
* cruelty
* shared combat
* healing
* lying
* public insult
* private confession
* kept promise
* broken promise
* gift
* intimidation
* romance scene
* moral disagreement
* faction conflict
* quest completion
* evidence revelation
* crime witnessed

Invalid causes:

* random AI says “they love you now” with no support
* Story Card says they trust player
* player declares “everyone likes me”
* one minor action causing extreme change without context
* duplicate relationship update

Relationship changes should be proportional.

18. Relationship memory examples

Rescued NPC

Event:

Player saves Tovin from wolves.

Possible relationship:

* gratitude increases
* trust increases
* fear may decrease
* Tovin may become helpful witness

Betrayed companion

Event:

Player lies to Mara about cursed artifact and she is injured.

Possible relationship:

* trust decreases
* resentment increases
* companion may confront player
* quest branch possible

Paid worker fairly

Event:

Player pays workers on time during dangerous project.

Possible relationship:

* worker morale improves
* local reputation improves if known
* foreman trust increases

Intimidated merchant

Event:

Player threatens merchant into discount.

Possible relationship:

* merchant fear increases
* trust decreases
* local reputation may worsen if public

19. Public reputation versus personal relationship

These must be separate.

Example:

Player saves town publicly.

Public reputation:

* local hero

But one NPC may hate the player because:

* their brother died in collateral damage

Example:

Player is feared as demon slayer.

Public reputation:

* respected/fearsome

But companion may distrust player due to cruelty.

Do not let public fame automatically overwrite personal relationships.

23. Reputation strength

Reputation strength levels:

Strength	Meaning
none	no known reputation
faint	a few people heard something
minor	local mention
moderate	recognized locally
strong	widely known in scope
famous	major recognition
legendary	known across regions
mythic	world-scale fame
infamous	severe negative reputation
feared	people react with fear
revered	people react with awe/devotion

Reputation should require spread.

A private heroic act does not create public fame unless someone knows and shares it.

24. Reputation spread

Reputation spreads through:

* witnesses
* survivors
* bards
* public announcements
* faction reports
* town gossip
* official records
* wanted posters
* magical communication
* rumors
* contracts
* newspapers/bulletins if setting supports
* soldiers/traders
* divine signs
* social media if modern setting

Spread speed depends on:

* communication technology
* faction networks
* distance
* event severity
* public visibility
* political interest
* secrecy

Example:

Killing a dragon in front of a city spreads fast.

Saving one child in a remote forest may stay private unless told.

25. Reputation decay and persistence

Some reputation fades.

Temporary reputation:

* helped at market
* won small brawl
* minor rumor

Persistent reputation:

* saved village
* betrayed kingdom
* killed noble
* founded guild
* became dragon slayer
* broke divine oath
* murdered civilians
* solved famous case

Decay factors:

* time
* distance
* competing news
* lack of proof
* deliberate cover-up
* reputation repair
* false rumor disproven

Persistent reputation may archive but remain relevant.

28. Title validation

Valid title sources:

* authority grants title
* public reputation creates nickname
* faction grants rank
* quest reward includes title
* legal appointment
* religious blessing
* arena victory
* major public feat
* curse/mark creates title
* enemy names player
* player self-title if recognized by no one yet

Invalid title sources:

* player simply declares everyone knows it
* Story Card text grants it
* random AI output without event
* duplicate of old title
* title from dream with no real consequence
* generic “Hero” with no scope

A self-proclaimed title may exist, but others may not respect it.

41. Actor knowledge versus player knowledge

AIDRPG must distinguish:

* what the player knows
* what an actor knows
* what a faction knows
* what is objectively true
* what is rumored
* what is public

Example:

Objectively true:

Lord Veyr ordered murder.

Player knows:

only blue wax clue and suspicion.

Actor knows:

Veyr’s servant knows the truth.

Public knows:

nothing.

Context for player should not reveal objective truth unless discovered.

Actor memory can store hidden facts only if the script internally supports hidden state, but output should not spoil it.

42. Witnesses and public knowledge

Witnesses connect events to reputation.

An event becomes public if:

* witnessed by public
* reported by witness
* recorded by authority
* announced
* discovered later

82. Safeguards

82.1 Not every NPC becomes permanent

Use importance filters.

82.2 Names must be clean

Reject junk, generic, or duplicate actor names.

82.3 Actor facts need truth status

Rumors, claims, suspicions, and confirmed facts are different.

82.4 Major actors need durable summaries

Do not rely on raw old text.

82.5 Relationships need causes

No unsupported instant loyalty/love/hatred.

82.6 Reputation has scope

Local fame is not global fame.

82.7 Reputation requires awareness

Private actions do not instantly become public.

82.8 Titles need sources

Formal, informal, and self-proclaimed titles differ.

82.9 Faction standing is separate from personal relationship

An actor may like the player while their faction distrusts them.

82.10 Faction awareness matters

No faction consequence without witnesses, evidence, reports, or discovery.

82.11 Companion presence must be enforced

Absent companions cannot act.

82.12 Actor custody matters

Items held by actors are not freely accessible.

82.13 Actor death/capture needs validation

Major actors should not be casually overwritten.

82.14 Social memory should not spoil hidden truth

Only inject what player knows or what current scene needs.

82.15 Generated actor cards are selective

Do not card every guard, merchant, worker, or rumor name.

82.16 Card triggers must be specific

Avoid generic triggers like guard, merchant, wolf, faction, companion.

82.17 Public accusations can backfire

Suspicion is not proof.

82.18 Workers are usually group records

Only promote important workers/foremen.

82.19 Sentient items and summons need hybrid handling

Do not treat them as simple inventory if they have will.

82.20 Every social update should be auditable

The player should know why trust, reputation, hostility, titles, or faction standing changed.

83. Final Chunk 31 doctrine

Actors are the social memory of the world.

Not every NPC deserves permanent memory.

Named, recurring, emotionally important, quest-linked, case-linked, contract-linked, faction-linked, companion, enemy, witness, or world-impacting actors should be tracked.

Actor facts must distinguish confirmed truth from rumors, claims, suspicions, and disputed information.

Relationship is personal.

Reputation is public.

Faction standing is organizational.

Titles are recognized labels with scope and source.

Private actions do not automatically become public reputation.

Public actions can spread through witnesses, reports, rumors, and faction networks.

Companions have autonomy, presence, loyalty, injuries, promises, and moral boundaries.

Workers and crowds should usually be group records unless promoted.

Actors can own or hold items, secrets, debts, promises, evidence, and social consequences.

Factions have goals, leaders, resources, territory, relationships, and memory.

Generated actor and faction cards should be selective, specific, and trigger-safe.

The goal is to make people remember what matters without flooding the system with random NPCs, corrupting memory with rumors, or letting AI Dungeon teleport, rename, duplicate, or emotionally rewrite important actors without validation.
