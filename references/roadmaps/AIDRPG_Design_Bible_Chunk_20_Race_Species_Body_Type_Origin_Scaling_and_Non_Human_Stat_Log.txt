AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 20: Race, Species, Body Type, Origin Scaling, and Non-Human Stat Logic

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 20 defines how AIDRPG handles race, species, ancestry, body type, biology, and origin-based scaling.

Chunk 19 defined the base Level 1–1000 scaling matrix using a mostly human-like character as the default.

Chunk 20 answers:

How does a human differ from an elf, dwarf, dragonkin, beastkin, fairy, giant, slime, undead, spirit, demon, angel, construct, vampire, or monster race?

How does AIDRPG infer racial traits from a Story Card?

How does it use common sense without making up too much?

How does species affect stats, HP, MP, EP, injury, recovery, poison, disease, fatigue, equipment, abilities, weaknesses, growth, and social reaction?

How does the system prevent race/species from becoming infinite free power?

How does the system safely handle custom races?

The central rule is:

Race/species modifies the level matrix, but does not replace it.

A Level 1 dragonkin is stronger than a Level 1 human in some ways, but is not automatically a god.

A Level 1 fairy may be faster or more magical than a human, but may be physically fragile.

A Level 1 undead may ignore poison, but may be vulnerable to holy power, fire, sunlight, or turning.

Race creates strengths, weaknesses, body rules, and story consequences.

It should not create free unlimited power.

2. Core philosophy

AI Dungeon frequently creates characters with unusual races, species, bodies, or origins.

Examples:

* human
* elf
* dwarf
* beastkin
* catgirl
* wolfkin
* dragonkin
* demon
* angel
* vampire
* undead
* slime
* spirit
* ghost
* fairy
* giant
* goblin
* orc
* oni
* automaton
* golem
* homunculus
* chimera
* half-dragon
* reincarnated human
* cursed human
* summoned hero
* monster evolution body
* divine avatar
* cosmic entity fragment

AIDRPG must support these without needing a hand-written rule for every possible race.

The system should use:

* explicit Story Card facts
* player sheet race/species field
* scenario setup
* confirmed narration
* common fantasy logic
* body type inference
* ability/passive records
* weaknesses and limits
* validation evidence

But it must avoid:

* giving unlimited bonuses from vague race names
* inventing dozens of traits without support
* assuming every dragonkin has full dragon powers
* assuming every demon is immune to all damage
* assuming every undead is invincible
* assuming every slime is immune to all physical harm
* assuming every fairy can fly at infinite speed
* assuming every race works the same in every setting
* overwriting Story Card canon with generic assumptions

The rule is:

Explicit setting facts override generic assumptions.

If the Story Card says “in this world, elves are short-lived iron-blooded desert nomads with no innate magic,” AIDRPG should use that, not generic elf logic.

3. Race/species ownership

Race/species touches many systems, but each truth still needs an owner.

PlayerReducer owns

* player race/species
* ancestry
* origin
* age
* appearance
* primary body category
* biological classification if known
* racial identity as part of the player sheet

BodyReducer owns

* body structure
* HP/MP/EP effects
* injury rules
* recovery rules
* fatigue logic
* poison/disease/bleeding logic
* body-state consequences
* death/unconsciousness boundary changes from species

ProgressionReducer owns

* level scaling
* stat growth changes
* XP effects
* domain XP effects
* growth caps/modifiers

AbilityReducer owns

* racial abilities
* innate passives
* breath weapons
* senses
* regeneration
* flight
* shapeshifting
* natural weapons
* resistances if ability-like
* racial candidate abilities

InventoryReducer owns

* equipment fit
* armor compatibility
* natural weapon/equipment interactions
* item access problems caused by body shape
* worn/equipped slots modified by race

ReputationReducer owns

* social reactions to race/species
* prejudice/fear/respect
* legal status
* faction-based species reputation
* public identity consequences

ActorReducer owns

* race/species of NPC actors if known
* species-based actor identity
* relationship consequences involving race

CardSync owns

* race/species information mirrored in Player Sheet
* generated race/species card only if the race is custom, complex, or important enough
* updates when confirmed racial facts change

The rule:

Race/species should be read by many systems, but written by the correct owner.

4. Race/species as a modifier stack

Race/species should not be one flat bonus.

It should be a modifier stack.

A race/species profile may include:

* base stat bias
* stat growth bias
* HP scaling modifier
* MP scaling modifier
* EP scaling modifier
* size category
* movement type
* senses
* natural weapons
* natural armor
* natural vulnerabilities
* recovery rules
* food/rest needs
* poison/disease rules
* aging/lifespan
* equipment compatibility
* social response
* innate abilities
* innate passives
* special body rules
* special death rules
* progression quirks
* magic affinity
* domain affinity
* environmental affinity
* card/context notes

This allows a race to be powerful in some areas and limited in others.

Example:

Dragonkin:

* higher ATK/DEF/HP
* possible breath candidate
* heat resistance
* intimidating appearance
* equipment fit problems
* anti-dragon weapons are dangerous
* social fear in some regions
* slower stealth/social blending
* possible instinct or hunger complications

That is much better than:

“Dragonkin = +100 to everything.”

5. Base human default

Human is the default baseline.

Unless the scenario says otherwise, human has:

* no extreme stat bonus
* flexible growth
* average HP scaling
* average MP scaling
* average EP scaling
* standard injury logic
* standard poison/disease/bleeding logic
* standard equipment compatibility
* standard sleep/food/rest needs
* normal lifespan
* high adaptability
* broad class compatibility
* low built-in natural weapons
* no automatic magical resistance
* no automatic natural armor

Human strength is flexibility.

Humans should not be weak by default.

They should be the reference point.

Human default Level 1 example:

Race/Species: Human
Level: 1
ATK: 5
DEF: 5
SPD: 5
INT: 5
LCK: 5
HP: 50–80
MP: 20–60
EP: 50–90
Traits: adaptable, no innate extreme modifiers

A human can become anything through class, training, abilities, items, blessings, mutations, or story events.

7. Race/species confidence

Race assumptions should have confidence levels.

Confirmed

Directly stated by player, scenario, Story Card, or validated narration.

Example:

“The player is a dragonkin.”

Strongly implied

Repeated or obvious from description.

Example:

“The player has scales, horns, a tail, and draconic ancestry.”

Common-sense inferred

Race name has familiar fantasy meaning, but details are not fully known.

Example:

“Elf” usually implies long life and agility, but magic affinity depends on setting.

Pending

A possible race trait exists but is not confirmed.

Example:

Dragonkin may have breath weapon, but has not shown it.

Rejected

A proposed trait does not fit current race/story facts.

Example:

AI says the human player has wings, but no mutation/ability/item/story supports it.

Race confidence prevents the system from overbuilding unsupported traits.

⸻

8. Generic race assumptions versus Story Card truth

Story Card or scenario truth overrides generic assumptions.

Example:

Generic assumption:

Elves are graceful, long-lived, magically inclined.

Story Card says:

“In this setting, elves are subterranean, short-lived, physically tough, and cannot use magic.”

AIDRPG should use the Story Card version.

This applies to:

* elves
* dwarves
* demons
* angels
* vampires
* undead
* dragons
* slimes
* beastkin
* spirits
* any custom race

Common sense fills gaps only when:

* the race is recognizable
* no Story Card contradicts it
* the inference is modest
* the inference is useful for validation
* uncertainty is preserved

Example:

If race is “wolfkin,” it is reasonable to infer enhanced smell as pending/likely.

It is not reasonable to infer full moon immortality unless supported.

⸻

9. Race/species stat modifier categories

Race/species should use broad modifiers rather than huge raw numbers.

Suggested categories:

Modifier	Meaning
Very Low	significant weakness
Low	below human baseline
Slightly Low	modest weakness
Baseline	human-like
Slightly High	modest advantage
High	strong advantage
Very High	major racial advantage
Extreme	only for large, divine, monster, or specialized bodies

This should affect base stats and growth, not always direct flat bonuses.

Example:

A giant may have Very High ATK and DEF, but Low SPD and possibly Low fine manipulation.

A fairy may have High SPD and High MP affinity, but Very Low HP and low physical ATK.

A slime may have unusual DEF against stabbing, but low structure against fire/drying/purification.

10. Race/species resource modifier categories

Resource scaling can also use categories.

Modifier	Resource Effect
Very Low	50–70% of human baseline
Low	70–85%
Slightly Low	85–95%
Baseline	100%
Slightly High	105–115%
High	115–140%
Very High	140–180%
Extreme	180%+ only with major tradeoffs

Resource categories apply to:

* HP
* MP
* EP

Example:

Fairy:

* HP Very Low
* MP High
* EP Slightly Low or Baseline depending flight/body rules

Giant:

* HP Very High
* MP Low or Baseline
* EP High but movement cost also high

Undead:

* HP may be High or strange
* MP may be Baseline/High depending magic
* EP may be replaced by animating force or stamina equivalent

⸻

11. Race/species growth modifier categories

Growth should be kept safe.

Suggested growth categories:

Growth Bias	Effect
Poor	stat/domain grows slower
Slow	modest penalty
Normal	human baseline
Fast	modest bonus
Exceptional	strong bonus with limits
Locked	cannot grow normally without special method
Alternative	grows through unusual source

Examples:

Elf:

* SPD growth Fast
* INT growth Fast
* DEF growth Slow maybe, depending setting

Dwarf:

* DEF growth Fast
* Crafting Domain Fast
* SPD growth Slow

Slime:

* physical stat growth Alternative through mass/absorption
* body damage rules Alternative

Construct:

* organic stat training may be Locked or Slow
* upgrade/crafting growth Alternative

Undead:

* normal recovery may be Alternative
* holy resistance/vulnerability may affect growth

Growth modifiers should never allow infinite free growth.

They affect rates and routes, not unlimited scaling.

⸻

12. Size categories

Size matters for combat, equipment, stealth, travel, carrying, and injury.

Recommended size categories:

Size	Examples	Effects
Tiny	fairy, small familiar, rat-sized slime	low HP/ATK, high stealth, equipment issues
Small	goblin, child-sized race, small beastkin	lower reach, easier stealth, smaller gear
Human-scale	human, elf, dwarf, common beastkin	default equipment and injury logic
Large	ogrekin, minotaur, young giant	higher HP/ATK, gear issues, harder stealth
Huge	giant, large dragonkin, troll body	major strength, major logistics issues
Colossal	dragon, titan, world beast	not normal player unless special campaign
Fluid/Formless	slime, ooze, mist body	special injury/equipment/body rules
Incorporeal	ghost, spirit	special physical interaction rules
Constructed	golem, automaton	special repair/recovery rules

Size is not pure advantage.

Large bodies are stronger but have:

* stealth problems
* equipment costs
* indoor movement limits
* social fear
* higher food/resource needs
* larger target profile

Tiny bodies have:

* fragile HP
* low carrying
* equipment issues
* but strong stealth/mobility advantages

13. Body category

Body category matters more than race name.

AIDRPG should classify bodies into categories:

Humanoid organic

Examples:

* human
* elf
* dwarf
* beastkin
* orc
* demon humanoid
* angel humanoid

Uses standard injury/equipment logic unless traits modify it.

Beast-bodied

Examples:

* quadruped wolfkin form
* centaur
* lamia
* harpy
* merfolk
* spiderfolk

Needs altered equipment/movement/injury slots.

Draconic/reptilian

Examples:

* dragonkin
* lizardfolk
* kobold
* half-dragon

May have scales, claws, tail, heat/cold modifiers.

Fluid/Formless

Examples:

* slime
* ooze
* liquid spirit

Special physical damage and equipment rules.

Undead

Examples:

* skeleton
* zombie
* lich body
* vampire if undead in setting
* wight

Special poison/disease/healing/death rules.

Spirit/Incorporeal

Examples:

* ghost
* elemental spirit
* astral body

Special physical interaction rules.

Construct/Artificial

Examples:

* golem
* automaton
* doll
* homunculus

Repair instead of healing, maintenance needs.

Divine/Celestial

Examples:

* angel
* demigod body
* avatar

Strong resistances, law/oath weaknesses, divine vulnerabilities.

Demonic/Infernal

Examples:

* demon
* oni
* fiend

Resistances and weaknesses tied to holy, contracts, summoning, corruption.

Hybrid/Chimera

Examples:

* half-dragon elf
* slime-human
* beast-demon hybrid

Requires combined profile with caps.

17. Beastkin

Beastkin is broad and must depend on animal type.

Generic beastkin profile:

Race: Beastkin
Body: humanoid organic with animal traits
Size: varies
ATK: varies by animal
DEF: varies
SPD: often high
INT: baseline
LCK: baseline
HP: baseline to high
MP: baseline unless magical species
EP: high
Growth: physical/sensory domains often fast
Equipment: usually standard with ear/tail/claw adjustments
Weaknesses: instincts, social prejudice, species-specific vulnerabilities
Strengths: senses, movement, natural weapons, animal affinity

Examples:

Catkin

* SPD high
* LCK slightly high
* stealth/climbing affinity
* ATK baseline
* DEF slightly low/baseline
* night vision likely
* water aversion only if setting/narration supports

Wolfkin

* ATK slightly high
* SPD high
* EP high
* scent tracking
* pack/social instinct
* hearing/smell advantages
* silver/moon effects only if setting supports

Bearkin

* ATK high
* DEF high
* HP high
* SPD slightly low
* EP high
* intimidation
* large body/equipment issues possible

Rabbitkin

* SPD very high
* LCK slightly high
* hearing high
* HP/DEF lower
* jump/mobility traits
* social prey-instinct if setting supports

Birdkin/Harpy-like

* SPD high
* flight or glide if wings support
* HP lower
* equipment issues
* wind/ranged mobility advantages

Beastkin must be typed.

AIDRPG should not treat all beastkin the same.

Body: tiny magical humanoid
Size: tiny
ATK: very low physically
DEF: very low physically
SPD: high to very high
INT: high or slightly high
LCK: high
HP: very low

Recovery: may be magic-dependent
Weaknesses: physical fragility, capture risk, anti-magic, iron if setting supports

22. Full dragon player

Full dragon is not the same as dragonkin.

Generic profile:

Race: Dragon
Body: large/huge draconic
Size: large to colossal depending age
ATK: very high to extreme
DEF: very high
SPD: varies; flight mobility high if winged
INT: high
HP: very high
MP: high
EP: high
Growth: age/power/magic hoard/story-linked
Equipment: mostly incompatible, natural body matters
Weaknesses: anti-dragon weapons, dragon hunters, hoard instincts, social/faction danger, size limitations
Strengths: natural armor, claws, bite, flight, breath, magic, fear aura

Full dragon should usually be an advanced or special campaign race.

A Level 1 young dragon may be stronger than a Level 1 human but still a young dragon.

It may be threatened by:

* dragon hunters
* older dragons
* magic nets
* poison
* cold/element counters
* divine weapons
* cramped spaces
* social/faction consequences

Full dragon needs special equipment, travel, and social rules.

25. Undead

Undead require special body rules.

Generic profile:

Race: Undead
Body: dead/animated organic or skeletal
Size: original body size
ATK: varies
DEF: varies; pain resistance high
SPD: varies
INT: varies by undead type
HP: alternative body integrity
MP: baseline to high if magical
EP: alternative animating stamina
Growth: may require souls, necromancy, death energy, or normal leveling depending setting
Equipment: standard if humanoid
Recovery: healing may not work normally
Weaknesses: holy power, fire, sunlight, turning, purification, body destruction, necromantic disruption
Strengths: poison resistance, disease resistance, no normal bleeding, pain resistance, no normal fatigue

Undead subtypes matter.

Skeleton

* low flesh vulnerability
* piercing damage reduced
* blunt damage dangerous
* no bleeding
* poison immune
* armor possible
* healing magic may fail unless necromantic

Zombie

* high pain resistance
* slow
* vulnerable to fire/holy
* poor fine control maybe
* poison/disease irrelevant

Vampire

* high regeneration if fed
* charm/blood abilities possible
* sunlight/holy/stake/permission weaknesses depending setting
* hunger matters
* social concealment matters

Lich

* high magic
* phylactery or anchor if setting supports
* body destruction may not equal final death
* anti-magic/holy/sealing important

Undead must not get free immortality unless their death rules are explicit.

27. Slime / Ooze / Gelatinous body

Slime is one of the most important freeform race categories because players often try anime-style growth with it.

Generic profile:

Race: Slime/Ooze
Body: fluid/formless
Size: small to large depending mass
ATK: low to baseline unless acidic/massive
DEF: unusual; resists some physical damage
SPD: low to baseline unless specialized
INT: varies from animalistic to high
HP: body mass/integrity
MP: varies; magical slimes may be high
EP: movement/cohesion energy
Growth: absorption, mass, cores, magic, evolution, leveling
Equipment: mostly incompatible unless transformed/adapted
Recovery: may regenerate mass if fed/energized
Weaknesses: fire, ice, salt, drying, purification, containment, core damage depending setting
Strengths: shapeshifting, compression, physical resistance, absorption, squeezing through gaps

Important slime rules:

* stabbing may be less effective
* blunt damage may disperse but not kill
* fire/heat/drying may be dangerous
* salt/purification may be dangerous if setting supports
* core may be vital if confirmed
* equipment needs special adaptation
* body parts are not normal limbs
* injury is mass loss, core crack, cohesion damage, contamination, or drying

A slime player may gain unusual abilities, but not for free.

Potential slime abilities:

* engulf
* dissolve
* reshape body
* split
* absorb material
* mimic texture
* armor-form
* weapon-form
* acid secretion
* regeneration
* core evolution

All should need rank/cost/limits.

EP: mechanical stamina/energy
Growth: upgrades, repairs, enchantments, learning core, installed parts
Equipment: may be integrated or modified
Recovery: repair instead of healing
Weaknesses: rust, EMP/magic disruption, core damage, maintenance, joints, control sigils
Strengths: poison immunity, disease immunity, pain resistance, durability, no normal fatigue

Construct rules:

* healing potions may not work
* repair kits/crafting matter
* poison/disease usually irrelevant
* parts can break
* maintenance matters
* upgrades may replace stat training
* emotions/free will depend on setting

Constructs are powerful defensively but need repair logic.

30. Orc versus goblin versus human distinction

AIDRPG should not reduce fantasy races to stereotypes.

Mechanical distinctions should be about body and setting facts.

Example:

Human:

* flexible baseline

Goblin:

* smaller, faster, stealthier, less durable, socially disadvantaged in many settings

Orc:

* stronger, tougher, larger, more intimidating, possible social prejudice

But intelligence, morality, culture, and personality should come from story facts, not automatic race rules.

This prevents harmful or simplistic assumptions.

31. Hybrid races

Hybrid races combine traits.

Examples:

* half-elf
* half-dragon
* demon-human
* angel-demon
* slime-human
* wolfkin-vampire
* fairy-dragon
* construct-human soulbound body

Hybrid rule:

Do not stack full bonuses from both races.

Use a hybrid blend:

* primary body category
* secondary ancestry traits
* one or two major strengths
* one or two major weaknesses
* limited innate candidates
* clear social implications
* capped resource/stat modifiers

Example:

Half-dragon human:

* humanoid equipment mostly works
* some scales/claws
* modest heat resistance
* breath weapon candidate
* HP/DEF higher than human
* not full dragon size, armor, flight, or breath unless confirmed

Example:

Slime-human hybrid:

* human-like social/equipment access may remain
* partial slime regeneration/reshape candidate
* vulnerability to drying/fire/containment maybe
* body instability possible
* no automatic full shapeshifting until developed

Hybrid should be powerful in flavor, controlled in mechanics.

Race and class should interact but not overwrite each other.

Example:

* human baseline
* monk class gives balanced physical/spiritual growth

Dragonkin Monk:

* dragonkin body gives durability/strength
* monk class gives discipline, martial growth, EP control
* breath ability remains race-linked
* unarmed style may use claws/tail if confirmed

Fairy Warrior:

* fairy body limits raw HP/weapon size
* warrior class improves combat
* may fight through speed, precision, magic, tiny blades, or mobility
* should not become human-sized tank unless transformation occurs

Slime Mage:

* slime body gives fluid/injury rules
* mage class gives MP and spells
* equipment/casting focus may need adaptation

Race modifies the body.

Class modifies training and build direction.

Both matter.

34. Race and stat calculation order

Final formulas come in Chunk 21, but order should be planned now.

Recommended stat/resource calculation order:

1. Start with level-based base human expectation.
2. Apply class/build bias.
3. Apply race/species base modifier.
4. Apply race/species growth modifier.
5. Apply stat training gains.
6. Apply domain/specialization bonuses.
7. Apply ability/passive bonuses.
8. Apply equipment modifiers.
9. Apply condition/injury/fatigue penalties.
10. Apply temporary buffs/debuffs.
11. Calculate effective stats/resources.

Race should not apply after every temporary buff in a way that multiplies infinitely.

Race modifiers should be controlled and predictable.

41. Race and death boundary

Death/unconsciousness differs by species.

Human/organic

* unconscious at severe HP loss
* death from lethal injury, bleeding, organ failure, etc.

Undead

* body destruction may not equal final death if anchor/phylactery exists
* holy destruction may be final
* unconsciousness may become inert state

Construct

* shutdown instead of unconsciousness
* destroyed core may be death
* repair may revive if core intact

Slime

* dispersed, dried, core cracked, absorbed, contaminated

* may reform if anchor remains

Divine/demonic

* physical defeat may banish rather than kill
* true death may require special conditions

This must be explicit.

AIDRPG should not casually kill an unusual body from ordinary human logic.

* unique bloodline name
* unique transformation name

Unsafe triggers:

* elf

* undead
* monster
* race
* species
* body

A card triggered by “slime” may fire constantly in any slime scene.

not:

60. Race/species safety rules

60.1 Story Card truth overrides generic assumptions

Use setting-specific race lore first.

60.2 Race modifies level; it does not replace level

A powerful race at Level 1 is still Level 1.

60.3 No unlimited racial power

Every major advantage needs limits.

60.4 No unsupported trait explosion

Do not invent 10 traits from a race name.

60.5 Body category controls injury logic

Do not apply human bleeding/poison rules to bodies that do not support them.

60.6 Equipment must fit the body

Race can block normal equipment use.

60.7 Social response is setting-scoped

Do not assume universal hatred/reverence.

60.8 Race does not decide morality or intelligence

Unless setting explicitly says so.

60.9 Hybrid races blend traits, not stack full packages

No full double bonuses.

60.10 Race transformation requires strong evidence

No race change from vague flavor.

60.11 Racial abilities start as candidates unless confirmed

Dragonkin breath, fairy glamour, vampire charm, etc., need validation.

60.12 Race cards mirror state

Cards do not create race truth.

60.13 Generic race triggers should be avoided

Use specific custom race names for triggers.

60.14 Absorption-style racial growth must be capped

Race-enabled stat growth still obeys anti-farm and validation.

60.15 Race assumptions should be auditable

The player should be able to see what was assumed and why.

62. Final Chunk 20 doctrine

Race and species define body logic.

Level defines progression scale.

Class defines training direction.

Origin defines story source.

These are different things.

A human is the baseline.

Non-human races modify the baseline through body, resources, growth, senses, weaknesses, equipment, recovery, and social consequences.

Explicit Story Card facts override generic assumptions.

Common sense may fill gaps only modestly and with uncertainty.

Strong racial advantages require limits.

Unusual bodies require unusual injury and recovery logic.

Undead should not use normal poison rules.

Constructs should not use normal healing rules.

Slimes should not use normal organ-wound rules.

Fairies should not use human equipment normally.

Giants should not ignore size problems.

Dragonkin should not get full dragon power for free.

Hybrid races blend traits instead of stacking full packages.

Racial abilities should be tracked as traits, passives, or candidates with costs and limits.

Race cards should mirror confirmed race state only.

Race assumptions must be auditable.

The goal is to let any AI Dungeon race or species feel meaningfully different without letting race become uncontrolled free power or unsupported canon.
