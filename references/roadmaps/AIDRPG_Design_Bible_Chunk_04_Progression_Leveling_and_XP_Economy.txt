AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 4: Progression, Leveling, and XP Economy

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 4 defines how character growth works.

This chunk answers:

* What does “leveling” mean?
* What gives XP?
* What types of XP exist?
* How do Level XP, Domain XP, Stat XP, and Ability Growth interact?
* How does progression stay realistic?
* How does progression avoid farming?
* How does the system reward failure without lying about success?
* How does progression scale from early game to late game?
* How does progression guide gameplay without forcing it?

This chunk does not cover the full stat system, combat matrix, or ability lifecycle in detail. Those come later. This chunk defines the growth economy that those systems will use.

The existing feature guide already separates Level XP, Ability XP, Domain XP, and Stat Training XP as distinct lanes, and it says XP/level/stat training/domain XP belong to ProgressionReducer while abilities and candidates belong to AbilityReducer.  ￼

So the purpose here is to turn that into a usable rule model.

⸻

2. Core progression philosophy

Progression should feel earned.

AIDRPG should not grant major growth just because the AI wrote a dramatic line.

Progression should come from:

* meaningful action
* confirmed consequence
* risk
* cost
* repetition with purpose
* training over time
* challenge
* discovery
* survival
* learning
* story milestones

Progression should not come from:

* vague narration
* dreams
* rumors
* jokes
* quoted dialogue
* pretend actions
* repeated trivial actions
* duplicate output
* failed parsing
* card text
* debug text
* unsupported power jumps

The clean-core rules already say parsers may propose progression events, but they must not award XP directly; progression state should be committed by ProgressionReducer.  ￼

The basic philosophy is:

Progression rewards meaningful confirmed gameplay, not text patterns.

⸻

3. The four growth lanes

AIDRPG should use four main growth lanes.

3.1 Level XP

Level XP is broad adventure growth.

It represents the player becoming more generally experienced, capable, and story-significant.

Level XP should come from things like:

* meaningful combat
* quest progress
* major discoveries
* survival under risk
* completing objectives
* important social victories
* dangerous travel
* domain milestones
* major ability breakthroughs
* significant failures survived

Level XP should not replace specific growth.

A character can gain Level XP from surviving a monster attack, but sword skill, survival skill, injury state, and reputation should still be tracked separately.

3.2 Total XP

Total XP is lifetime Level XP earned.

It does not reset when the player levels.

It is mainly useful for:

* audit
* balancing
* long-term history
* progression reports
* anti-farm analysis
* debugging

Current XP moves toward the next level.

Total XP records the full history.

The existing guide already treats current XP, XP-to-next, and total XP as separate values.  ￼

3.3 Domain XP

Domain XP tracks competence in broad activity areas.

Example domains:

* melee
* ranged
* defense
* magic
* stealth
* survival
* crafting
* investigation
* social
* leadership
* labor
* domain management
* travel
* medicine
* trade

Domain XP says:

The player is becoming experienced in this field.

It should not automatically create a named ability.

Example:

Repeatedly surviving forest travel may increase Survival Domain XP.

It does not automatically create “Forest Mastery” unless the ability system separately validates that as a passive/talent.

3.4 Stat Training XP

Stat Training XP tracks practice toward improving core stats.

Core stat lanes:

* ATK XP
* DEF XP
* SPD XP
* INT XP
* LCK XP

Stat Training XP should grow from repeated meaningful actions.

Examples:

* ATK XP from striking, weapon drills, strength exertion, offensive combat
* DEF XP from blocking, enduring, armor training, bracing, survival under impact
* SPD XP from dodging, sprinting, footwork, acrobatics, chase scenes
* INT XP from study, magic control, tactics, crafting, investigation, planning
* LCK XP from rare-chance survival, improvisation, high-risk gambits, fortune events

The clean-core roadmap already defines these same stat lanes and examples.  ￼

3.5 Ability Growth

Ability Growth is related but should be treated carefully.

Ability candidates, evidence, stabilization, refinement, costs, limits, and mastery belong primarily to AbilityReducer.

ProgressionReducer may track broad progression rewards connected to ability use, but AbilityReducer owns ability-specific growth.

So the rule is:

* AbilityReducer owns ability evidence, stages, reliability, cost knowledge, and mastery.
* ProgressionReducer owns Level XP, Domain XP, and Stat Training XP that may be awarded from ability-related events.
* Ability use can create both ability growth and progression growth, but through separate owner paths.

Example:

The player tries to form Mana Slash and fails.

Possible results:

* AbilityReducer: records failed real attempt / evidence
* ProgressionReducer: small Magic Domain XP or INT XP if effort was meaningful
* BodyReducer: MP or EP strain if narrated
* No stable ability yet

This prevents “ability XP” from becoming a second uncontrolled XP system.

⸻

4. What level means

Level is broad adventure maturity.

Level should represent:

* experience under pressure
* general resilience
* story competence
* ability to handle greater threats
* broader scale of adventure
* improved resource ceilings
* better baseline plausibility

Level should not represent:

* automatic victory
* mastery of every skill
* immunity to danger
* guaranteed spell success
* instant social authority
* automatic stat superiority
* automatic ability stabilization

A level 10 scholar may still lose a direct fistfight to a level 5 trained brawler.

A level 5 clever rogue may beat a stronger enemy with terrain, stealth, preparation, or luck.

Level is a broad scaling signal, not the whole character.

⸻

5. Recommended level bands

AIDRPG should use level bands to guide tone and plausibility.

Levels 1–3: Novice

The player is fragile and still forming identity.

Expected feel:

* low resources
* unstable abilities
* minor threats matter
* injuries are dangerous
* training gains are noticeable
* simple gear matters
* local quests are meaningful

Typical threats:

* wolves
* bandits
* minor monsters
* street fights
* local hazards
* small mysteries

Levels 4–7: Capable

The player has reliable basics.

Expected feel:

* some abilities stabilize
* stats start to distinguish build
* ordinary enemies become manageable
* resource use matters
* quests can involve larger stakes
* companions and reputation become more relevant

Typical threats:

* trained soldiers
* monster packs
* dangerous ruins
* faction agents
* magical hazards

Levels 8–12: Veteran

The player has a clear build identity.

Expected feel:

* specialized tactics matter
* abilities are more reliable
* enemy matchup matters more
* domain ranks become meaningful
* major quests and faction consequences emerge
* ordinary threats may be overcome efficiently

Typical threats:

* elite enemies
* boss creatures
* cursed sites
* multi-stage quests
* political or faction conflicts

Levels 13–18: Heroic

The player can influence larger events.

Expected feel:

* high-impact abilities
* large-scale conflicts
* domain/base systems matter more
* reputation and titles matter
* failure still possible through counters, exhaustion, injuries, scale, or story leverage

Typical threats:

* powerful monsters
* faction leaders
* elite groups
* magical disasters
* regional crises

Levels 19+: Legendary

The player is exceptional, but not beyond consequence.

Expected feel:

* large-scale stakes
* legendary enemies
* powerful abilities
* domain/faction/world changes
* death may still happen if the story clearly supports it
* counters, costs, conditions, and scale still matter

Typical threats:

* legendary beings
* armies
* ancient curses
* domain wars
* world-shaping events

The rule is:

Higher level expands what is plausible. It does not delete risk.

⸻

6. Recommended level curve

AIDRPG needs a predictable XP-to-next formula.

The exact numbers can be tuned later, but the design should begin with a simple curve.

Recommended formula:

XP-to-next = 50 + current level × 25 + current level² × 5

Examples:

* Level 1 to 2: 80 XP
* Level 2 to 3: 120 XP
* Level 3 to 4: 170 XP
* Level 4 to 5: 230 XP
* Level 5 to 6: 300 XP
* Level 10 to 11: 800 XP
* Level 15 to 16: 1,550 XP
* Level 20 to 21: 2,550 XP

Why this curve works:

* early levels arrive fast enough to feel rewarding
* mid levels require repeated meaningful play
* late levels require major achievements
* trivial farming becomes less effective over time
* the system can still support very long adventures

This is a design default, not a sacred final number.

The important part is that the curve should grow enough that late-game progression requires larger story achievements.

⸻

7. Level-up rewards

Leveling should give broad growth, not erase specific systems.

When the player levels up, the system may grant:

* max HP increase
* max MP increase
* max EP increase
* possibly one broad build tag update
* possible stat cap/potential increase
* possible unlock pressure for ability stabilization
* improved matchup baseline
* progression milestone record

Leveling should not automatically:

* create new abilities
* fully heal the player unless specifically configured
* raise every stat
* complete quests
* create cards directly
* bypass training
* solve resource problems
* erase injuries

Recommended default level-up reward:

* max HP +5
* max MP +3
* max EP +5
* one progression milestone
* optional build identity recalculation
* no automatic stat point unless stat rules later allow it

For low-magic or high-magic settings, MP scaling can be adjusted.

The important rule is:

Leveling improves capacity and narrative plausibility, while specific competence comes from stats, domains, abilities, equipment, and story context.

⸻

8. XP source categories

Progression events should be classified by source.

8.1 Training XP

Training includes:

* weapon drills
* magic practice
* physical conditioning
* study
* crafting practice
* footwork drills
* meditation
* survival practice
* sparring
* labor practice

Training usually gives:

* Stat Training XP
* Domain XP
* possible small Level XP
* possible Ability evidence if training a real developing ability

Training usually costs:

* time
* EP
* MP if magical/focus-based
* materials if crafting
* risk if dangerous

Training should not give large Level XP unless there is risk, breakthrough, teacher challenge, or meaningful story context.

8.2 Combat XP

Combat includes:

* attacks
* defense
* survival
* tactical victories
* enemy defeat
* escape from danger
* protecting allies
* surviving ambush
* overcoming dangerous odds

Combat may give:

* Level XP
* Stat Training XP
* Domain XP
* Ability Growth
* reputation
* quest progress
* loot

Combat rewards should depend on:

* threat tier
* risk
* injury
* resource cost
* party help
* preparation
* enemy importance
* victory quality
* whether the event was confirmed

Combat must not give large XP from unsupported or impossible wins.

8.3 Discovery XP

Discovery includes:

* finding hidden places
* solving mysteries
* uncovering clues
* identifying weaknesses
* learning lore
* mapping routes
* discovering recipes
* discovering abilities
* learning enemy patterns

Discovery may give:

* Level XP if important
* Investigation Domain XP
* Survival/Travel Domain XP
* INT XP
* LCK XP if discovery came from rare fortune or risky improvisation
* Ability evidence if relevant

Discovery should not grant XP from every random description.

The discovery must matter.

8.4 Quest XP

Quest XP comes from:

* accepting major responsibility
* progressing objectives
* completing objectives
* resolving consequences
* making meaningful choices
* surviving quest climax

Quest XP may give:

* Level XP
* Domain XP tied to quest type
* Stat XP if actions support it
* reputation
* item rewards
* currency rewards
* title progress

Quest completion should usually be one of the strongest sources of Level XP.

But quest completion must be validated.

A rumor that the quest was completed should not grant XP.

8.5 Crafting XP

Crafting XP comes from:

* successful crafting
* partial crafting progress
* repairing items
* improving item quality
* learning recipes
* solving material problems
* improvising under constraints

Crafting may give:

* Crafting Domain XP
* INT XP
* sometimes ATK/DEF/SPD if physically demanding
* small Level XP for major creations
* Ability evidence if magical crafting is involved

Crafting should cost:

* time
* materials
* tools/station access
* EP or MP where appropriate

8.6 Social XP

Social growth comes from:

* negotiation
* persuasion
* leadership
* deception
* diplomacy
* public speaking
* intimidation
* resolving conflicts
* gaining witnesses
* managing reputation

Social events may give:

* Social Domain XP
* Leadership Domain XP
* INT XP
* LCK XP in rare improvisational outcomes
* Level XP for major social victories
* reputation changes

Not every conversation gives XP.

The conversation must have stakes or meaningful result.

8.7 Survival and travel XP

Survival/travel growth comes from:

* dangerous travel
* harsh weather
* navigation
* escaping hazards
* foraging
* enduring exposure
* finding routes
* surviving wilderness
* avoiding ambushes

May give:

* Survival Domain XP
* Travel Domain XP
* DEF XP
* SPD XP
* INT XP
* LCK XP
* small Level XP if danger was real

Ordinary walking through safe roads should not give meaningful XP.

8.8 Failure XP

Failure can give progression if the attempt was real and meaningful.

Failure XP should be smaller than success XP but still useful.

Examples:

* failed spell attempt gives ability evidence
* failed attack teaches combat lessons
* failed investigation produces clue or contradiction
* failed crafting teaches recipe limits
* failed negotiation creates social learning
* failed escape creates survival experience

Failure XP requires:

* real attempt
* meaningful action
* cost or risk
* non-trivial situation

Failure XP should not reward:

* pretend actions
* jokes
* repeated spam
* impossible unsupported attempts
* dreams
* quoted actions
* debug text

This supports fun because failure still matters without lying about success.

9. XP reward size table

This is the recommended initial reward scale.

9.1 No reward

Use when:

* trivial repetition
* pure flavor
* dreams
* rumors
* quoted actions
* pretend actions
* unsupported claims
* duplicate output
* weak narration with no meaningful action

Reward:

* 0 XP
* possible audit note only

9.2 Tiny reward

Use when:

* minor meaningful effort
* low-risk practice
* small clue
* light observation
* weak but real attempt
* basic training scene

Reward:

* 1–3 Stat XP
* 1–3 Domain XP
* 0 Level XP usually
* possible ability evidence if relevant

9.3 Small reward

Use when:

* confirmed useful action
* safe training with time/EP cost
* minor combat success
* partial crafting progress
* minor investigation progress
* small social success

Reward:

* 3–8 Stat XP
* 3–8 Domain XP
* 1–5 Level XP if meaningful
* possible ability evidence

9.4 Moderate reward

Use when:

* risky training
* even-threat combat
* meaningful quest progress
* clear clue/evidence gain
* successful crafting result
* difficult travel survival
* useful ability manifestation

Reward:

* 8–20 Stat XP
* 8–20 Domain XP
* 5–20 Level XP
* ability evidence or ability rank progress if relevant

9.5 Major reward

Use when:

* dangerous combat victory
* quest completion
* major discovery
* ability breakthrough
* important contract/domain milestone
* solving a case
* major reputation event
* surviving high-risk failure

Reward:

* 20–50 Stat/Domain XP where relevant
* 25–100 Level XP
* ability stabilization or major evidence if relevant
* possible title/reputation/item rewards

9.6 Legendary reward

Use rarely.

Use when:

* major boss defeated
* large-scale crisis resolved
* legendary ability mastered
* domain/faction-changing event
* campaign-defining quest completion
* impossible odds overcome with proper leverage

Reward:

* 100+ Level XP
* major Domain XP
* major Ability Growth
* title/reputation/domain consequences
* possible level-up milestone

Legendary rewards should require strong validation.

They should not come from a single unsupported sentence.

Safe sword training

Possible rewards:

* ATK XP
* DEF XP if blocking/guard training
* Melee Domain XP
* tiny or no Level XP

Costs:

* time
* EP
* fatigue

Dangerous duel

Possible rewards:

* Level XP
* ATK XP
* DEF XP
* SPD XP
* Melee Domain XP
* Ability Growth if using a developing combat ability

Costs:

* HP risk
* EP cost
* equipment condition risk
* injury risk

Failed spell attempt

Possible rewards:

* Ability evidence
* Magic Domain XP
* INT XP

Costs:

* MP strain
* fatigue or condition if severe

No reward if pretend, fake, or quoted.

Crafting a rough spear

Possible rewards:

* Crafting Domain XP
* INT XP
* maybe ATK XP if physical shaping matters
* small Level XP only if useful or difficult

Costs:

* materials
* time
* EP
* tool/station requirement

Solving a case

Possible rewards:

* Level XP
* Investigation Domain XP
* INT XP
* Reputation if public
* quest progress/completion if linked

Costs:

* time
* risk
* social consequences

Surviving a blizzard route

Possible rewards:

* Level XP if dangerous
* Survival Domain XP
* Travel Domain XP
* DEF XP
* LCK XP if rare survival
* possible reputation if witnessed

Costs:

* EP
* conditions
* injury risk
* time

11. Anti-farm rules

Progression must resist farming.

Farming means repeating low-risk actions just to gain numbers.

11.1 Same-action diminishing returns

If the player repeats the same safe training action in the same day/context:

* first meaningful scene: full reward
* second similar scene: 50–75% reward
* third similar scene: 25–50% reward
* more repetitions: 0–25% reward unless new risk, teacher, tool, difficulty, or breakthrough appears

11.2 Risk and cost requirement

Meaningful growth should usually require at least one of:

* time cost
* EP cost
* MP cost
* material cost
* danger
* social stakes
* opportunity cost
* failure risk
* teacher/challenge
* new environment
* actual consequence

No cost and no risk means little or no reward.

11.3 Trivial enemy reduction

Combat against trivial enemies should give little or no Level XP.

It may still give:

* tiny Stat XP
* tiny Domain XP
* no reward if repeated excessively

A level 15 character should not farm goblins for meaningful level progress unless the goblins are part of a larger dangerous scene.

11.4 Duplicate output protection

Same output replayed must not grant XP twice.

This is not just anti-farm. It is safety.

OperationLedger must block duplicate XP, ability evidence, stat training, domain XP, and level-up operations.

The clean-core safeguards explicitly require replayed outputs not to duplicate XP, item gains, ability evidence, quest progress, damage, card writes, actor/place mentions, or contract/domain progress.

11.5 Weak narration cap

Weak narration cannot grant large XP.

Examples:

* “You feel stronger.”
* “You seem more skilled.”
* “You remember your training.”
* “You are probably better now.”

These may create no reward or tiny flavor progress, but not major growth.

11.6 Failed attempt limit

Failed attempts can produce learning, but repeated identical failure should diminish.

Example:

Trying the same spell ten times with no change should not give ten full evidence rewards.

Later attempts need:

* changed method
* teacher input
* new insight
* new condition
* increased risk
* meaningful cost
* partial manifestation

Otherwise reward decays.

⸻

12. Stat Training XP and stat increases

Stat Training XP should be slower and more evidence-based than Level XP.

A stat should not increase from one weak sentence.

Recommended model:

Each core stat has:

* base value
* trained value
* temporary modifiers
* injury/condition penalties
* equipment modifiers
* effective value
* Stat Training XP

Stat increases should require accumulated XP.

Recommended starting stat scale will be fully defined in Chunk 5, but Chunk 4 defines the training cost philosophy.

12.1 Recommended stat increase threshold

Default formula:

Stat XP needed for next stat point = 40 + current stat × 15

Examples:

* raising stat 5 to 6 costs 115 Stat XP
* raising stat 10 to 11 costs 190 Stat XP
* raising stat 15 to 16 costs 265 Stat XP
* raising stat 20 to 21 costs 340 Stat XP

Why this works:

* early improvement is achievable
* mid-game growth requires repeated meaningful action
* late-game stats require major training, danger, teachers, breakthroughs, or special circumstances

12.2 Stat increase validation

A stat point should increase only when:

* enough Stat XP exists
* the XP came from relevant actions
* the action history supports that stat
* no injury/condition blocks normal growth
* duplicate XP did not inflate the lane
* the stat is not beyond setting-appropriate caps without special justification

Example:

Hours of sword drills may produce ATK XP.

Actually raising ATK requires accumulated ATK XP over time.

12.3 Stat XP overflow

If a stat increases and leftover Stat XP remains, it may carry over.

However, large overflow should be audited.

This prevents a bad parser from granting 9999 ATK XP and raising the stat repeatedly.

12.4 Stat growth and level

Level should not automatically raise every stat.

Instead, level can affect stat potential.

Recommended rule:

* stats can grow through training even without leveling
* level may set soft expectations or caps
* exceeding level-expected stats requires focused training, special traits, gear, species, or story justification

This allows unusual builds without letting every level-up flatten the character.

⸻

13. Domain XP and domain ranks

Domain XP tracks broad competence.

Domains should have ranks.

Recommended rank scale:

* Rank 0: Untrained
* Rank 1: Novice
* Rank 2: Practiced
* Rank 3: Competent
* Rank 4: Skilled
* Rank 5: Expert
* Rank 6: Master
* Rank 7: Legendary

13.1 Domain rank thresholds

Recommended XP thresholds:

* Rank 1: 50 XP
* Rank 2: 150 XP
* Rank 3: 350 XP
* Rank 4: 700 XP
* Rank 5: 1,200 XP
* Rank 6: 2,000 XP
* Rank 7: 3,200 XP

Domain ranks should grow slower than individual event rewards.

They represent long-term experience.

13.2 Domain rank effects

Domain ranks should affect plausibility and interpretation.

They should not guarantee success.

Examples:

Melee Domain:

* improves plausibility of weapon control, combat pressure, reading opponents, and training efficiency

Magic Domain:

* improves plausibility of spell control, mana handling, ability stabilization, and cost interpretation

Crafting Domain:

* improves quality, repair success, recipe improvisation, and material efficiency

Investigation Domain:

* improves clue interpretation, contradiction detection, and evidence confidence

Survival Domain:

* improves travel, weather response, foraging, hazard avoidance, and endurance

Social Domain:

* improves negotiation, persuasion, rumor management, witness handling, and reputation scenes

Leadership Domain:

* improves party coordination, workers, morale, command, and domain/labor scenes

13.3 Domain rank and Context

Relevant Domain rank can appear in Context when it matters.

Example:

In a magical training scene, Context might mention:

The player has Practiced Magic Domain experience but Mana Slash is still unstable.

Do not dump all domains every turn.

⸻

14. Ability Growth and progression relationship

Ability Growth should not be reduced to simple XP only.

Abilities should grow through:

* evidence
* attempts
* failures
* partial success
* repeated use
* teacher guidance
* breakthroughs
* cost observation
* limit discovery
* refinement
* mastery

AbilityReducer owns those records.

ProgressionReducer may still award:

* Magic Domain XP
* relevant Stat XP
* small Level XP for breakthroughs
* general progression milestones

Example:

A new ability stabilizes.

Possible progression effects:

* AbilityReducer promotes candidate to stable ability
* ProgressionReducer awards Magic Domain XP
* ProgressionReducer awards INT XP
* ProgressionReducer may award Level XP if the breakthrough was major
* CardSync may later mirror stable ability if card-eligible

The important rule:

Ability stabilization is not simply “reach 100 XP.”

It should require evidence and story confirmation.

Chunk 6 will define the full ability lifecycle.

⸻

15. Progression and failure

Failure should be meaningful but not exploitable.

15.1 Failure can teach

A failed real attempt may give:

* small Stat XP
* small Domain XP
* ability evidence
* clue/progress
* relationship consequence
* tactical knowledge

Example:

The player fails to climb a wall but learns the stone is slick and weak on one side.

That may produce:

* small SPD or DEF XP
* Survival/Travel Domain XP
* scene clue
* no successful climb

15.2 Failure must cost something

Meaningful failure should usually involve:

* time
* EP
* MP
* risk
* injury
* lost opportunity
* social consequence
* material loss
* alerting enemies

If failure costs nothing, reward should be tiny or zero.

15.3 Hard-denied failure gives no progress

No progression from:

* pretend failure
* dream failure
* quoted failure
* debug examples
* non-canon text
* intentionally fake attempts

⸻

16. Progression and story realism

Progression should follow the story’s context.

AIDRPG should ask:

* Was the action real?
* Was it meaningful?
* Was there effort?
* Was there cost?
* Was there risk?
* Was there feedback?
* Did the player learn something?
* Did something change?
* Was the output direct narration or weak flavor?
* Does the current character plausibly grow from this?

Example:

“You train with the sword for five minutes.”

Likely reward:

* tiny ATK XP
* maybe tiny Melee Domain XP
* no Level XP

“You spend the afternoon drilling sword forms under Captain Jora’s corrections until your arms tremble.”

Likely reward:

* small/moderate ATK XP
* small/moderate Melee Domain XP
* EP loss
* possible fatigue
* no or tiny Level XP unless this is a meaningful milestone

“You duel Captain Jora and barely survive his final strike, learning to read his shoulder movement.”

Likely reward:

* moderate ATK/DEF/SPD XP
* Melee Domain XP
* Level XP
* injury/EP cost
* possible relationship or reputation change

⸻

17. Progression and scaling

Progression must scale across the whole game.

Early game:

* small rewards feel meaningful
* stat increases happen occasionally
* abilities are unstable
* ordinary enemies matter

Mid game:

* domain ranks distinguish build
* abilities stabilize
* XP-to-next grows
* stat growth slows
* quests and combat give larger rewards

Late game:

* minor actions give little progress
* major achievements drive leveling
* mastery requires special conditions
* world-scale actions matter
* domain projects and reputation can become major progression sources

The progression system should avoid both extremes:

Bad extreme 1:

The player gains nothing unless they complete huge quests.

Bad extreme 2:

The player levels every few turns from random narration.

The middle path is:

Small actions build lanes gradually. Major validated achievements move level meaningfully.

⸻

18. Progression validation outcomes

Progression events should use the same validation outcomes as every other event.

Accept

Use when the progression source is confirmed and meaningful.

Example:

The player defeats an even-threat bandit after a real fight.

Result:

* combat XP accepted
* relevant Stat/Domain XP accepted

Reject

Use when the event does not justify progression.

Example:

“The bard says you must have grown stronger.”

Result:

* XP rejected
* reason: social statement, no confirmed growth action

Pending

Use when progress may come later.

Example:

The player starts a week-long apprenticeship.

Result:

* training plan pending
* no full reward until time/work is confirmed

Soft_fail

Use when the effort was real but success was partial or failed.

Example:

The spell fizzles after real concentration.

Result:

* ability evidence maybe
* small Magic Domain XP maybe
* no stable ability

Hard_deny

Use when the source is non-canon or fake.

Example:

“I pretend to become level 10.”

Result:

* hard deny
* no XP
* no level

19. Progression event examples

Example 1 — Safe training

Input:

“I practice sword forms for an hour.”

Output:

“You drill basic cuts until sweat runs down your back.”

Events:

* intent.train
* progression.statTraining.gain ATK
* progression.domainXp.gain Melee
* resource.ep.change
* time.pass

Validation:

* accept small ATK XP
* accept small Melee Domain XP
* accept EP loss
* accept time passage if clear

State result:

* no level-up unless thresholds reached
* no instant stat increase unless ATK XP threshold reached
* no ability unless ability rules detect relevant candidate

Example 2 — Repeated training spam

Input repeated many times:

“I practice sword forms again.”

Output repeats similar training.

Events:

* progression.statTraining.gain
* progression.domainXp.gain

Validation:

* accept reduced reward or reject after repetition
* antiFarm flag recorded

State result:

* diminished XP
* EP/fatigue may continue
* no infinite farm

Example 3 — Failed ability attempt

Output:

“You try to form Mana Slash, but the light sputters out.”

Events:

* ability.attempt
* progression.domainXp.gain Magic maybe
* progression.statTraining.gain INT maybe
* resource.mp.change maybe

Validation:

* ability.attempt soft_fail
* small Magic/INT progression accepted if meaningful
* no stable ability

Example 4 — Quest completion

Output:

“The mayor thanks you for returning the missing ring and declares the matter settled.”

Events:

* quest.complete
* progression.xp.gain
* reputation.public.change maybe
* inventory.coins.gain or item.gain if reward given

Validation:

* quest.complete accepted if quest objective matches
* XP accepted
* reward accepted only if actually granted

State result:

* quest archived
* XP gained
* reputation maybe changed
* no reward invented if not mentioned

Example 5 — Rumored victory

Output:

“People say you defeated the ogre in the hills.”

Events:

* reputation.rumor.add
* progression.xp.gain maybe falsely detected

Validation:

* rumor accepted as rumor if relevant
* XP rejected
* ogre defeat not canon unless already confirmed

State result:

* no Level XP from rumor
* possible rumor record

Example 6 — Duplicate combat output

Output processed twice:

“You drive your blade through the wolf, and it collapses.”

Events:

* combat victory
* XP gain
* possible loot
* actor/group defeated

Validation:

* first pass accepted
* second pass duplicate skipped by OperationLedger

State result:

* XP only once
* wolf defeated once
* no duplicate loot

21. Progression card behavior

Progression should be mirrored carefully.

Protected cards may include progression summaries.

Possible protected card mirrors:

* Player Sheet
* Progression Summary
* Abilities Overview
* Domain Summary if needed

Generated cards should usually not exist for ordinary XP.

Do not create a Story Card for every stat gain.

Do not create a Story Card for every training scene.

CardSync may update protected summaries when progression state changes.

Ability cards may update when stable ability growth changes.

Domain or specialization summaries may update when build identity changes.

But XP events themselves should usually stay in state/audit, not become cards.

22. Progression safety rules

The progression system must enforce these safety rules.

22.1 No XP from non-canon text

No XP from:

* dreams
* rumors
* quotes
* pretend actions
* debug text
* card text
* hypothetical scenes
* jokes

22.2 No direct parser XP

Parsers may propose XP events.

ProgressionReducer commits XP.

22.3 No duplicate XP

OperationLedger blocks repeated output.

22.4 No huge weak XP

Weak narration cannot grant large rewards.

22.5 No free infinite training

Training requires time, effort, cost, risk, teacher, challenge, or diminishing returns.

22.6 No level-only power logic

Level influences plausibility, but stats, abilities, equipment, resources, injuries, domain ranks, party, terrain, and story context still matter.

22.7 No ability stabilization from XP alone

Ability evidence and story confirmation matter.

22.8 No reward without owner routing

Combat, ability, crafting, quest, and investigation systems may propose progression rewards.

ProgressionReducer owns the actual XP.

24. Chunk 4 acceptance tests

These are design-level tests later code must satisfy.

Test 1 — safe training gives small relevant progress

Given:

The player spends one hour practicing sword forms.

Expected:

* ATK XP increases modestly
* Melee Domain XP increases modestly
* EP decreases
* time passes if confirmed
* no instant level unless threshold reached
* no new ability unless ability evidence supports it

Test 2 — repeated safe training diminishes

Given:

The player repeats identical sword training many times in the same context.

Expected:

* XP rewards diminish
* anti-farm record appears
* EP/fatigue may still accumulate
* no infinite farming

Test 3 — failed real ability gives evidence, not success

Given:

The player attempts Mana Slash and fails.

Expected:

* ability attempt soft-fails
* possible ability evidence
* possible Magic Domain XP / INT XP
* possible MP cost
* no stable ability

Test 4 — pretend ability gives no progress

Given:

The player pretends to cast Mana Slash.

Expected:

* hard deny
* no ability evidence
* no XP
* no card

Test 5 — meaningful combat gives appropriate XP

Given:

The player survives an even-threat fight.

Expected:

* Level XP may increase
* relevant Stat XP increases
* relevant Domain XP increases
* resource/injury consequences may apply
* duplicate output does not apply twice

Test 6 — trivial combat gives reduced XP

Given:

A high-level player defeats a trivial rat.

Expected:

* little or no Level XP
* maybe tiny relevant Stat/Domain XP
* no major reward

Test 7 — quest completion gives major XP only when confirmed

Given:

Quest objective is directly fulfilled.

Expected:

* quest completion accepted
* Level XP awarded
* reward events emitted if stated
* quest archived

Given:

Rumor says the quest was completed.

Expected:

* no quest completion
* no XP
* possible rumor record

Test 8 — Level XP and Total XP separate correctly

Given:

Player gains 100 Level XP and levels up.

Expected:

* current XP rolls over or resets according to curve
* total XP increases by 100
* level increases only if threshold crossed

Test 9 — stat increase requires enough Stat XP

Given:

Player gains a small amount of ATK XP.

Expected:

* ATK XP increases
* ATK stat does not increase until threshold reached

Test 10 — domain rank requires threshold

Given:

Player gains several small Magic Domain XP rewards.

Expected:

* Magic Domain XP accumulates
* Magic Domain rank increases only when threshold is reached

Test 11 — duplicate output blocked

Given:

Same XP-granting output is processed twice.

Expected:

* first pass commits XP
* second pass is skipped
* audit shows duplicate skip

Test 12 — weak narration capped

Given:

Output says “You feel much stronger now” with no supporting action.

Expected:

* no major XP
* no stat increase
* maybe no reward or tiny pending flavor note

25. Final Chunk 4 doctrine

Progression should reward meaningful confirmed play.

Level represents broad adventure maturity.

Total XP records lifetime growth.

Domain XP records broad competence.

Stat Training XP records specific physical/mental growth.

Ability Growth records evidence, reliability, stabilization, cost knowledge, and mastery through AbilityReducer.

One event can reward multiple lanes, but each lane has an owner.

Failure can teach, but it cannot lie.

Training can grow the player, but it must cost time, effort, or risk.

Combat can reward the player, but only if the consequence is validated and the threat is meaningful.

Quest milestones can grant major progress, but only when truly completed.

Duplicate outputs never give duplicate rewards.

Weak narration never grants huge growth.

Level helps scale the story, but stats, domains, abilities, equipment, resources, injuries, party, terrain, and context still matter.

The goal is not to make numbers dominate the story.

The goal is to make the story’s consequences accumulate into believable growth.
