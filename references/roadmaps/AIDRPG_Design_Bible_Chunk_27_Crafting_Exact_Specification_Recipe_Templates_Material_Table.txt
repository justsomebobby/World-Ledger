AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 27: Crafting Exact Specification, Recipe Templates, Material Tables, Quality Math, Repair, Upgrade, Enchanting, and Output Certainty

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 27 defines how AIDRPG handles crafting, repairs, upgrades, recipes, materials, tools, stations, item quality, failure, partial success, and crafted item truth.

Earlier chunks established:

* level scaling
* race/species scaling
* stats and resources
* XP/progression
* creature/enemy tiers
* combat math
* abilities and costs
* absorption/anime-style growth

Chunk 27 answers:

How does crafting actually work?

What is required to craft an item?

How does the system know if the player has the materials?

How does item quality get determined?

How do repairs work?

How do upgrades work?

How does enchanting work?

How does magical crafting differ from normal crafting?

When are materials consumed?

When does an item become real?

How does the system stop AI Dungeon from randomly granting legendary items?

How does crafting work in freeform play when the player says things like “I try to make a dagger from the scrap metal”?

The central rule is:

Crafting is a validated project, not an instant item generator.

A crafted item becomes canon only when:

* the recipe or design is plausible
* required materials exist and are accessible
* required tools/station are available or substituted
* required time/effort is spent
* skill/domain/stat checks are plausible
* output is validated
* materials are consumed or reserved correctly
* item quality is determined
* operation ledger prevents duplication
* InventoryReducer commits the item

2. Core philosophy

AI Dungeon crafting is freeform.

The player may write:

* “I sharpen my sword.”
* “I try to repair my armor.”
* “I make a dagger from scrap metal.”
* “I brew a healing potion.”
* “I use the slime core to make acid-resistant gloves.”
* “I enchant my sword with fire.”
* “I combine the wolf fang and mana crystal.”
* “I ask Mara to repair the sword.”
* “I have the workers build a wall.”
* “I craft armor from dragon scales.”
* “I make a living slime shield.”
* “I use Earth knowledge to invent gunpowder.”
* “I try to build a generator.”
* “I improvise a trap from rope and broken glass.”

AIDRPG must allow this creativity.

But it must not allow:

* crafting items from nonexistent materials
* instantly making complex items with no tools
* creating legendary artifacts at Level 1 from scraps
* duplicating crafted outputs
* consuming materials twice
* gaining XP repeatedly from the same craft
* treating recipe ideas as completed items
* letting Story Cards create inventory
* letting AI Dungeon randomly say “you now have a divine sword” without validation

Crafting should feel flexible but grounded.

The player should be rewarded for preparation, materials, tools, recipes, skill, time, and creativity.

4. Crafting categories

AIDRPG should distinguish crafting types.

Basic crafting

Making simple mundane items.

Examples:

* rope snare
* wooden spear
* torch
* bandage
* simple meal
* crude knife

Repair

Restoring condition of an existing item.

Examples:

* fix sword edge
* patch armor
* mend cloak
* repair shield
* restore cracked focus

Maintenance

Preventing degradation.

Examples:

* oil sword
* clean armor
* recharge minor item
* sharpen blade
* preserve leather

Upgrade

Improving an existing item.

Examples:

* reinforce shield
* add silver edge
* improve bowstring
* fit armor to dragonkin tail
* add hidden sheath

Enchanting

Adding magical properties.

Examples:

* fire blade
* frost-resistant cloak
* mana-conductive staff
* self-repairing armor
* light charm

Alchemy

Mixing ingredients into potions, oils, powders, acids, medicines, toxins, or catalysts.

Cooking

Preparing food with possible buffs, healing support, morale effects, or poison risks.

Engineering

Constructing devices, traps, mechanisms, machines, or technical objects.

Ritual crafting

Creating magical items through circles, sacrifices, chants, moon phases, divine/demonic contracts, or rare events.

Biological/monster crafting

Using monster parts, cores, blood, scales, slime, bone, organs, venom, hide, or spirit residue.

Domain-scale construction

Building walls, workshops, roads, farms, mines, defenses, homes, guild halls, or holdings.

Each category needs different requirements.

5. Crafting project lifecycle

Every non-trivial craft should follow a lifecycle.

5.1 Idea

The player wants to craft something.

No item exists yet.

5.2 Design / Recipe Candidate

The system determines whether the idea is plausible and what it needs.

5.3 Requirement Check

Materials, tools, station, time, skill, and safety are checked.

5.4 Material Reservation

Materials may be reserved before work begins.

5.5 Work In Progress

Crafting progresses over time, actions, or scene beats.

5.6 Partial Product

A partially complete item may exist.

5.7 Completion Attempt

The system evaluates quality and success.

5.8 Output

Item is created, repaired, upgraded, failed, damaged, or partially successful.

5.9 Inventory Commit

InventoryReducer creates or updates the item.

5.10 Cleanup / Archive

Crafting project is completed, failed, abandoned, or archived.

Important:

The item should not exist as usable inventory until the output is committed.

8. Recipe knowledge states

Recipes should have knowledge states.

Recipe State	Meaning
Unknown	player does not know recipe
Idea	player has concept but not process
Candidate	plausible recipe being tested
Partial	some requirements known
Known	recipe can be attempted reliably
Practiced	player has made it before
Mastered	high reliability and quality
Secret/Locked	requires discovery, teacher, book, guild, ritual
Forbidden	dangerous or illegal
Corrupted	recipe works but has taint/risk
Improvised	no formal recipe, penalty applies

Example:

Player says:

“I make a healing potion.”

If no recipe/materials:

* recipe unknown or candidate
* cannot instantly create potion

Player has recipe book and herbs:

* recipe known
* can attempt

Player has made it many times:

* practiced/mastered, lower failure risk

11. Material substitution

Players will improvise.

Substitution should be allowed if plausible.

Substitution types:

* equivalent material
* lower-quality substitute
* higher-quality substitute
* magical substitute
* improvised substitute
* incompatible substitute
* dangerous substitute

Examples:

Wooden handle substitute:

* bone handle may work
* leather wrap may work
* slime handle probably unstable unless treated

Healing herb substitute:

* similar herb may work at penalty
* poisonous herb may create toxin
* unknown glowing mushroom is risky

Iron ingot substitute:

* scrap iron may work at lower quality
* silver may change properties
* dragon bone changes recipe

Substitution should modify:

* difficulty
* output quality
* failure risk
* item properties
* material consumption

15. Crafting score

Crafting Score:

Crafting Score =
INT
+ relevant Domain Bonus
+ recipe familiarity bonus
+ tool/station bonus
+ material quality modifier
+ assistant/labor bonus
+ ability/passive bonus
+ time/preparation bonus
- missing tool penalty
- bad material penalty
- injury/fatigue penalty
- unsafe environment penalty
- complexity penalty

Relevant domains may include:

* Crafting
* Smithing
* Alchemy
* Enchanting
* Cooking
* Tailoring
* Engineering
* Medicine
* Ritual Magic
* Survival
* Construction
* Domain Management

For physical labor-heavy crafting, DEF/ATK/EP may also matter.

For precise work, SPD may matter slightly.

For unstable magical crafting, LCK may matter.

⸻

16. Crafting score ratio

Compare Crafting Score to target.

Crafting Ratio = Crafting Score / Crafting Target

Ratio	Expected Result
below 0.25	impossible or severe failure
0.25–0.5	likely failure, maybe crude partial
0.5–0.75	difficult, poor/crude output possible
0.75–1.0	contested, common output possible
1.0–1.25	success, common/good output
1.25–1.75	good/fine output possible
1.75–2.5	excellent output possible
2.5–4.0	masterwork possible
4.0+	superior output, but capped by materials/tools/recipe

Important:

High skill cannot make legendary output from trash materials unless the craft is about transforming trash with magic.

Materials and tools impose quality caps.

18. Quality cap rule

Output quality cannot exceed the limiting factors without special abilities.

Quality cap factors:

* material quality
* tool quality
* station quality
* recipe knowledge
* crafter domain rank
* time spent
* magic support
* item complexity
* player level/story scale

Examples:

Poor iron + campfire + no forge:

* cannot produce fine sword normally

Dragon scale + master forge + master smith:

* can produce legendary armor candidate

Unknown recipe + rare materials:

* high risk; rare materials do not guarantee success

Master crafter with poor tools:

* can make good improvised work, but likely not masterwork

This prevents “I make a legendary sword from rusty nails.”

20. Material reservation and consumption

Materials should not always be consumed immediately.

Use reservation states:

Reserved

Materials are set aside for project.

Partially consumed

Some materials used during progress.

Fully consumed

Materials spent.

Returned

Unused materials returned if project stopped early.

Ruined

Materials lost due to failure.

Transformed

Materials become part of output.

Byproduct

Leftover product created.

Material consumption timing depends on craft type.

Examples:

Potion ingredients are consumed during brewing.

Metal ingot becomes blade during forging.

Enchantment crystal may be consumed at ritual completion.

Leather patches may be partially used.

Monster core may remain intact until final infusion.

This prevents “start craft, cancel, materials vanish” bugs unless failure justifies it.

21. Crafting output certainty

Crafting outcomes should be classified.

Certain output

Recipe known, materials/tools available, difficulty low.

Example:

Lighting torch from existing torch and oil.

Probable output

Reasonable craft with adequate skill.

Example:

Repairing minor sword nick at forge.

Uncertain output

Recipe partial, materials substitute, difficulty high.

Example:

Making healing potion from unfamiliar herbs.

Experimental output

New invention or magical attempt.

Example:

Combining slime core and metal shield.

Dangerous output

High risk, volatile materials, curse, powerful magic.

Example:

Binding demon flame into sword.

Impossible output

Requirements impossible or missing.

Example:

Crafting full plate armor instantly in forest with no metal/tools.

AIDRPG should not treat all crafting attempts equally.

22. Failure result categories

Crafting can fail in different ways.

Failure types:

* no progress
* wasted time
* wasted material
* damaged material
* damaged base item
* low-quality output
* dangerous flawed item
* unstable magic
* injury
* poison/explosion
* corruption
* broken tool
* recipe insight despite failure
* partial component produced
* byproduct created
* project delayed

Failure should be based on craft type.

Alchemy failure may create fumes or poison.

Smithing failure may crack metal.

Enchanting failure may drain MP or curse item.

Engineering failure may create unstable mechanism.

Cooking failure may create bad food.

23. Partial success categories

Partial success is important for freeform play.

Partial success examples:

* crude version made
* item works once
* item has reduced durability
* item has side effect
* item is incomplete but usable as component
* repair improves condition but not fully
* enchantment weak but stable
* potion works but causes fatigue
* trap works but is obvious
* armor fits poorly
* weapon is sharp but brittle
* recipe knowledge improves

Partial success should often be preferred over binary failure.

24. Item condition scale

Items need condition.

Condition scale:

Condition	Meaning
pristine	perfect/new
excellent	very good
good	normal functional
worn	functional but used
damaged	reduced effectiveness
badly damaged	unreliable
broken	unusable without repair
ruined	cannot be repaired normally
corrupted	dangerous/tainted
unstable	may fail or backlash
consumed	no longer exists

Repairs move items upward.

Combat/use moves items downward.

Enchanting may improve or destabilize condition.

⸻

25. Item quality versus condition

Quality and condition are different.

Quality:

How well the item was made.

Condition:

Current state of wear/damage.

Example:

A masterwork sword can be badly damaged.

A crude dagger can be pristine.

Item record should track both:

Quality: masterwork
Condition: damaged

Repair improves condition.

Upgrade may improve quality or add trait.

Crafting creates initial quality and condition.

33. Charges, cooldowns, and maintenance

Possible limits:

* charges
* daily uses
* MP recharge
* cooldown
* attunement
* maintenance ritual
* material fuel
* sunlight/moonlight recharge
* blood cost
* repair risk
* corruption buildup
* oath/contract requirement

Example:

* recharges at dawn
* using all charges overheats ring

49. Crafting anti-farm

Anti-farm rules:

* mass-producing trivial items decays
* repeated same recipe below skill gives little/no XP
* safe known recipe gives minimal XP
* auto-worker production gives little/no player XP
* failed attempts from same mistake decay
* duplicated output does not duplicate XP
* crafting useless items solely for XP decays
* material/source caps apply

Example:

Player makes 100 wooden spoons at Level 100.

* maybe no Level XP
* tiny/no Crafting XP after decay
* output may exist if materials/time spent, but no farming

Player makes first high-quality enchanted spoon from rare fae wood for a quest.

* meaningful XP possible because story/material/difficulty matter

62. Final Chunk 27 doctrine

Crafting is validated creation.

AIDRPG should support freeform crafting, repairs, upgrades, enchanting, alchemy, monster-part crafting, living items, engineering, and domain construction.

But every craft needs requirements.

Materials must exist.

Tools and stations matter.

Time matters.

Recipe knowledge matters.

Skill and domains matter.

Quality is capped by materials, tools, station, recipe, and crafter ability.

Failure and partial success should be possible.

Repairs update existing items instead of duplicating them.

Upgrades add traits and tradeoffs.

Enchantments require power, cost, limits, and stability.

Monster parts require harvesting and compatibility.

Living materials require special handling.

NPC crafting changes custody and access.

Domain-scale construction needs labor, materials, money, and time.

Crafting XP must be meaningful and anti-farm protected.

Crafted items become canon only when InventoryReducer commits them.

Story Cards do not create items.

AI Dungeon narration is validated before output becomes state.

The goal is to let players create clever, personalized, story-driven items without letting crafting become an uncontrolled generator of free legendary gear, duplicated inventory, impossible modern technology, or unsupported magic artifacts.
