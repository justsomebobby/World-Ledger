AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 25: Ability Cost Calculator, Skill/Passive/Talent Templates, Scaling, Cooldowns, and Failure Modes

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 25 defines how AIDRPG handles abilities, skills, passives, talents, traits, specializations, and power costs.

Earlier chunks established:

* level scaling
* race/species logic
* stats and resources
* XP growth
* enemy tiers
* combat math

Chunk 25 answers:

How does AIDRPG determine the cost of an ability?

How does a weak ability differ from a mythic ability?

How does a candidate become a stable ability?

How does a stable ability improve over time?

How do passives work without becoming infinite immunity?

How do talents differ from abilities?

How do racial traits differ from skills?

How does AIDRPG handle freeform anime-style powers without letting every idea become real?

How does the system decide whether an ability costs HP, MP, EP, time, materials, cooldown, focus, or risk?

The central rule is:

Abilities are validated capabilities, not just things the AI or player mentions.

An ability must have:

* source
* category
* activation rule
* effect
* cost
* limits
* failure modes
* scaling path
* evidence
* rank
* owner
* update rules

No ability should be infinite, free, perfectly reliable, and universally useful unless the campaign explicitly supports godlike play and the ability has equally meaningful limits.

⸻

2. Core philosophy

AI Dungeon freeform play creates powers constantly.

The player may write:

* “I try to channel mana through my sword.”
* “I copy the monster’s movement.”
* “I breathe fire.”
* “I turn my shadow into a blade.”
* “I let the slime cover my arm as armor.”
* “I adapt to the poison.”
* “I use my luck to find a weak point.”
* “I absorb the wolf’s strength.”
* “I focus until my body hardens.”
* “I try to remember boxing from Earth.”
* “I ask the spirit if I have any skills.”
* “I activate Quick Learner.”
* “I use my passive regeneration.”

AIDRPG must support this creativity.

But it must not allow:

* every attempt to instantly create a stable ability
* every anime idea to work for free
* every copied power to become mastered instantly
* every passive to be always-on immunity
* every failed attempt to become success
* every AI-generated skill name to become canon
* ability cards to appear for candidates
* abilities to ignore MP/EP/HP costs
* high-level abilities to have low-level costs
* low-level characters to freely use world-scale effects

The system should allow creativity through candidate abilities, partial success, evidence, cost discovery, failure modes, and stabilization.

4. Ability categories

AIDRPG should separate capability types.

Active Ability

A deliberate power or technique the player activates.

Examples:

* Mana Slash
* Firebolt
* Shadow Step
* Healing Touch
* Dragon Breath
* Slime Armor
* Blood Spear

Skill

A trained action or technique, usually mundane or semi-mundane.

Examples:

* Sword Guard
* Boxing Jab
* Lockpicking
* Silent Step
* Shield Bash
* First Aid

Skills may cost EP, tools, time, or focus.

Spell

A magical active ability.

Examples:

* Fireball
* Ice Lance
* Barrier
* Detect Magic
* Teleport

Spells usually cost MP.

Passive

A trait that works automatically under conditions.

Examples:

* Dragonkin Scales
* Undead Body
* Wolfkin Scent
* Quick Learner
* Fire Resistance
* Regeneration

Passives must have scope and limits.

Talent

A learned or natural style advantage.

Examples:

* Brawler’s Instinct
* Mana Sensitivity
* Tactical Eye
* Improvised Weapon Talent
* Slime Compatibility

Talents influence actions but are not usually activated like spells.

Trait

A body, race, curse, blessing, or origin property.

Examples:

* Slime Body
* Construct Body
* Fairy Wings
* Vampire Hunger
* Cursed Bloodline

Traits often alter validation.

Specialization

A build-defining focus.

Examples:

* Mana Brawler
* Fire Mage
* Defensive Sword Saint
* Adaptive Slime Knight
* Shadow Duelist

Specializations guide growth and ability interpretation.

Ultimate / Mythic Ability

A high-cost, high-scale, story-defining ability.

Examples:

* World-Cutting Slash
* Sunfall Ritual
* Divine Resurrection
* Dragon Sovereign Breath
* Reality Anchor

These require high rank, major costs, cooldowns, or story constraints.

5. Ability lifecycle

Every ability should follow a lifecycle.

5.1 Not known

No ability exists.

A mention alone does not create it.

5.2 Hint

Something suggests a possible ability.

Example:

The player feels warmth in their palms when focusing mana.

5.3 Candidate

There is enough evidence to track a possible ability.

Example:

Mana gathers along the sword but collapses.

5.4 Unstable

The ability manifests sometimes but is unreliable.

Example:

Mana Slash appears once in panic but cannot be repeated consistently.

5.5 Basic stable

The player can use it in calm or ordinary conditions.

5.6 Reliable

The player can use it under pressure with known cost.

5.7 Refined

The player understands cost, range, limits, and counters.

5.8 Advanced

The ability is strong and flexible.

5.9 Expert / Signature

The ability is central to the build.

5.10 Legendary / Mythic

The ability affects boss fights, regions, domains, or world-scale events.

5.11 Evolved / Variant

The ability develops into a stronger form or branch.

5.12 Sealed / Lost / Corrupted

The ability becomes unavailable, changed, or dangerous.

Cards should only be generated for stable, important abilities.

Candidates should stay in protected Abilities overview only.

7. Ability evidence types

Abilities grow through evidence.

Evidence types:

* attempted
* failed attempt
* partial manifestation
* successful use
* controlled use
* stressful use
* repeated use
* taught use
* observed technique
* copied pattern
* absorbed source
* breakthrough
* backlash
* limitation discovered
* cost discovered
* weakness discovered
* counter discovered
* variant discovered
* evolution trigger
* sealed/lost event

Evidence must be validated.

Pretend use, dream use, quotes, card text, or jokes do not count unless the campaign explicitly makes them real.

⸻

8. Evidence quality

Not all evidence is equal.

Evidence Quality	Value
invalid	ignored
weak	small candidate support
normal	useful development
strong	major development
breakthrough	may stabilize or rank up
story milestone	may evolve ability

Examples:

Weak evidence:

* mana flickers once during practice

Normal evidence:

* weak Mana Slash cuts grass

Strong evidence:

* Mana Slash wounds enemy in real combat

Breakthrough:

* Mana Slash works under pressure after training and cost is understood

Story milestone:

* Mana Slash evolves after defeating sword master or absorbing dragon mana

Evidence quality matters more than raw count.

9. Ability discovery rules

An ability may be discovered from:

* race/species trait
* class/background
* training
* combat stress
* magical item
* teacher
* book/recipe/manual
* observed enemy technique
* absorbed essence/core
* divine blessing
* curse
* mutation
* dream that has real supernatural consequences
* system/status interface
* repeated creative attempts
* story milestone

Discovery should not happen from:

* unsupported player claim
* random AI list with no source
* Story Card text alone
* debug text
* command output
* hypothetical statement
* joke
* failed pretend action

Example:

Player says:

“I have Fireball.”

If not established:

* reject as stable ability
* maybe create candidate only if context supports magical background

Player says:

“I try to form fire in my hand.”

AI says:

“A tiny spark appears, then dies.”

* create Fire Spark or Fire Magic candidate, not full Fireball

⸻

10. Ability stabilization rules

An ability stabilizes when enough evidence proves:

* activation method
* effect
* cost or cost range
* repeatability
* limits
* failure pattern
* relevant stat/domain support
* source legitimacy

Minimum stabilization should usually require:

* at least one successful manifestation
* at least one repeated or controlled use
* cost paid or cost estimated
* no major contradiction
* ability is not merely dream/quote/card text

Example:

Mana Slash candidate becomes stable when:

* mana forms on blade more than once
* at least one controlled slash produces real effect
* MP/EP cost is observed
* player can intentionally activate it
* it does not rely on one-time artifact surge unless that is part of ability

11. Ability cost philosophy

Every meaningful active ability should have some cost or limit.

Cost types:

* MP
* EP
* HP
* time
* cooldown
* concentration
* material
* item charge
* ammunition
* stance/position
* environmental requirement
* risk
* corruption
* fatigue
* backlash
* debt/oath
* summon upkeep
* social/legal cost
* opportunity cost

Not every ability needs MP.

Not every ability needs EP.

But every ability needs at least one balancing factor unless it is tiny or passive with narrow scope.

Examples:

Sword Slash:

* EP cost

Fireball:

* MP cost

Mana Slash:

* MP + EP cost

Blood Spear:

* HP + MP cost

Teleport:

* MP + cooldown + risk

Regeneration:

* passive, but costs time/energy/nutrition/mana or has counter conditions

Quick Learner:

* passive, but affects growth rate only and obeys anti-farm

12. Cost class table

Ability costs should use cost classes.

Cost Class	Meaning	Resource Cost
0	negligible / passive	0 or flavor only
1	tiny	1–2% of relevant max resource
2	light	3–5%
3	moderate	6–10%
4	heavy	11–20%
5	severe	21–35%
6	extreme	36–50%
7	overcharge	50%+ and risk
8	sacrificial	HP/life/lasting condition/story cost
9	mythic	requires special story resource, ritual, domain, or cooldown

Relevant max resource means:

* max MP for magic
* max EP for physical exertion
* max HP for blood/body cost
* special pool if ability has one

Percentage costs scale naturally from Level 1 to Level 1000.

13. Base ability effect tier

Before cost is calculated, determine effect tier.

Effect Tier	Meaning	Example
0	cosmetic	small glow, harmless spark
1	minor utility	light, small push, brief sense
2	basic combat/utility	weak bolt, short dash, minor heal
3	standard combat	Mana Slash, Firebolt, Shield Bash
4	strong combat	Fireball, Shadow Step, strong heal
5	advanced	barrier dome, multi-target strike
6	elite	battlefield control, powerful summon
7	legendary	dragon breath-scale attack, resurrection-lite
8	mythic	army-scale effect, large ritual
9	world-scale	city/region-scale effect
10	cosmic/divine	reality, fate, god-scale effect

Effect tier should match player level, ability rank, resources, and story.

A Level 5 candidate ability should usually be Tier 1–2.

A Level 1000 mythic ability may reach Tier 8–10 with proper cost and constraints.

16. Ability rank cost modifier

Higher rank improves efficiency.

Ability Rank	Cost Modifier
Candidate	cannot use reliably; cost unknown/high
1 Unstable	+2 cost class or backlash risk
2 Basic	+1 cost class
3 Reliable	+0
4 Practiced	-0 to -1
5 Refined	-1
6 Advanced	-1 to -2
7 Expert	-2
8 Master	-2 to -3
9 Legendary	-3 for familiar uses, but higher-scale variants cost more
10 Mythic	efficiency high, but mythic effects still huge
11+	special scaling

Important:

Rank lowers cost for known uses.

It does not make world-scale variants free.

A Rank 9 Fireball may cast ordinary fireballs cheaply.

A city-burning inferno still costs heavily.

17. Domain/stat support cost modifier

Relevant domain and stat support can reduce cost or failure.

Use:

* INT for magic/control
* ATK for forceful physical powers
* DEF for barriers/body hardening
* SPD for movement powers
* LCK for unstable/fate powers
* relevant domain rank
* specialization/build identity

Support levels:

Support Quality	Effect
poor support	+1 to +3 cost/failure
adequate support	no change
strong support	-1 cost or failure
excellent support	-2 cost or failure
perfect specialization	-2 to -3, but not below minimum

Example:

A Mana Brawler using Mana Punch:

* strong ATK/INT/domain support
* cost lower and reliability higher

A pure scholar trying Dragon Breath with no dragon trait:

* invalid or high failure

⸻

18. Minimum cost rule

Cost reductions cannot reduce meaningful abilities to zero unless the ability is explicitly passive/free.

Minimum costs:

Ability Type	Minimum Cost
minor utility	can be 0–1
basic combat ability	at least tiny/light
standard combat ability	at least light/moderate
strong combat ability	at least moderate
advanced ability	at least heavy unless ritualized
legendary ability	severe/extreme/story cost
mythic ability	extreme/overcharge/special cost

Example:

A master swordsman’s Mana Slash may become cheap.

But if it still fires damaging mana, it should usually cost at least some MP/EP unless the campaign defines it as fully internalized.

19. Ability cost calculator workflow

When an ability is used:

1. Identify ability.
2. Check if ability exists, candidate, stable, or unknown.
3. Identify intended effect.
4. Determine effect tier.
5. Determine base cost class.
6. Add range modifier.
7. Add duration modifier.
8. Add target count modifier.
9. Add precision modifier.
10. Add speed modifier.
11. Add resistance modifier.
12. Apply ability rank modifier.
13. Apply domain/stat support modifier.
14. Apply focus/equipment modifier.
15. Apply environment modifier.
16. Apply current resource state.
17. Determine final cost type and amount.
18. Check if player can pay.
19. If cannot pay, reject, weaken, or overcharge.
20. Record cost evidence if ability cost was unknown.
21. Commit resource cost through BodyReducer.
22. Resolve effect through appropriate owner system.
23. Record ability evidence.
24. Mark dirty targets.
25. Audit result.

This workflow is mandatory for important abilities.

Minor flavor abilities can use simplified handling.

25. Example cost calculation — Regeneration passive

Ability:

Regeneration

Type:

Passive

Rank:

Depends on source

Passive cost model:

Regeneration does not need activation every time, but must have limits.

Possible costs/limits:

* consumes MP over time
* consumes EP/metabolism
* requires blood/food/mass
* blocked by fire/holy/silver/acid
* slower during combat
* cannot restore instantly
* does not regrow major parts until high rank
* fails when core/anchor destroyed
* causes hunger/fatigue

Rank examples:

Rank 1:

* closes minor scratches slowly

Rank 3:

* heals minor wounds during rest

Rank 5:

* heals moderate wounds over time

Rank 7:

* can regenerate serious injuries outside direct counter damage

Rank 9:

* restores lost limbs with cost/time/source

Rank 10:

* mythic regeneration, still has special counter

Regeneration must not become “cannot be harmed.”

31. Ability scaling dimensions

42. Ability failure modes

Ability failures should be specific.

Failure categories:

No activation

Nothing happens.

Weak activation

Effect appears but is too weak.

Misfire

Effect hits wrong place or behaves badly.

Backlash

User suffers resource loss, injury, condition, or instability.

Overload

Effect is too strong or uncontrolled.

Partial success

Effect works partly but not fully.

Delayed success

Effect starts but takes longer.

Corruption

Ability creates taint, mutation, curse, or mental effect.

Reversal

Effect rebounds or enemy absorbs/reflects it.

Collateral damage

Effect works but harms environment/ally/item.

Failure should depend on:

* rank
* cost paid
* resource state
* pressure
* enemy resistance
* environment
* body compatibility
* ability complexity

49. Ability and resource state

Low resources affect ability use.

Low MP

* spell weakens
* failure chance rises
* cost may be impossible
* overcharge possible
* backlash risk rises

Low EP

* physical techniques weaken
* movement skills fail
* dodges/slashes become risky
* overexertion possible

Low HP

* blood/body abilities dangerous
* concentration harder
* death-boundary risk

Fatigue

* cost increases
* precision drops
* ability rank effectively lowered for physical actions

Injury

* ability using injured body part impaired
* spell gestures/focus may fail
* breath weapon affected by throat/lung injury

Ability use should always check current state.

* target is resistant

* emotional crisis
* divine/demonic force intervenes
* artifact amplifies ability

Overcharge may produce:

* stronger effect
* partial success
* major cost
* backlash
* condition
* injury
* corruption
* temporary ability lock
* equipment damage
* unconsciousness
* death-boundary

Overcharge should be dramatic but dangerous.

62.1 Mention is not mastery

64. Final Chunk 25 doctrine

Abilities are earned, validated capabilities.

AIDRPG supports freeform creativity by allowing hints, candidates, unstable manifestations, partial success, failure evidence, breakthroughs, and evolution.

But it does not treat every idea as a stable power.

Active abilities need costs.

Passives need scope.

Talents guide actions.

Traits alter body and validation.

Specializations guide growth.

Costs depend on effect tier, range, duration, target count, precision, speed, resistance, rank, domain, stat support, equipment, environment, and current resources.

MP, EP, HP, time, cooldown, concentration, materials, risk, and story cost are all valid costs.

Low resources matter.

Overcharge is possible but dangerous.

Copying does not grant mastery.

Absorption does not bypass validation.

Healing does not erase all consequences.

Regeneration is not immortality.

Dragon Breath is not free because the player is dragonkin.

Shadow Step is not reliable because it was mentioned once.

Mana Slash should update over time as cost, limits, rank, and variants become known.

Generated ability cards belong only to stable important abilities.

The protected Abilities card tracks candidates and summaries.

Context should remind the AI of relevant ability limits without flooding it.

The goal is to make abilities feel flexible, anime-friendly, and exciting while keeping them bounded, explainable, scalable, and safe from freeform power inflation.
