AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 24: Combat Math, Damage Severity, Defense, Resources, Matchup Resolution, and Freeform Combat Validation

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 24 defines how AIDRPG handles combat consequences.

Chunk 19 defined player level scale.

Chunk 20 defined race/species and body logic.

Chunk 21 defined stats and resources.

Chunk 22 defined XP and growth.

Chunk 23 defined enemy tiers and threat scaling.

Chunk 24 answers:

How does combat actually resolve?

How does ATK compare to DEF?

How does SPD affect dodging?

How does armor reduce damage?

How much HP damage should a hit cause?

When does damage become an injury?

How do MP and EP costs apply during combat?

How does AIDRPG handle freeform fighting instead of rigid turn-based attacks?

How does the system handle overpowered AI narration like “you instantly kill the dragon”?

How does the system handle underpowered AI narration like “a rat instantly kills the Level 500 player”?

How do group fights, bosses, summons, companions, and unusual bodies work?

The central rule is:

Combat math should guide validation and consequence severity, not replace the story.

AIDRPG should not become a rigid dice system unless the user later chooses that mode.

Instead, combat math provides a grounded framework for freeform AI Dungeon narration.

⸻

2. Core philosophy

AI Dungeon combat is freeform.

The player may write:

“I punch him in the jaw.”

“I raise my shield.”

“I try to dodge between the slimes.”

“I throw sand in his eyes.”

“I use Mana Slash.”

“I tackle the goblin leader.”

“I aim for the dragon’s wounded wing.”

“I grab the slime core if I can see it.”

“I don’t fight; I back away slowly.”

“I try to talk the bandits down.”

AI Dungeon may respond with:

* clean success
* partial success
* failure
* injury
* exaggerated victory
* exaggerated defeat
* impossible action
* new enemy traits
* environmental changes
* ally actions
* enemy actions
* ambiguous consequences

AIDRPG should read the response and decide what is mechanically true.

It should not decide everything before the AI responds unless the player uses a command or the script is configured for stricter simulation.

Combat should feel like narrative RPG play with consistent consequences.

4. Combat is event-based, not turn-locked

AIDRPG should not require every combat to follow strict turns.

Instead, combat is made of events.

Combat event examples:

* attack attempt
* defense attempt
* dodge attempt
* spell cast
* ability use
* grapple
* shove
* retreat
* counterattack
* injury
* condition
* enemy defeat
* group casualty
* ally intervention
* morale break
* boss phase change
* environmental hazard
* overexertion
* surrender
* capture

A single AI output may contain several combat events.

Example:

“The goblin lunges, but you twist aside and cut across its arm.”

Possible events:

* enemy attack
* player dodge
* player counterattack
* enemy injury
* enemy HP damage
* EP cost

Each event must be validated and deduped separately.

5. Combat source rules

Combat consequences can come from:

* AI output
* player command/manual override
* validated script event
* migration only if preserving prior combat state

Combat consequences should not come from:

* Story Card text
* Context text
* command output
* debug text
* fake examples
* dreams unless dream combat has real consequences
* quotes
* rumors
* hypotheticals
* plans only
* player input alone unless manual/command mode

Player input usually creates an attempt.

AI output usually confirms or denies consequence.

Example:

Player input:

“I stab the wolf.”

This is an attack attempt.

It does not automatically damage the wolf unless the script mode supports pre-AI simulation or the AI output confirms it.

AI output:

“Your blade sinks into the wolf’s shoulder.”

This can create HP damage and injury.

8. Effective combat score

AIDRPG can use effective scores internally.

Suggested formula:

Effective Action Score =
Relevant Stat
+ Domain Bonus
+ Ability Rank Bonus
+ Equipment Bonus
+ Race/Body Modifier
+ Context Modifier
- Injury/Fatigue/Burden Penalty

Examples:

Sword attack:

ATK + Sword Domain + sword bonus + ability bonus - injury penalty

Dodge:

SPD + Evasion/Athletics Domain + terrain modifier - burden/fatigue penalty

Shield block:

DEF + Shield Domain + shield bonus - EP/fatigue penalty

Spell attack:

INT + Magic Domain + ability rank + focus item - low MP penalty

Grapple:

ATK + DEF partial + Brawling Domain + size modifier - injury penalty

Social intimidation in combat:

ATK or INT + Reputation + Social/Intimidation Domain + enemy morale context

The score is used to judge plausibility and severity.

It should not be displayed unless debug mode asks.

9. Attack versus defense comparison

For direct damaging actions, compare attacker offensive score to defender defensive score.

Attack Ratio = Attacker Offensive Score / Defender Defensive Score

Use this table:

Attack Ratio	Expected Result
below 0.25	nearly ineffective unless weakness/leverage
0.25–0.5	glancing/minor effect, hard to damage
0.5–0.75	low damage, needs good narration or repeated effort
0.75–1.25	contested, normal damage possible
1.25–1.75	solid damage likely
1.75–2.5	heavy damage likely
2.5–4.0	severe damage possible
4.0+	overwhelming unless special resistance

This ratio helps decide:

* whether AI damage is plausible
* whether HP damage should be small or large
* whether injury is possible
* whether a boss should resist
* whether a low-level enemy should be trivial

Example:

Player sword attack score 120.

Enemy defense score 240.

Ratio:

120 / 240 = 0.5

Result:

The player may scratch, stagger, or minor-wound the enemy, but clean heavy damage is suspicious unless targeting weakness.

⸻

10. Dodge and evasion comparison

For dodging, compare defender evasion score to attacker accuracy/pressure score.

Attacker pressure score may use:

* ATK
* SPD
* weapon domain
* ability rank
* enemy size/area effect
* surprise
* terrain

Defender evasion score uses:

* SPD
* Evasion/Athletics/Acrobatics domain
* terrain
* burden
* EP
* injury

Evasion Ratio = Defender Evasion Score / Attacker Pressure Score

Evasion Ratio	Expected Result
below 0.25	dodge nearly impossible
0.25–0.5	dodge unlikely, partial mitigation possible
0.5–0.75	hard dodge, injury likely if failed
0.75–1.25	contested
1.25–1.75	dodge likely
1.75–2.5	clean dodge likely
2.5+	attacker unlikely to hit unless AoE/surprise/counter

Important:

AoE, traps, swarms, and unavoidable effects reduce normal dodge value.

A high-SPD rogue can dodge a sword better than a room-filling explosion.

⸻

11. Block and guard comparison

Blocking uses:

* DEF
* Shield/Weapon/Guard Domain
* equipment quality
* stance
* EP
* injury
* attack type

Block Ratio = Defender Block Score / Attacker Offensive Score

Block Ratio	Expected Result
below 0.25	block fails badly
0.25–0.5	block reduces little; injury/equipment damage likely
0.5–0.75	partial block
0.75–1.25	contested block
1.25–1.75	solid block
1.75–2.5	strong block, minor cost
2.5+	attack stopped unless special property

Blocking can still cost EP.

A successful block may damage shield/weapon.

A failed block may reduce damage but still cause injury.

Blocking magic with a mundane shield may fail unless shield/material/ability supports it.

12. Damage severity categories

AIDRPG should classify damage before exact HP.

Damage categories:

* none
* scratch
* minor
* moderate
* serious
* severe
* critical
* lethal/death-boundary
* overwhelming/special

Damage category is determined by:

* attack ratio
* weapon/spell type
* target body
* armor
* resistances
* vulnerabilities
* location
* narration severity
* player/enemy level
* current HP
* boss rules
* conditions

The category then converts to HP percentage.

⸻

13. HP damage percentage table

Default HP damage as percentage of target max HP:

Damage Severity	HP Damage
none	0%
scratch	1–2%
minor	3–7%
moderate	8–15%
serious	16–30%
severe	31–50%
critical	51–80%
lethal/death-boundary	81–100%
overwhelming	special, usually only with validation

Use this as guidance, not exact rigid dice.

Example:

A wolf bite to a Level 5 human may be moderate or serious.

A wolf bite to a Level 100 armored tank may be scratch or none.

A god spear through the chest may be lethal even at high level if it bypasses defenses and is validated.

⸻

14. Flat damage option

If a stricter mode needs flat damage, use:

Damage = Base Severity % of Max HP

Example:

Target HP 1000.

Moderate damage:

8–15% = 80–150 HP

Serious damage:

16–30% = 160–300 HP

This scales naturally across levels.

For most AI Dungeon play, severity-based HP percentage is easier and more stable than fixed weapon numbers.

15. Weapon damage categories

Weapons should influence severity.

Improvised light weapon

Examples:

* stick
* bottle
* small rock

Usually scratch/minor unless high ATK or vulnerable target.

Light weapon

Examples:

* dagger
* short knife
* small club

Minor/moderate, serious on vital hit or stealth.

Standard weapon

Examples:

* sword
* spear
* axe
* mace
* bow

Moderate/serious against comparable foes.

Heavy weapon

Examples:

* greatsword
* warhammer
* greataxe
* lance

Serious/severe if it lands, higher EP cost.

Fine/masterwork weapon

Improves effective attack and reliability.

Magic weapon

May bypass resistance, add element, or count as special.

Artifact weapon

May shift tier, bypass defenses, or create special effects, but should have conditions/costs.

Weapon quality modifies attack score and possible severity.

It should not override all context.

⸻

16. Armor and defense

Armor reduces damage severity or effective attack.

Armor types:

* cloth
* leather
* reinforced leather
* chain
* scale
* plate
* monster hide
* magical armor
* natural armor
* divine armor
* shield/barrier

Armor effects:

* reduce damage severity
* reduce injury chance
* protect body parts
* increase EP cost
* reduce SPD
* fail against certain damage types
* degrade from hits
* be bypassed by precision, magic, poison, or weak points

Suggested armor severity reduction:

Armor Quality / Relevance	Severity Reduction
irrelevant armor	0
light relevant armor	reduce by 0–1 step
medium relevant armor	reduce by 1 step
heavy relevant armor	reduce by 1–2 steps
excellent/magical armor	reduce by 2+ steps
perfect counter armor	may block or greatly reduce

Example:

A knife slash against plate armor may be scratch/none.

A warhammer against plate may still cause blunt trauma.

Acid may damage armor directly.

Lightning may bypass metal armor or worsen effect depending setting.

⸻

17. Resistances and vulnerabilities in damage

Resistance modifies damage severity.

Resistance Level	Severity Modifier
Vulnerable	increase 1–3 steps
Normal	no change
Resistant	reduce 1 step
Highly resistant	reduce 2–3 steps
Immune	no damage unless bypassed
Absorbs	damage becomes healing/charge
Reflects	redirects effect
Conditional	depends on context

Examples:

Fire against ice creature:

* vulnerability, increase severity

Sword against ghost:

* immune/resistant unless weapon is magical/spirit-compatible

Piercing against slime:

* reduced unless core hit

Blunt against skeleton:

* increased or normal

Poison against construct:

* immune unless corrosive/magical

Resistance should be based on confirmed or strongly supported traits.

Do not invent weaknesses freely.

⸻

18. Injury threshold rules

HP damage and narration can create injuries.

Injury should be considered when:

* damage is moderate or higher
* hit targets specific body part
* attack type supports injury
* HP drops below threshold
* critical hit/strong narration
* body type supports injury
* armor fails or is bypassed
* repeated damage accumulates

Suggested injury chance by severity:

Damage Severity	Injury Result
scratch	no injury, cosmetic mark
minor	minor injury possible
moderate	minor or moderate injury likely
serious	moderate injury likely, severe possible
severe	severe injury likely
critical	severe/critical injury
lethal	death-boundary or catastrophic injury

Injury must fit body type.

A slime gets cohesion/core damage.

A skeleton gets cracked bones.

A construct gets structural damage.

A spirit gets essence disruption.

⸻

19. Injury location

If narration gives a location, use it.

Examples:

* arm
* leg
* shoulder
* ribs
* chest
* head
* eye
* hand
* wing
* tail
* core
* power crystal
* spirit anchor

If no location is given, infer only if safe.

Do not invent crippling injuries from vague damage.

Example:

“The wolf bites you.”

Possible:

* generic bite wound
* limb bite if scene says it lunged at arm/leg

Not automatic:

* severed artery
* broken spine
* infected wound

Unless narration supports it.

21. Critical hits

Critical hits should not be random by default unless a random mode exists.

In freeform AIDRPG, critical effects happen when story supports:

* exposed weak point
* surprise attack
* high LCK
* enemy vulnerability
* strong attack ratio
* precise technique
* ability crit
* enemy careless
* player preparation
* dramatic moment

Critical hit effects may:

* increase damage severity
* add injury
* bypass armor
* trigger condition
* stagger enemy
* break equipment
* discover weakness
* shift morale

Critical hits should not instantly kill bosses unless defeat condition and scale support it.

⸻

22. Grazes and partial hits

Not every hit is full damage.

Partial hit examples:

* blade scratches armor
* arrow grazes shoulder
* fire singes cloak
* claw tears sleeve
* spell clips enemy
* shield absorbs most force
* slime only brushes boot

Partial hits are important for freeform narrative.

They allow AI output to matter without overcommitting.

AIDRPG should use:

* scratch
* minor damage
* item damage
* condition warning
* morale shift
* ability evidence
* no major HP loss

when full damage is not plausible.

⸻

23. EP cost in combat

Physical combat should cost EP.

EP costs depend on:

* action intensity
* weapon weight
* armor burden
* movement
* injury
* fatigue
* race/body
* environment
* ability use
* duration of exchange

Suggested EP cost by action:

Action	EP Cost
quick step / small movement	1–2% max EP
normal attack	2–4%
heavy attack	5–10%
full combo/flurry	8–15%
dodge	2–6%
desperate dodge	6–12%
block	2–8% depending force
grapple	5–15%
sprint/chase burst	8–20%
forced overexertion	HP/injury cost possible
prolonged combat exchange	5–20% per scene beat

At high levels, percentages keep costs meaningful.

A Level 1000 warrior has huge EP, but mythic actions cost large amounts.

⸻

24. MP cost in combat

Magic, supernatural abilities, barriers, summons, and energy attacks cost MP unless specifically passive/free.

Suggested MP cost by action:

Magic Action	MP Cost
cantrip/tiny effect	1–2% max MP
basic spell	3–5%
moderate combat spell	6–10%
strong spell	11–20%
severe spell / wide AoE	21–35%
extreme spell	36–50%
overcharge/mythic spell	50%+ plus risk

Cost modifiers:

* ability rank may reduce cost
* domain rank may reduce waste
* focus item may reduce cost
* casting under pressure may increase cost
* enemy resistance may require more power
* large target count increases cost
* duration increases cost
* range increases cost
* low MP increases failure/backlash risk

⸻

25. Hybrid MP/EP costs

Many abilities use both MP and EP.

Examples:

* Mana Slash
* elemental weapon strike
* spiritual martial art
* dragon breath
* teleport dodge
* shadow step
* magic-enhanced sprint
* barrier block

Hybrid cost example:

Mana Slash Rank 2:
MP 6–10% max MP
EP 3–6% max EP

As ability ranks up:

* cost may drop
* damage/control may rise
* failure risk drops
* range/duration may improve

Hybrid builds must manage both resources.

⸻

26. HP cost abilities

Some abilities cost HP or body integrity.

Examples:

* blood magic
* berserk overexertion
* forbidden spell
* undead sacrifice
* slime mass sacrifice
* construct core overload
* demonic contract backlash
* divine martyr ability

HP-cost abilities require strong validation.

They should clearly show:

* cost
* risk
* benefit
* recovery path
* death-boundary risk

Example:

Blood Spear:

* costs 10% HP
* costs MP
* causes bleeding risk
* powerful piercing effect
* cannot be spammed safely

HP costs should never be hidden.

⸻

27. Overexertion in combat

When resources are too low, player may overexert.

Overexertion examples:

* swing at 0 EP
* cast at 0 MP
* block fatal hit with broken arm
* sprint while exhausted
* force transformation while wounded
* maintain barrier past limit

Possible costs:

* HP damage
* injury worsening
* new condition
* unconsciousness
* ability backlash
* temporary stat penalty
* equipment damage
* resource debt
* race/body instability
* corruption

Overexertion may allow heroic action but should not be free.

Example:

A Level 50 brawler at 0 EP throws one final punch.

Possible accepted outcome:

* punch lands weakly or partially
* HP cost
* collapses after
* arm injury worsens

Not acceptable:

* full-power unlimited combo at no cost.

28. Combat action intensity

AIDRPG should classify action intensity.

Light

Examples:

* jab
* quick cut
* small dodge
* careful step

Low EP/MP cost.

Normal

Examples:

* standard attack
* normal dodge
* shield block
* basic spell

Moderate cost.

Heavy

Examples:

* power swing
* full sprint
* strong spell
* forceful grapple

High cost.

Desperate

Examples:

* last stand
* overcharge
* reckless lunge
* cast while empty

Very high cost and risk.

Mythic

Examples:

* world-cutting slash
* divine barrier
* battlefield-scale spell

Requires high level, ability, resource, and story support.

Intensity helps scale costs and consequences.

29. Enemy attacks against player

Enemy attacks should use the same logic.

When AI says enemy hits player, validate:

* enemy offensive score
* player defense/evasion
* player armor
* player resources
* player body type
* enemy damage type
* enemy tier
* narration severity
* player current state
* operation duplicate

Example:

A Tier 2 goblin stabs Level 100 armored player.

Likely:

* scratch/minor or blocked
* maybe no HP if armor
* no severe injury unless poison/weak point/surprise

Example:

A Tier 8 dragon breathes fire at Level 100 player.

Likely:

* severe/critical unless cover/resistance/barrier
* escape or partial protection possible
* full no-damage requires strong leverage

⸻

30. Player attacks against enemy

When AI says player hits enemy, validate:

* player attack score
* enemy defense
* enemy body type
* enemy resistances
* enemy weak points
* weapon/ability type
* enemy tier
* boss rules
* group rules
* narration severity
* duplicate operation

Example:

Level 5 player slashes adult dragon.

Likely:

* no damage/scratch unless weak point/artifact
* maybe dragon notices
* no dragon death

Example:

Level 300 player with dragon-slaying spear hits wounded dragon’s exposed heart.

Likely:

* serious/severe/critical
* boss phase or defeat possible
* if defeat condition met, victory possible

⸻

31. Combat with unusual bodies

Combat must respect body types.

Slime

Normal cuts may:

* split mass
* cause minor cohesion damage
* fail to injure if no core hit

Fire/drying/purification may:

* cause severe damage
* create condition
* reduce mass/cohesion

Core hit, if confirmed:

* severe/critical

Skeleton

Piercing may be weak.

Blunt may be strong.

No bleeding.

Holy effects may be strong.

Construct

Poison mostly ineffective.

Blunt/armor-piercing/core damage important.

Repair needed after structural damage.

Spirit

Mundane attacks fail unless spirit is manifested or weapon is special.

Holy/spirit/ritual attacks matter.

Fairy

Tiny body means low HP.

Dodging/flight/stealth important.

Physical hits may be severe if they land.

Giant

High HP/DEF.

Hard to move or knock back.

Large target.

Cramped spaces reduce advantage.

Body logic should override generic human injury logic.

⸻

32. Boss damage limits

Bosses should not be erased by one unsupported hit.

Boss damage should respect:

* tier gap
* boss resources
* armor/resistance
* phases
* defeat condition
* story leverage
* weak points
* player resource cost
* ability rank
* narration support

If an AI output says:

“You stab the ancient dragon and it dies instantly.”

Validation should check:

* Is player level/tier high enough?
* Is weapon appropriate?
* Was weak point exposed?
* Was dragon already near death?
* Was anti-dragon leverage present?
* Is this dream/flavor/exaggeration?
* Does boss have remaining phase?

If unsupported:

* soften to wound/stagger/anger
* reject death
* record damage if plausible
* Context correction if needed

Bosses can die quickly only when setup supports it.

Example:

A lich’s body can be destroyed by a strong attack, but final death may require phylactery.

⸻

33. Minion damage limits

Minions should not be too durable.

If player massively outscales minions:

* attacks may defeat multiple
* morale may break
* HP tracking can be abstracted
* group count updates matter
* XP may be low

Example:

Level 500 player attacks Tier 2 goblins.

Likely:

* many defeated or routed
* no meaningful XP
* collateral/social consequences may matter

But if objective is “capture alive” or “protect civilians,” minions can still create challenge.

⸻

34. Group damage and casualties

Group combat should update counts.

Possible outcomes:

* one defeated
* several wounded
* several killed
* group scattered
* group routed
* leader killed
* morale broken
* no casualties but formation disrupted
* group advances

Casualty validation depends on:

* player attack scale
* area effect
* enemy density
* enemy tier
* player level
* weapon/ability type
* environment
* group morale
* boss/minion protection

Example:

“Your fireball engulfs the front rank of goblins.”

Possible:

* 3–8 goblins killed/wounded depending group size, fireball rank, tier
* not necessarily all 30 unless spell scale supports it

Group casualty event must be deduped.

⸻

35. AoE damage

Area effects use different scaling.

AoE factors:

* area size
* intensity
* target density
* enemy resistance
* cover
* friendly fire
* terrain
* resource cost
* ability rank
* casting time

AoE should cost more MP/EP.

AoE is strong against:

* swarms
* groups
* weak minions
* clustered enemies

AoE is weaker against:

* single high-DEF boss
* spread enemies
* resistant enemies
* enemies behind cover
* friendly-fire-risk scenes

AoE should not automatically distinguish enemies from allies unless ability supports precision.

⸻

36. Grappling and restraint

Grappling uses:

* ATK
* DEF
* SPD
* size
* body type
* Brawling/Wrestling Domain
* limbs/tentacles
* slippery body
* equipment
* injury
* EP

Grapple comparison:

Grapple Score = ATK + DEF partial + Grapple Domain + Size Modifier

Size matters.

A tiny fairy grappling a giant normally fails unless magic/leverage.

A slime may grapple through engulfing instead of limbs.

A ghost may be ungrappleable unless manifested or bound.

Grapple results:

* restrained
* pinned
* shoved
* thrown
* disarmed
* partially held
* escaped
* counter-grappled

Grappling costs EP each beat.

⸻

37. Disarming and equipment attacks

Players may target weapons, shields, armor, or items.

Disarm depends on:

* SPD
* ATK
* weapon domain
* enemy grip/DEF
* surprise
* size
* item type
* technique
* relative strength
* injury

Equipment damage depends on:

* attack type
* item condition
* material
* enchantment
* hit severity
* weapon hardness
* ability effect

Examples:

* acid slime damages boots/armor
* warhammer dents shield
* sword may fail against iron golem
* fire burns wooden shield
* magic weapon can cut cursed chain

InventoryReducer owns item condition changes.

Combat proposes them.

⸻

38. Called shots and weak points

Called shots are attacks targeting specific weak points.

Examples:

* eye
* throat
* exposed joint
* core
* wing
* tendon
* magic crystal
* phylactery
* open wound
* broken armor seam

Called shots are harder but can increase severity.

Requirements:

* target visible/known
* player can reach it
* relevant accuracy/SPD/domain
* enemy not fully guarding it
* attack type appropriate
* weak point confirmed or plausibly inferred

If called shot fails:

* miss
* glancing hit
* counterattack risk
* wasted EP/MP
* worse position

Do not allow called shots to invent weak points.

Example:

A slime core must be confirmed or strongly suspected before targeting it as fact.

⸻

39. Stealth attacks and ambushes

Ambush attacks can increase damage and condition chance.

Ambush depends on:

* Stealth Domain
* SPD
* terrain
* enemy senses
* light/noise
* enemy alertness
* equipment burden
* party noise
* preparation

Benefits of successful ambush:

* first strike
* increased damage
* armor bypass
* morale shock
* surprise condition
* enemy unable to dodge/block fully

Ambush does not automatically kill bosses.

Bosses may have:

* danger sense
* guards
* wards
* thick armor
* regeneration
* multiple phases

Assassination of major targets requires strong setup.

⸻

40. Counterattacks

Counterattacks occur when:

* defender reads attack
* attacker overextends
* defender has high SPD/domain
* attack fails badly
* enemy is skilled
* boss has counter trait
* player uses reckless action

Counterattack may cause:

* HP damage
* injury
* disarm
* knockdown
* resource loss
* condition
* positional disadvantage

Counterattacks should be likely against skilled enemies if the player repeatedly uses predictable attacks.

This helps prevent spam.

⸻

41. Combat morale and surrender

Combat does not always end in death.

Possible resolutions:

* enemy flees
* enemy surrenders
* enemy is captured
* player retreats
* negotiation begins
* enemy routs
* boss withdraws
* enemy feigns surrender
* combat pauses due to larger threat

Morale depends on:

* enemy intelligence
* group losses
* leader status
* fear
* loyalty
* fanaticism
* undead/mindless state
* player reputation
* intimidation
* overwhelming power
* escape route

AIDRPG should avoid requiring every fight to end with total slaughter.

⸻

42. Nonlethal combat

Players may choose nonlethal action.

Nonlethal options:

* shove
* grapple
* disarm
* knock out
* pin
* bind
* intimidate
* exhaust
* trip
* stun
* disable weapon
* restrain with magic

Nonlethal validation depends on:

* target body type
* relative power
* ability precision
* weapon choice
* enemy fragility
* player control
* situation

Nonlethal is harder when:

* using lethal weapons
* enemy is fragile
* player overpowers target massively
* target has unusual body
* chaotic AoE
* combat is frantic

Example:

A Level 500 player punching a Tier 1 human “nonlethally” requires control; otherwise accidental severe injury is possible.

⸻

43. Retreat and escape

Retreat is a valid combat result.

Escape depends on:

* SPD
* EP
* terrain
* route/exits
* enemy speed
* enemy ranged attacks
* enemy intelligence
* party speed
* carrying burden
* injuries
* weather
* stealth
* distraction
* mounts/vehicles

Retreat outcomes:

* clean escape
* partial escape with injury
* split party
* dropped item
* enemy pursues
* delayed escape
* escape into worse hazard
* captured

AIDRPG should support “survived by escaping” XP if threat was meaningful.

Escape should not be treated as failure unless objective required victory.

⸻

44. Defense without attacking

Players may defend, observe, or stall.

Defensive actions can:

* reduce damage
* recover tiny EP through controlled breathing if safe
* identify enemy pattern
* protect ally
* buy time
* force enemy to reveal ability
* improve next action
* reduce morale loss

Examples:

“I keep my shield up and watch his footwork.”

Potential effects:

* block/guard
* INT/Combat Domain observation
* enemy pattern clue
* EP cost
* lower damage risk

Defensive play should be rewarded when meaningful.

⸻

45. Combat observation and analysis

Players may analyze enemies during combat.

Relevant stats:

* INT
* SPD if reading movement
* domain knowledge
* senses
* prior evidence
* time available
* danger level

Possible results:

* identify weakness
* identify ability pattern
* estimate tier
* reveal fake opening
* discover armor gap
* learn enemy is baiting
* confirm immunity/resistance
* update threat estimate
* create Investigation/Monster Lore XP

Analysis should not reveal everything instantly unless player has overwhelming relevant knowledge or ability.

* ability rank
* target
* range
* duration
* enemy resistance
* scene pressure
* body/equipment requirements
* cooldown/maintenance

Stable abilities are more reliable.

Using an ability can create:

* damage
* defense
* movement
* condition
* buff

* terrain change
* clue
* cost discovery
* rank evidence
* backlash

AbilityReducer owns ability truth.

47. Combat and passives

Passives may trigger during combat.

Examples:

* scales reduce damage
* danger sense warns of ambush
* regeneration heals over time
* fire resistance reduces burn
* Quick Learner improves evidence gain
* undead body ignores poison
* slime body resists piercing
* berserker rage activates under low HP

Passive trigger rules:

* passive must exist
* trigger condition must be met
* cost/upkeep checked if any
* cooldown checked if any
* effect scoped properly
* not duplicate-triggered from same event

Passives must have limits.

Fire resistance is not fire immunity unless defined.

Regeneration may fail under fire/holy/acid/sealing depending trait.

49. Combat and summons

High-level combat should include collateral risk.

* evidence damaged

* domain damage
* route blocked

A Level 1000 player can annihilate enemies, but may destroy the city if careless.

This keeps high-level combat meaningful.

52. Combat and death boundary

Death must be careful.

Player death or unconsciousness should require strong evidence.

Death-boundary factors:

* HP at or below 0
* lethal injury
* body type
* death mode settings
* source validity
* enemy tier
* player protections
* passives
* LCK/fate abilities
* story context
* safety settings

Recommended default:

AIDRPG should use a death-boundary state before irreversible death unless lethal mode is enabled.

Examples:

* unconscious
* dying
* inert
* dispersed
* core cracked
* soul slipping
* shutdown
* banished
* captured
* near death

Permanent death should require:

* validated lethal event
* no protective passive/setting
* no body-specific survival rule
* death mode allows it
* audit records it

Enemy death also needs validation, especially bosses and unusual bodies.

⸻

53. Combat result categories

Combat events should resolve into categories:

No effect

Action fails or cannot affect target.

Minor effect

Small damage, pressure, or information.

Partial success

Some goal achieved with cost or limitation.

Success

Intended result works.

Strong success

Better-than-normal result due to stats/leverage.

Critical success

Major result, usually with setup/weakness/high ratio.

Failure

Action fails.

Soft failure

Action partially fails but teaches or changes something.

Severe failure

Action fails with injury/cost.

Impossible / hard-denied

Action cannot happen under current canon.

Use these categories in debug.

55. Damage calculation workflow

When damage is proposed, follow this workflow:

1. Classify source.
2. Identify attacker.
3. Identify target.
4. Identify attack type.
5. Identify target body type.
6. Check if attacker/target are present.
7. Check weapon/ability/item access.
8. Calculate or estimate offensive score.
9. Calculate or estimate defensive/evasion/block score.
10. Apply tier/matchup context.
11. Apply armor/resistance/vulnerability.
12. Apply environment.
13. Apply resource state.
14. Apply narration severity.
15. Determine damage category.
16. Convert to HP damage range.
17. Determine injury/condition possibility.
18. Check death-boundary rules.
19. Check operation ledger.
20. Commit through BodyReducer or enemy/Scale owner.
21. Mark dirty targets.
22. Audit result.

This workflow prevents random damage from slipping into state.

* reputation change

ProgressionReducer decides XP.

57. Loot after combat

Loot should not automatically enter inventory.

Combat may create available loot.

Example:

“The goblin drops a rusty dagger.”

This creates:

* scene item / available loot

Not automatically:

* inventory item

Player must take it, or output must confirm acquisition.

Exceptions:

* AI explicitly says player picks it up
* command adds it
* automatic loot setting enabled

Loot must respect:

* item condition
* enemy equipment
* ownership/custody
* legality
* carrying capacity if used
* duplicate prevention

Combat Context should be immediate and compact.

61. Combat safety rules

61.1 Player input is attempt, not automatic success

“I stab the dragon” does not mean dragon is damaged unless output or simulation confirms it.

61.2 AI output is validated, not blindly trusted

AI can exaggerate; validator checks scale.

61.3 Damage must fit body type

No bleeding skeletons unless setting supports it.

61.4 Resources matter

EP/MP costs should apply to demanding actions.

61.5 Overexertion has consequences

No full-power action at 0 resources for free.

61.6 Bosses need defeat validation

No unsupported instant boss kills.

61.7 Minions should not be overprotected

Weak enemies can be defeated quickly if outscaled.

61.8 Groups need counts

Do not lose track of remaining enemies.

61.9 Absent allies cannot act

Companion presence must be checked.

61.10 Combat rewards go through ProgressionReducer

Combat does not directly award XP.

61.11 Loot is not inventory until acquired

Dropped items are not automatically owned.

61.12 Cards do not create combat truth

Enemy cards and ability cards mirror state.

61.13 Duplicate output cannot apply damage twice

OperationLedger dedupes HP damage, resource cost, injuries, casualties, and rewards.

61.14 Social/legal combat consequences need witnesses or evidence

Killing a guard privately is different from killing one publicly.

61.15 Death should use death-boundary safeguards

Permanent death requires strong validation and settings support.

63. Final Chunk 24 doctrine

Combat in AIDRPG is freeform, but not consequence-free.

Player input creates attempts.

AI output proposes consequences.

CanonValidator decides what is real.

Combat math guides plausibility.

ATK, DEF, SPD, INT, LCK, domains, abilities, equipment, race, injuries, resources, terrain, and enemy tier all matter.

Damage should be classified by severity before HP is changed.

Armor, resistance, vulnerability, body type, and boss rules modify damage.

EP limits physical action.

MP limits magic.

HP costs and overexertion are possible but dangerous.

Injuries and conditions must fit the body.

Groups need counts.

Bosses need defeat conditions.

Companions must be present to help.

Loot must be acquired before entering inventory.

XP must route through ProgressionReducer.

Story Cards do not create combat truth.

Duplicate outputs cannot apply damage twice.

The goal is not to turn AI Dungeon into rigid turn-based combat.

The goal is to make freeform combat believable, scalable, inspectable, and safe from impossible victories, absurd defeats, duplicate damage, and ignored resources.
