AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 30: Investigation, Cases, Clues, Evidence, Rumors, Witness Knowledge, Truth Validation, and Solved-Case Archive

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 30 defines how AIDRPG handles investigation gameplay.

This includes:

* cases
* mysteries
* clues
* evidence
* witness knowledge
* rumors
* claims
* theories
* contradictions
* suspect tracking
* proof standards
* solved-case validation
* false leads
* investigation quests
* case archives
* actor/place/card updates from confirmed facts

Earlier chunks defined:

* stats and resources
* progression
* enemies
* combat
* abilities
* absorption
* crafting
* economy
* quests and contracts

Chunk 30 answers:

How does the system know what is true?

How does it separate a rumor from a fact?

How does it handle clues without instantly solving everything?

How does INT help investigation without creating hidden truth from nothing?

How does witness knowledge work?

How does evidence become confirmed?

How does a case become solved?

How does the system prevent AI Dungeon from randomly declaring the wrong culprit?

How does the system update actor/place/story cards only from confirmed facts?

How does it archive solved cases without bloating active context?

The central rule is:

Investigation is evidence-driven truth management.

AIDRPG should never treat suspicion, rumor, theory, hallucinated narration, or player guesses as confirmed truth unless validated by evidence.

⸻

2. Core philosophy

AI Dungeon often creates mystery, suspicion, and partial information naturally.

The player may encounter:

* a murdered merchant
* a missing child
* a stolen relic
* a suspicious noble
* a cursed village
* a lying witness
* strange tracks
* a locked room
* a missing shipment
* a corrupted slime outbreak
* a ghost repeating the same phrase
* a rumor about a dragon cult
* a fake bounty
* a forged contract
* a poisoning
* a noble’s secret
* a hidden tunnel
* a traitor in the camp
* an unexplained disease
* a strange magical residue

AIDRPG must support this kind of play.

But it must prevent:

* high INT instantly solving mysteries
* rumors becoming facts
* NPC lies being treated as canon
* player guesses becoming truth
* AI Dungeon randomly naming a culprit without support
* witness statements updating actor cards as confirmed fact
* duplicate clues
* clue spam
* solved cases without proof
* case rewards triggering twice
* evidence being created from Story Card text
* old disproven facts staying active
* false leads being treated as mistakes rather than intentional uncertainty

Investigation should feel flexible and story-rich, but it needs a strong truth model.

4. Investigation types

AIDRPG should classify investigation content.

Case

A structured mystery or problem requiring evidence.

Example:

“Who killed the merchant?”

Clue

A meaningful observation, object, statement, trace, or fact that may help resolve a case.

Example:

“Blue wax was found on the victim’s sleeve.”

Evidence

A clue strong enough to support a claim.

Example:

“The blue wax matches the seal used by House Veyr.”

Proof

Evidence strong enough to establish a conclusion for a case, authority, or quest.

Example:

“House Veyr’s servant was seen entering the room, and the dagger with Veyr crest has the victim’s blood.”

Rumor

Unverified information circulating through people.

Example:

“They say the old miller deals with wolves.”

Witness statement

Something an actor says they saw, heard, believe, suspect, or claim.

Example:

“The stable boy says he saw Mara near the alley after sunset.”

Theory

A possible explanation not yet proven.

Example:

“Mara may have had access to the murder weapon.”

Contradiction

Two pieces of information conflict.

Example:

“Mara says she was at the inn, but the innkeeper says she never arrived.”

Lead

A direction for further investigation.

Example:

“Check the blue wax supplier.”

False lead

A clue or claim that seems important but later proves misleading.

Example:

“The bloodied scarf belongs to the victim’s brother, but he used it earlier to stop a nosebleed.”

Confirmed fact

A truth validated strongly enough to update canon memory.

Example:

“The victim died from poison, not the knife wound.”

5. Truth status model

Every investigation fact should have a truth status.

Truth statuses:

Status	Meaning
Unknown	no reliable information
Rumored	people say it, not verified
Claimed	a specific source says it
Suspected	plausible but unproven
Observed	directly seen by player or reliable narration
Supported	evidence supports it
Strongly Supported	multiple evidence points support it
Confirmed	accepted as canon truth
Disputed	credible conflicting evidence exists
Contradicted	evidence shows it is likely false
False	confirmed false
Forged/Fabricated	evidence or claim was manufactured
Ambiguous	truth cannot currently be resolved
Retconned/Invalid	previous record rejected by validation

This is one of the most important safeguards in the whole system.

A statement can be useful without being confirmed.

⸻

6. Source reliability model

Every clue or claim should know where it came from.

Source reliability categories:

Source Type	Reliability
direct player observation	high, but interpretation can still be wrong
validated AI narration	high if not contradicted
physical evidence	high if authentic
magical evidence	high if spell/source reliable
witness statement	variable
expert testimony	variable to high
rumor	low
enemy claim	low to variable
drunk witness	low to variable
biased actor	variable
forged document	false once proven
dream/vision	special, uncertain unless setting validates
Story Card text	not evidence by itself
Context text	not evidence by itself
debug/command output	not evidence unless command intentionally mutates state

Reliability should be tracked separately from truth.

A reliable witness can be wrong.

An unreliable source can accidentally be correct.

7. Case lifecycle

Cases should follow a lifecycle.

7.1 Unopened

A mystery exists but is not tracked.

7.2 Rumored

The player hears about a possible issue.

7.3 Open

A case is created.

7.4 Active

Player is investigating.

7.5 In Progress

Clues/evidence have been found.

7.6 Blocked

Progress requires missing clue, access, witness, item, authority, or location.

7.7 Theory Formed

A plausible explanation exists but is not proven.

7.8 Evidence Sufficient

Enough evidence may support a solution.

7.9 Ready to Present

The player can present findings to authority/giver/target.

7.10 Solved

The truth is validated internally.

7.11 Resolved

The case result has been acted on publicly or narratively.

7.12 Archived

The case is no longer active.

7.13 Reopened

New evidence challenges the old conclusion.

Status examples:

* Open
* Active
* Blocked
* Ready to Present
* Solved
* Resolved
* Archived
* Reopened
* Failed
* Abandoned
* Cold

10. Core question and subquestions

Every case should have a core question.

Examples:

* Who killed the merchant?
* Where is the missing child?
* What caused the plague?
* Who sabotaged the wall?
* Is Frostmaw actually attacking the mill?
* Where did the stolen ring go?
* Why are the slimes becoming corrupted?
* Is the contract forged?
* Who opened the gate?
* What is haunting the tower?

Subquestions break the mystery into trackable parts.

Example:

Case: Merchant’s Murder

Core question:

Who killed Dalen?

Subquestions:

What was the true cause of death?
Who had access to the room?
Who benefits from his death?
Where did the blue wax come from?
Why was the knife wound staged?

Subquestions produce leads and prevent one-clue solutions.

11. Clue definition

A clue is any meaningful information that may help answer a case question.

Clues can be:

* physical object
* trace
* wound
* footprint
* smell
* sound
* document
* witness statement
* magical residue
* location detail
* behavior pattern
* contradiction
* missing item
* timing fact
* item condition
* body condition
* rumor
* map mark
* ledger entry
* relationship fact
* environmental anomaly

A clue is not automatically evidence.

A clue becomes evidence when its relevance is established.

Example:

A blue thread found at a scene is a clue.

It becomes evidence if linked to a suspect’s cloak, uniform, or location.

13. Evidence strength

Evidence should have strength.

Strength	Meaning
none	not useful yet
weak	suggests possibility
moderate	supports a claim
strong	strongly supports a claim
decisive	enough to prove if authentic
corroborating	supports other evidence
contradictory	undermines a claim
exculpatory	clears or weakens suspicion
fabricated	fake evidence
contaminated	unreliable due to mishandling
inadmissible	may be true but not accepted by authority

Evidence strength depends on:

* source reliability
* directness
* authenticity
* corroboration
* chain of custody
* interpretation
* whether alternate explanations remain

A bloody dagger can be strong evidence.

But if the wound was postmortem, it may be misleading.

17. Witness reliability factors

Witness reliability depends on:

* firsthand or hearsay
* visibility
* distance
* time elapsed
* fear
* intoxication
* bias
* relationship to suspect
* motive to lie
* memory quality
* magical influence
* injury

* expertise
* pressure/coercion
* consistency
* corroboration

18. Lies and deception

Witnesses may lie.

Possible lie types:

* direct lie
* omission
* mistaken identity
* false alibi
* exaggeration
* coerced lie
* planted rumor
* misdirection
* self-protection
* protecting someone else
* fear-based silence
* magical compulsion
* memory alteration
* social pressure

Investigation should allow lies without breaking canon.

A lie is still a real statement.

Example:

Confirmed truth:

Mara was not at the alley.

Witness statement remains:

Stable boy claimed Mara was at the alley.

Then mark:

Statement contradicted / likely false.

This preserves continuity.

27. Deductions

A deduction is an inference drawn from clues.

Deduction types:

* obvious deduction
* skill/stat-supported deduction
* expert deduction
* magical deduction
* speculative deduction
* false deduction
* partial deduction

INT and Investigation Domain help with deductions.

But they should not create facts from nothing.

Example:

Evidence:

* victim’s lips are blue
* cup smells bitter
* knife wound has little blood

Possible deduction:

* poison likely killed victim before stabbing

This is supported.

Invalid deduction:

* “Lord Veyr did it because I have high INT.”

High INT may suggest questions, not confirm culprit.

* notice inconsistencies

* remember relevant details
* understand mechanisms

* reduce false assumptions

Investigation Domain helps:

* search efficiently
* preserve evidence
* interview witnesses
* read crime scenes
* identify contradictions

* build proof chain

High INT can generate leads.

31. Clue existence

AIDRPG should distinguish between:

* clue exists and is found
* clue exists but missed
* clue does not exist
* clue is hidden behind blocker
* clue was destroyed
* clue was moved
* clue is false/planted
* clue is irrelevant

AI Dungeon may improvise clues.

The system may accept them if they fit the case and do not contradict protected truth.

But clues should not magically solve everything without a chain.

33. Evidence contamination

Evidence can become weaker if mishandled.

Contamination causes:

* touching blood with bare hands
* exposing potion to air
* mixing samples
* breaking seal
* letting suspect handle proof
* storing magical residue poorly
* fire/water damage
* corpse decay
* crowd trampling tracks
* spell interference

Effects:

* evidence strength reduced
* authority disputes proof
* analysis harder
* false result possible
* case branch changes

High Investigation Domain can prevent contamination.

* pressured
* coerced
* false confession
* magical compulsion
* partial confession
* confession to lesser crime

* dream symbolism
* cursed interference

Magic can provide strong evidence, but not always absolute truth.

⸻

39. Speak with dead / spirit testimony

If the setting allows asking the dead:

Consider:

* corpse condition
* soul availability
* memory limits
* willingness
* trauma
* language
* time since death
* killer disguise
* magical interference
* whether the dead saw the killer
* whether the dead is truthful
* cost/ritual
* legal acceptance

A victim may not know who killed them.

Example:

Victim poisoned from behind.

Spirit may remember taste, fear, a voice, or pain, but not the culprit’s face.

This prevents magic from trivializing every case.

⸻

40. Truth spells

Truth spells should define scope.

Questions:

* Does it detect intentional lies only?
* Does it detect false beliefs?
* Can it be resisted?
* Does silence avoid it?
* Does half-truth bypass it?
* Does memory alteration bypass it?
* Is it legal?
* Does it require consent?
* Does it affect fae/demons/undead?
* Does it produce exact truth or confidence?

A truthful statement can still mislead.

Example:

“I did not kill him.”

Could be true if:

* they hired someone else
* poison killed him before they stabbed him
* they think someone else did
* definition of kill differs magically

Truth spells are useful but not omnipotent.

⸻

41. False leads

False leads are valid gameplay.

False lead types:

* mistaken witness
* planted evidence
* coincidental clue
* outdated rumor
* red herring
* framed suspect
* magical disguise
* false confession
* forged document
* contaminated evidence
* enemy misdirection

False leads should eventually be resolvable.

When disproven:

* mark as false lead
* keep record
* do not delete blindly
* update suspect/evidence status
* maybe grant XP for resolving

Example:

The bloodied scarf seems to implicate the brother.

Later proof shows it was from helping the victim earlier.

Update:

* scarf clue remains
* implication contradicted
* brother suspicion reduced

⸻

42. Forgery and fabricated evidence

Evidence can be fake.

Fabrication signs:

* inconsistent material
* wrong ink age
* incorrect seal
* impossible timeline
* witness contradiction
* magical residue mismatch
* suspicious convenience
* tamper marks
* handwriting mismatch
* too-perfect placement

Forgery detection uses:

* INT
* Investigation Domain
* relevant craft/domain
* magic detection
* expert help
* comparison sample

Fake evidence can become evidence of the framer.

Example:

A forged letter implicating Mara becomes evidence someone tried to frame Mara.

⸻

43. Case proof standards

Different situations require different proof standards.

Context	Proof Needed
personal certainty	moderate evidence may be enough
quest giver satisfaction	depends on giver
bounty payout	proof item/witness/authority
court/legal	strong chain of evidence
noble politics	public or undeniable proof
divine trial	oath, truth magic, ritual proof
faction accusation	faction-specific evidence
personal confrontation	enough to pressure suspect
internal case solved	sufficient validated truth chain

AIDRPG should not require court-level proof for every minor mystery.

But serious consequences require stronger proof.

⸻

44. Solving a case

A case is solved when:

* core question is answered
* answer is supported by enough evidence
* major contradictions are resolved or explained
* false leads are handled
* culprit/cause/location/item is identified
* proof standard for case context is met
* InvestigationReducer marks solution validated
* operation ledger records solve event

A case may be internally solved before publicly resolved.

Example:

Player knows Lord Veyr did it.

But cannot prove it publicly yet.

Status:

* Solution Status: internally solved / public proof insufficient

This distinction is important.

⸻

45. Case resolution

Resolution is what happens after solving.

Possible resolutions:

* culprit arrested
* culprit killed
* culprit escapes
* victim rescued
* missing item recovered
* curse lifted
* case reported
* authority accepts proof
* faction suppresses truth
* player keeps secret
* false accused cleared
* public trial begins
* reward paid
* new quest opens
* case remains unsolved publicly

A solved case is not always resolved.

Quest reward may require resolution/turn-in.

⸻

47. Reopening cases

A case can reopen if:

* new evidence appears
* solution contradicted
* culprit returns
* false confession revealed
* body found
* witness recants
* magical proof changes
* archive was incomplete
* political cover-up exposed
* new victim appears
* quest sequel begins

Reopening should not erase the old archive.

Create:

* reopened status
* linked new case
* or add reopened branch

Example:

“Merchant’s Murder Reopened: Veyr’s True Patron”

48. Investigation XP

Investigation can reward progression.

Rewards may include:

* Investigation Domain XP
* INT XP
* Social Domain XP
* Magic Domain XP if spells used
* Level XP for major breakthroughs
* quest XP if linked
* reputation rewards

XP-worthy events:

* finding meaningful clue
* resolving contradiction
* identifying false evidence
* protecting witness
* proving suspect innocent
* identifying culprit
* solving case
* presenting proof successfully
* exposing conspiracy
* preventing wrongful punishment

Anti-farm applies.

Repeatedly asking the same question should not farm XP.

⸻

49. Investigation anti-farm

Investigation anti-farm blocks:

* repeatedly searching same room with no new method
* repeatedly questioning same witness with same question
* inventing endless clues
* harvesting XP from fake rumors
* repeatedly accusing people randomly
* using high INT to generate XP without evidence
* reprocessing same clue
* duplicate “case solved” output
* repeated clue collection from same source
* cycling false leads intentionally

Meaningful new method can bypass decay.

Example:

Search room again with new UV-like magic after finding blood clue.

Valid.

Search room again the same way five times.

Decay/no new XP.

⸻

50. Case and card update rules

Investigation can update actor/place/item cards, but only from confirmed or strongly supported facts.

Do update cards for:

* confirmed actor guilt
* confirmed actor innocence if important
* known faction membership
* confirmed location secret
* confirmed building hidden room
* confirmed item origin
* confirmed curse source
* solved case result
* major witness relationship
* major false accusation outcome

Do not update cards for:

* rumors
* suspicions
* untested theories
* weak clues
* claims from unreliable witness
* player guesses
* case leads
* temporary suspicion
* false leads unless relevant

Example:

Rumor:

“Lord Veyr is a murderer.”

Do not update Lord Veyr card as murderer.

Maybe update case record:

Rumor: Lord Veyr may be involved.

Confirmed:

Evidence proves Lord Veyr ordered the murder.

Update actor card if important.

55. Investigation Context behavior

Context should include investigation information only when relevant.

Good Context:

You are investigating Dalen’s murder. Confirmed: the knife wound was staged. Suspected: poison may be true cause. Current lead: identify blue wax source.

Good Context:

Mara is only a suspect, not confirmed guilty. Her alibi is disputed but not disproven.

Good Context:

The stable boy claims he saw Mara near the alley, but this statement is unverified and may be unreliable.

Inject every rumor, every witness statement, every old clue, and every archived case.

* confirmed facts
* current lead

59. Investigation validation outcomes

Accept

A clue, evidence, or fact is supported and added.

Example:

Player collects blue wax sample from victim’s sleeve.

Reject

Claim is unsupported.

Example:

AI says suspect is guilty without evidence.

Pending

Information may be true but needs verification.

Example:

Rumor says the miller feeds wolves.

Soft_fail

Action gives partial clue or complication.

Example:

Player searches room but contaminates footprint while finding torn cloth.

Hard_deny

Invalid/non-canon.

Examples:

* Story Card creates evidence
* debug text solves case
* high INT creates culprit
* duplicate clue collected twice
* dream accusation changes real actor card
* random AI “case solved” contradicts state

62. Investigation safeguards

62.1 Rumors are not facts

Rumors create leads, not truth.

62.2 Claims are not facts

Witnesses can lie, misremember, or be wrong.

62.3 Theories are not facts

A theory can guide play without rewriting canon.

62.4 High INT does not create hidden truth

It improves analysis and leads.

62.5 Evidence must have a source

No source, no evidence.

62.6 Physical evidence needs custody if important

Proof items can be lost, stolen, damaged, or contaminated.

62.7 Clues must be deduped

Repeated AI output should not create duplicate clues.

62.8 Evidence strength matters

Weak clues should not prove major accusations.

62.9 Contradictions create leads, not instant answers

Conflict must be resolved.

62.10 False leads are valid state

Do not delete them; mark them.

62.11 Magical investigation has limits

Truth magic, divination, and spirit testimony can fail or be resisted.

62.12 Accusations require proof

Public accusations can backfire.

62.13 Confessions require reliability checks

False or coerced confessions are possible.

62.14 Case solved and case resolved are different

Knowing truth is not the same as public/legal resolution.

62.15 Actor/place cards update only from confirmed facts

Do not pollute long-term memory with suspicion.

62.16 Story Cards do not create evidence

Cards mirror state only.

62.17 Context should not spoil hidden truth

Only inject known facts, claims, and leads.

62.18 Duplicate solved-case rewards are blocked

Quest/progression/economy rewards pay once.

62.19 Archives preserve history

Old case outcomes should not vanish.

62.20 Every case update should be auditable

The player should understand what is known, suspected, disproven, or confirmed.

⸻

63. Final Chunk 30 doctrine

Investigation is truth management.

Cases track mysteries.

Clues are meaningful information.

Evidence is a clue that supports a claim.

Proof is evidence strong enough to establish a conclusion.

Rumors are not facts.

Witness statements are claims.

Theories are not canon truth.

Contradictions are leads.

False leads are valid and should be preserved.

High INT and Investigation Domain help the player notice, connect, and interpret information, but they do not create hidden facts from nothing.

Magic can help investigate, but it has cost, scope, resistance, and failure modes.

A case can be internally solved before it is publicly resolved.

Solved cases should archive cleanly.

Actor, place, item, and story cards should update only from confirmed or strongly supported facts.

Quest rewards, reputation changes, arrests, and public outcomes require appropriate proof and validation.

The goal is to make AI Dungeon mysteries playable without letting the AI scramble truth, turn rumors into canon, duplicate evidence, falsely solve cases, or permanently corrupt memory with unsupported suspicion.
