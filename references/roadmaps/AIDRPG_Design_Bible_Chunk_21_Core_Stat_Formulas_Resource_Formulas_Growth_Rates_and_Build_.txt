AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 21: Core Stat Formulas, Resource Formulas, Growth Rates, and Build Archetypes

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 21 defines how AIDRPG turns level, race, class, training, abilities, equipment, injuries, and conditions into usable player numbers.

Chunk 19 defined the Level 1–1000 scale.

Chunk 20 defined race/species modifiers.

Chunk 21 answers:

What does ATK mean?

What does DEF mean?

What does SPD mean?

What does INT mean?

What does LCK mean?

How are HP, MP, and EP calculated?

How do stats grow?

How do race and class affect stats?

How do injuries and fatigue reduce effective stats?

How does the system estimate stats for freeform AI Dungeon gameplay?

What should a Level 50 warrior look like?

What should a Level 500 mage look like?

What should a Level 1000 specialized build look like?

The central rule is:

Stats should guide plausibility, not replace narrative validation.

AIDRPG should use stats to keep outcomes believable, but the story still matters.

A high ATK does not guarantee a hit.

A high DEF does not ignore poison.

A high SPD does not bypass locked doors.

A high INT does not solve a case without evidence.

A high LCK does not rewrite canon.

Stats influence what is plausible, likely, costly, risky, or impossible.

⸻

2. Core stat philosophy

AIDRPG uses five core stats:

* ATK
* DEF
* SPD
* INT
* LCK

These stats are broad.

They are not meant to replace every possible skill.

Instead, they work with:

* domains
* abilities
* equipment
* race/species
* body state
* conditions
* terrain
* enemy traits
* story leverage
* validation evidence

The same stat can matter differently in different scenes.

Example:

ATK helps with sword attacks, punching, breaking objects, grappling, and some forceful abilities.

DEF helps with resisting damage, enduring pain, holding ground, surviving weather, resisting physical strain, and maintaining stability.

SPD helps with dodging, running, quick attacks, stealth movement, initiative, reaction, and chase scenes.

INT helps with magic, analysis, crafting, investigation, tactics, memory, learning, and complex ability control.

LCK helps with unstable outcomes, rare discoveries, avoiding disaster, loot fortune, gamble-like actions, and chaotic ability behavior.

Stats should never be the only deciding factor.

They are the player’s baseline capability.

⸻

3. Stat definitions

ATK

ATK represents forceful offensive capability.

It includes:

* physical striking power
* weapon force
* grappling force
* destructive output
* ability to push through resistance
* offensive martial pressure
* ability to damage barriers
* raw combat aggression
* some natural weapon strength

ATK affects:

* melee damage
* ranged physical damage if strength-based
* unarmed damage
* breaking objects
* overpowering enemies
* weapon clashes
* monster natural attacks
* certain physical skills
* some intimidation when physical power is obvious

ATK does not automatically mean:

* accuracy
* technique
* magical power
* social dominance
* instant kill

Domains and abilities refine ATK.

Example:

A high ATK character with no Sword Domain may swing hard but clumsily.

A lower ATK duelist with high Sword Domain may land cleaner hits.

⸻

DEF

DEF represents durability, resistance, stability, and endurance under pressure.

It includes:

* physical toughness
* damage resistance
* pain tolerance
* body stability
* ability to block
* armor use
* endurance under impact
* resistance to harsh environments
* ability to maintain posture
* ability to survive heavy strain

DEF affects:

* HP scaling
* damage reduction
* injury resistance
* resistance to knockback
* shield blocking
* armor effectiveness
* fatigue resistance
* environmental endurance
* recovery stability
* ability to survive poison/disease indirectly if body supports it

DEF does not automatically mean:

* poison immunity
* curse immunity
* emotional resilience
* perfect armor
* never being injured

A low DEF character can still avoid damage with SPD.

A high DEF character can still be bypassed by poison, soul damage, social traps, sealing, or status effects.

⸻

SPD

SPD represents speed, reaction, mobility, and timing.

It includes:

* movement speed
* reaction time
* dodge ability
* quick attacks
* chase ability
* initiative
* agility
* stealth movement
* balance
* repositioning
* escape ability

SPD affects:

* dodging attacks
* acting before enemies
* running away
* chasing targets
* avoiding traps
* stealth movement
* quick weapon use
* fast casting gestures
* acrobatics
* travel pace in short bursts

SPD does not automatically mean:

* long-distance endurance
* teleportation
* perfect stealth
* guaranteed escape
* precision technique

EP, terrain, injuries, and load can heavily reduce effective SPD.

⸻

INT

INT represents reasoning, magical control, learning, analysis, memory, technical skill, and complex problem-solving.

It includes:

* spell control
* mana shaping
* tactical thinking
* crafting planning
* investigation
* memory
* learning speed
* understanding systems
* recipe comprehension
* strategy
* social reading when analytical
* ability design/control

INT affects:

* MP scaling
* magical accuracy/control
* crafting quality
* investigation reasoning
* ability stabilization
* ritual work
* tactical combat
* learning from failure
* identifying enemy traits
* understanding interfaces/status panels
* domain growth in knowledge-heavy fields

INT does not automatically mean:

* knowing hidden facts
* solving cases without evidence
* social wisdom
* emotional intelligence
* perfect memory of unknown lore
* free spellcasting

High INT helps interpret clues.

It does not create proof.

⸻

LCK

LCK represents fortune, fate pressure, opportunistic timing, rare chances, instability handling, and narrative luck.

It includes:

* narrow escapes
* lucky finds
* rare drops
* chance-based outcomes
* resisting bad random turns
* unstable ability behavior
* critical opportunities
* gamble-based actions
* coincidence in the player’s favor
* survival at the edge of failure

LCK affects:

* critical hit chance
* rare loot chance
* avoiding worst-case failure
* unstable ability outcomes
* chance discoveries
* favorable timing
* random encounter tilt
* escape from death-boundary only when plausible
* chaotic magic or mutation outcomes

LCK does not automatically mean:

* immunity to consequences
* guaranteed success
* rewriting truth
* free resurrection
* always finding treasure
* escaping every death

LCK can soften danger.

It cannot override hard validation.

4. Base human stat formula

Chunk 19 gave target stat ranges.

Chunk 21 gives a usable formula.

A simple default base stat formula for a human-like balanced character:

BaseStat = 5 + floor(Level × 1.25)

This gives:

Level	Balanced Base Stat
1	6
5	11
10	17
20	30
50	67
100	130
200	255
500	630
1000	1255

This lands inside the “average stat” range from Chunk 19.

However, actual stats should not all remain equal.

Actual stats should be modified by:

* race/species
* class/build
* stat training
* domain growth
* ability/passive bonuses
* equipment
* injuries/conditions
* temporary buffs/debuffs

So a Level 100 human with no specialization might have stats near 130, but a focused warrior may have ATK 190 and INT 80.

5. Stat spread model

AIDRPG should use stat spread categories instead of giving every character equal stats.

At each level, stats should fall into one of these categories:

Category	Multiplier Against Balanced Base
Neglected	×0.45–0.65
Weak	×0.65–0.85
Average	×0.85–1.15
Strong	×1.15–1.6
Specialized	×1.6–2.1
Extreme	×2.1–2.6
Supernatural Extreme	×2.6+ only with strong support

Example:

Level 100 balanced base stat = 130.

A Level 100 warrior might have:

* ATK specialized: 130 × 1.7 = 221
* DEF strong: 130 × 1.4 = 182
* SPD average: 130 × 1.0 = 130
* INT weak: 130 × 0.7 = 91
* LCK average: 130 × 0.9 = 117

This matches the Chunk 19 expected ranges.

⸻

6. Final stat calculation order

AIDRPG should calculate current stats in layers.

Recommended order:

1. Level Base
2. Race/Species Modifier
3. Class/Build Modifier
4. Stat Training Additions
5. Domain/Specialization Bonuses
6. Ability/Passive Bonuses
7. Equipment Bonuses
8. Temporary Buffs
9. Injuries/Conditions/Fatigue Penalties
10. Effective Stat Output

This prevents race, buffs, and equipment from multiplying each other endlessly.

The system should distinguish:

* permanent base stat
* trained stat bonus
* passive stat bonus
* equipment stat bonus
* temporary stat modifier
* effective current stat

Player-facing /sheet should usually show current effective stats and maybe base stats if useful.

Debug commands can show the full breakdown.

7. Race/species stat modifiers

Race/species should modify stats using modest multipliers or category shifts.

Suggested numerical conversion:

Race Modifier Category	Multiplier
Very Low	×0.65
Low	×0.8
Slightly Low	×0.9
Baseline	×1.0
Slightly High	×1.1
High	×1.25
Very High	×1.45
Extreme	×1.75+ only with major tradeoffs

Example:

A Level 50 human balanced base stat is 67.

A Level 50 dragonkin with High DEF:

DEF = 67 × 1.25 = 83 before class/training

A Level 50 fairy with Very Low physical DEF:

DEF = 67 × 0.65 = 43 before class/training

Race modifiers should usually be applied before class/build and training.

Race defines the body.

Class/training defines what the character does with that body.

9. Build archetype philosophy

Build identity should affect stat growth, not force exact classes.

A player may begin as “Brawler” but develop magic.

A mage may train swordplay.

A fairy warrior may fight through speed and precision rather than raw strength.

A giant scholar may have high INT and high HP.

AIDRPG should track build identity as a summary:

Build Identity: Human Brawler with developing mana control

or:

Build Identity: Dragonkin defensive spellblade

Build identity helps:

* Context
* stat growth direction
* ability candidate interpretation
* XP lane selection
* enemy matchup validation
* card summaries

It should not prevent creative growth.

⸻

10. Stat training additions

Stats should grow from use.

AIDRPG should distinguish level-based growth from training-based growth.

Basic model:

PermanentStat = LevelStatAfterRaceAndBuild + TrainingBonus + PassivePermanentBonus

TrainingBonus comes from stat XP.

Exact XP curve is in Chunk 22, but Chunk 21 defines how training appears.

Training should affect:

* ATK when attacking, lifting, grappling, breaking, weapon training
* DEF when blocking, enduring, armor training, resisting strain
* SPD when dodging, sprinting, stealth-moving, reacting
* INT when studying, casting, crafting, analyzing, investigating
* LCK when surviving risky uncertainty, gambling, chaotic magic, rare opportunities

Training should not raise stats from trivial repetition forever.

Example:

Punching a tree at Level 200 should not keep meaningfully raising ATK.

Fighting a dangerous monster, training with a master, or learning a new technique should.

⸻

11. Stat soft caps by level

To avoid absurd early stat spikes, AIDRPG should use soft caps.

Suggested soft cap:

Level Soft Cap for a Stat = BalancedBaseStat × 2.1

At Level 10:

BalancedBaseStat = 17.

Soft cap:

17 × 2.1 = 35

So a Level 10 player having ATK 35 is extreme but possible with race/class/specialization.

ATK 200 at Level 10 should require extraordinary story support, such as divine blessing, monster transformation, temporary buff, or broken artifact.

At Level 1000:

BalancedBaseStat = 1255.

Soft cap:

1255 × 2.1 = 2635

This fits the Chunk 19 extreme stat range.

AIDRPG can allow above soft cap, but only if clearly supported.

⸻

12. Stat hard warning threshold

Instead of a strict hard cap, use a warning threshold.

Suggested warning threshold:

Stat Warning Threshold = BalancedBaseStat × 2.6

If a stat exceeds this without strong support, audit should flag it.

Examples of valid reasons to exceed warning threshold:

* divine transformation
* racial evolution
* artifact bond
* stat absorption milestone
* major class awakening
* high-risk curse
* temporary buff
* world-scale blessing

Examples of invalid reasons:

* repeated trivial training
* AI randomly says “your strength triples”
* card text
* dream
* joke
* status panel contradiction

This keeps high-scale power possible but controlled.

⸻

13. Derived stat concepts

AIDRPG can optionally use derived values internally.

Do not expose too many derived stats to the player by default.

Useful derived values:

Physical Power

Physical Power = ATK + (DEF × 0.25) + (EP factor)

Used for grappling, lifting, breaking, raw force.

Combat Defense

Combat Defense = DEF + equipment armor + shield + relevant ability

Used for reducing damage.

Evasion

Evasion = SPD + relevant domain + terrain modifier - burden penalty

Used for dodge/chase/avoidance.

Magic Control

Magic Control = INT + relevant magic domain + focus/equipment + ability rank

Used for spell stability and ability costs.

Survival Endurance

Survival Endurance = DEF + EP factor + survival domain + race modifier

Used for travel, weather, hunger, exhaustion.

Fortune Pressure

Fortune Pressure = LCK + relevant fate/passive modifiers

Used for rare/lucky/unstable events.

These derived stats should guide validation but should not become a second confusing character sheet unless needed.

14. HP formula

HP represents body integrity, health, survivability, or equivalent body stability.

Default human-like HP formula:

Max HP = 50 + (Level × 10) + (DEF × 8)

Examples:

Level 1 human, DEF 5:

50 + 10 + 40 = 100 HP

Level 50 warrior, DEF 90:

50 + 500 + 720 = 1270 HP

Level 100 mage, DEF 80:

50 + 1000 + 640 = 1690 HP

Level 1000 tank, DEF 2600:

50 + 10000 + 20800 = 30850 HP

This fits the Chunk 19 HP expectations.

Race/species then modifies HP.

16. MP formula

MP represents mana, spiritual power, magic capacity, divine/infernal energy, psychic force, or equivalent.

Default human-like MP formula:

Max MP = 20 + (Level × 7) + (INT × 10)

Examples:

Level 1 human, INT 5:

20 + 7 + 50 = 77 MP

Level 50 mage, INT 120:

20 + 350 + 1200 = 1570 MP

Level 100 warrior, INT 80:

20 + 700 + 800 = 1520 MP

Level 1000 archmage, INT 2550:

20 + 7000 + 25500 = 32520 MP

This supports high-level spellcasting while allowing meaningful costs.

18. EP formula

EP represents stamina, exertion capacity, physical energy, focus endurance, action endurance, or body-control energy.

Default human-like EP formula:

Max EP = 40 + (Level × 9) + ((DEF + SPD) × 5)

Examples:

Level 1 human, DEF 5, SPD 5:

40 + 9 + 50 = 99 EP

Level 50 brawler, DEF 85, SPD 75:

40 + 450 + 800 = 1290 EP

Level 100 rogue, DEF 95, SPD 190:

40 + 900 + 1425 = 2365 EP

Level 1000 sword saint, DEF 950, SPD 1700:

40 + 9000 + 13250 = 22290 EP

EP should be highly relevant for:

* combat action
* sprinting
* dodging
* blocking
* travel exertion
* crafting labor
* physical training
* maintaining physical stances
* overexertion

20. Resource calculation order

Recommended resource calculation:

1. Calculate base stat values.
2. Calculate Max HP from Level and DEF.
3. Calculate Max MP from Level and INT.
4. Calculate Max EP from Level, DEF, and SPD.
5. Apply race/species resource modifiers.
6. Apply class/build resource modifiers.
7. Apply passive/trait modifiers.
8. Apply equipment/artifact modifiers.
9. Apply curses/blessings.
10. Clamp current resources to new max unless special rule says otherwise.

Important:

When max resources increase from level-up, current resources do not automatically fully refill unless the design decides level-up restores resources.

Recommended default:

* level-up restores a small percentage
* full refill only if setting/system says so

Example:

Level-up may restore:

10% HP, 10% MP, 15% EP

or simply increase maximums without restoring current values.

This should be a setting.

22. Injury and condition effective stat penalties

AIDRPG should distinguish permanent stats from effective stats.

Effective stats can be reduced by injuries, conditions, fatigue, or resource depletion.

Suggested penalty table:

State	Effective Penalty
minor injury	-5% to relevant action/stat
moderate injury	-10% to -20%
severe injury	-25% to -50%
critical injury	-50%+ or action blocked
light fatigue	-5% SPD/ATK/EP actions
tired	-10% to -15%
exhausted	-25% to -40%
0 EP	physical actions severely impaired
low MP	magic actions unstable
poison	varies, often DEF/SPD/INT penalty
fear/panic	INT/SPD/action control penalty
bleeding	HP loss over time and physical penalty
broken limb	action-specific block or heavy penalty
blinded	accuracy, navigation, reading penalty
stunned	immediate action blocked/reduced

Examples:

Broken right arm:

* sword attacks using right arm may be blocked
* two-handed weapons blocked
* casting gestures impaired if needed
* general ATK not permanently reduced, but effective action is heavily impaired

Exhausted:

* ATK may remain high on sheet
* effective physical actions are risky
* EP costs may increase
* overexertion may cause injury

23. Resource threshold effects

Resources should have thresholds that affect validation.

HP thresholds

HP %	Meaning
100–75%	healthy or mostly stable
74–50%	wounded but functional
49–25%	heavily wounded
24–10%	critical danger
9–1%	death-boundary / collapse risk
0%	unconscious, dying, destroyed, inert, or equivalent depending body

MP thresholds

MP %	Meaning
100–50%	normal casting
49–25%	cautious casting
24–10%	unstable or limited casting
9–1%	severe risk, weak magic only
0%	no normal magic unless overcharge/sacrifice

EP thresholds

EP %	Meaning
100–50%	normal exertion
49–25%	tiring
24–10%	exhausted actions risky
9–1%	near collapse
0%	physical overexertion, collapse risk, severe penalties

Thresholds should affect Context.

Example:

If EP is below 10%, Context should mention exhaustion when physical action is likely.

⸻

24. Low resource penalties

Low resources should not always prevent action.

They should increase cost, risk, and failure.

Low HP

Effects:

* injury worsens easily
* death-boundary risk
* reduced endurance
* less tolerance for damage
* fear/social vulnerability if visible

Low MP

Effects:

* spell failure
* weaker output
* backlash risk
* inability to maintain summons/barriers
* loss of magical perception
* overcharge temptation

Low EP

Effects:

* slower movement
* weak attacks
* poor dodges
* block failure
* collapse risk
* worse travel
* worse crafting labor
* longer recovery needed

AIDRPG should avoid binary “can/cannot” unless resource is truly insufficient.

⸻

25. Overexertion

Players may act beyond safe resource limits.

Overexertion allows dramatic freeform play.

Example:

“I force myself to stand and swing one more time.”

If EP is 0, this may still happen, but with consequences.

Possible overexertion costs:

* HP damage
* injury worsening
* condition
* unconsciousness after action
* temporary stat penalty
* ability backlash
* fatigue increase
* resource debt
* partial success

Overexertion should be possible but dangerous.

It should not be free.

26. Stat comparison bands

AIDRPG needs stat comparison bands for validation.

Compare effective player stat against effective enemy stat or difficulty.

Ratio	Meaning
player < 0.25×	nearly impossible
0.25–0.5×	heavily outmatched
0.5–0.75×	disadvantaged
0.75–1.25×	contested/even
1.25–1.75×	favored
1.75–2.5×	strongly favored
2.5–4×	overwhelming
4×+	trivial unless special context

Example:

Player ATK 100 versus enemy DEF 200:

Ratio 0.5.

Direct damage is disadvantaged.

Player may need technique, weakness, magic, or repeated effort.

Player ATK 500 versus enemy DEF 100:

Ratio 5.

Physical damage is overwhelming unless enemy has special resistance.

When the player writes a natural action, AIDRPG should identify the dominant stat.

Primary:

Secondary:

* SPD, Brawling Domain, EP

“I brace behind my shield.”

Primary:

* DEF

Secondary:

* equipment, Shield Domain, EP

Primary:

* SPD

Secondary:

* EP, terrain, injury

Primary:

* INT

Secondary:

* Magic Domain, Investigation Domain, relevant lore

Primary:

* SPD or ATK depending action

* LCK, EP

* ATK/SPD depending action

This keeps freeform inputs grounded.

29. Stat and domain relationship

Stats are broad ability.

Domains are trained application.

Example:

High ATK but low Sword Domain:

* strong swings
* poor blade control
* vulnerable to skilled counters

Low ATK but high Sword Domain:

* precise technique
* can exploit openings
* less raw damage

High INT but low Magic Domain:

* understands theory
* unstable spellcasting

Low INT but high Fire Magic Domain:

* practiced fire spells
* weaker at unfamiliar magic

High SPD but low Stealth Domain:

* fast movement
* may still make noise

Stat plus domain gives best results.

Neither fully replaces the other.

32. Equipment contribution

Equipment should affect effective stats, not usually permanent stats.

Examples:

* sword improves weapon damage
* armor improves combat defense
* boots improve travel/dodge
* staff improves spell control
* ring improves MP or LCK
* shield improves blocking
* tool improves crafting

Equipment bonuses should be contextual.

A magic staff should not improve punching.

Heavy armor may improve DEF but reduce SPD and increase EP cost.

Example:

Iron Sword:
ATK +20 for sword attacks
No bonus to unarmed or magic
Heavy Plate:
DEF +150 against physical attacks
SPD -50
EP costs +10% in travel/combat

Equipment is important but should not dominate everything unless artifact-level.

38. Stat update from level-up

When level increases, stats should not automatically all jump equally unless the build is balanced.

Suggested level-up stat gain:

Each level grants:

* small automatic growth to all stats
* extra growth to build-favored stats
* extra growth from recent training lanes
* possible race/species growth bias

Example:

Human Brawler level-up may favor:

* ATK
* DEF
* EP-related growth
* Brawling Domain

Elf Mage level-up may favor:

* INT
* MP
* Magic Domain
* SPD modestly

Slime evolution level-up may favor:

* body-related DEF/HP
* absorption traits
* shapeshift ability candidates

Exact XP/stat growth is Chunk 22.

Chunk 21 rule:

Level-up should respect build, race, and recent behavior.

39. Stat update from training

Training should affect specific stats.

Examples:

Punching drills:

* ATK XP
* Brawling Domain XP
* EP cost

Shield drills:

* DEF XP
* Shield Domain XP
* EP cost

Sprint training:

* SPD XP
* Athletics/Travel Domain XP
* fatigue

Meditation/mana control:

* INT XP
* Magic Domain XP
* MP recovery/control maybe

Gambling/risky survival:

* LCK XP only if meaningful risk or fortune event

Training must be:

* time-consuming
* resource-consuming
* risk/effort-based
* anti-farm controlled

No infinite stat growth from repeating trivial lines.

40. Stat update from ability absorption

Some abilities may grant stats from absorbed creatures or powers.

Chunk 26 defines this in detail.

Chunk 21 sets the rule:

Absorption should usually grant stat XP, not direct stat points.

Direct permanent stat points require:

* strong source
* meaningful risk
* compatible target
* cap check
* operation ledger
* validation
* anti-farm check

Example:

Absorbing a minor slime at Level 100 should not grant +50 DEF.

It might grant:

* tiny Body/Adaptation XP
* Slime Body candidate evidence
* no meaningful stat gain if far below player scale

Absorbing a dragon core at Level 300 may grant:

* significant stat XP
* ability evidence
* mutation risk
* temporary condition
* possible race evolution candidate

Not automatic full dragon stats.

⸻

41. Stat update from equipment

Equipment should usually provide bonuses while equipped.

Examples:

Iron sword:

* ATK bonus for sword attacks

Heavy armor:

* DEF bonus against physical hits
* SPD penalty
* EP cost increase

Mana ring:

* MP max bonus
* maybe Magic Control bonus
* possible corruption cost

Boots of haste:

* SPD bonus
* EP or MP upkeep
* cooldown or exhaustion

Equipment should not permanently increase base stats unless it is a bound artifact, graft, or transformation.

Even then, it should be logged and validated.

42. Stat update from conditions

Conditions can temporarily alter stats.

Examples:

Blessing:

* +10% to relevant stats

Poison:

* -20% DEF/SPD or HP drain

Rage:

* +20% ATK
* -15% INT
* increased EP drain

Fear:

* -20% ATK/INT for direct confrontation
* maybe +10% SPD for fleeing

Focus:

* +15% INT/Magic Control
* EP/MP upkeep

Exhaustion:

* -30% physical action effectiveness

Transformation:

* may replace race/body modifiers temporarily

Conditions should have duration and source.

No permanent stat change from temporary condition unless transformation stabilizes.

⸻

43. Stat update from race transformation

If race changes, stats/resources may need recalculation.

Example:

Human becomes Vampire.

Possible changes:

* HP modifier changes
* MP modifier changes
* EP/fatigue logic changes
* poison/disease rules change
* sunlight weakness added
* hunger condition added
* racial abilities added as candidates/passives
* equipment may still fit
* social/reputation consequences possible

This should mark dirty:

* Player Sheet
* Body & Status
* Abilities
* Inventory/Equipment if body changed
* Context
* race/species card if any

Race transformation is not a simple stat buff.

It changes body logic.

49. Resource recovery formulas

Exact recovery may vary by setting, but Chunk 21 should define defaults.

Short rest

Requires:

* safety
* several minutes to an hour
* no active combat

Default recovery:

EP: 15–30% max
MP: 5–15% max
HP: 0–5% max unless treated

Long rest / sleep

Requires:

* several hours
* relative safety
* food/water/shelter as needed

Default recovery:

EP: 60–100% max
MP: 40–100% max
HP: 10–40% max depending injuries/treatment

Full downtime

Requires:

* days or proper care

Default recovery:

HP: major recovery
injuries: improve
conditions: may resolve
MP/EP: full if no blockers

Race/body modifies recovery.

Examples:

Construct needs repair for HP.

Undead may need death energy.

Slime may need mass/water/mana.

⸻

50. Recovery and maximum resources

When max resources change, current values should be handled carefully.

If max HP increases from 100 to 120:

Default:

Current HP remains same unless level-up recovery applies.

Example:

HP 50/100 becomes HP 50/120.

If level-up gives +10% new max recovery:

HP 50/100 → max 120 → recover 12 → HP 62/120

Do not automatically full heal unless setting says level-up fully restores.

This prevents level-up from being an exploit.

⸻

51. Stat/resource safeguards

51.1 No stat change from vague feeling

“You feel stronger” is not enough for permanent ATK increase.

It may be:

* flavor
* temporary confidence
* pending training result
* level-up flavor if XP threshold reached

51.2 No AI status override without validation

AI-generated stats must not overwrite state unless source is valid initialization/manual/system display.

51.3 No duplicate stat/resource change

OperationLedger dedupes stat gains, HP damage, MP cost, EP cost, recovery, level-up.

51.4 No resource costs ignored

Abilities, sprinting, heavy attacks, crafting labor, and overexertion should cost appropriate resources.

51.5 No impossible resource spending

If MP is 0, normal spellcasting should fail or overcharge.

51.6 No stat gain from trivial repetition

Anti-farm applies.

51.7 No race/class infinite multiplier stacking

Apply modifiers in controlled order.

51.8 No permanent equipment stat unless valid

Equipment bonuses are usually conditional.

51.9 No injury-free low HP

Low HP should matter.

51.10 No high stat automatic proof

INT does not solve cases without evidence.

56. Final Chunk 21 doctrine

Stats are the player’s broad capability foundation.

ATK measures offensive force.

DEF measures durability and stability.

SPD measures speed and reaction.

INT measures reasoning, learning, crafting, investigation, and magic control.

LCK measures fortune, unstable outcomes, and rare opportunity.

HP measures body integrity.

MP measures magical or spiritual power.

EP measures exertion and action endurance.

Level creates base scale.

Race modifies body logic.

Class/build modifies growth direction.

Training adds earned improvement.

Domains and abilities refine application.

Equipment modifies context-specific actions.

Injuries, conditions, fatigue, and low resources change effective stats.

Stats should guide freeform validation, not replace it.

High stats do not create truth.

Low resources should create risk.

Race and class should not stack into infinite multipliers.

Equipment should not grant permanent stats unless explicitly bonded or transformative.

AI-generated stat panels are mirrors unless validated.

Commands show exact numbers.

Context usually shows practical meaning.

The goal is to make the player’s growth measurable, scalable, and meaningful from Level 1 to Level 1000 without turning AI Dungeon into a rigid spreadsheet or letting freeform narration ignore the numbers entirely.
