AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 26: Stat Absorption, Copy Abilities, Devour Growth, Adaptation, Evolution, and Anime-Style Power Systems

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 26 defines how AIDRPG handles anime-style growth systems.

This includes:

* absorbing stats
* devouring monsters
* eating cores
* copying abilities
* mimicking techniques
* stealing powers
* adapting to damage
* evolving after combat
* gaining traits from defeated enemies
* learning by observation
* growing through pain
* mutating from monster essence
* awakening bloodlines
* transforming race/species
* gaining powers from souls, contracts, spirits, divine blessings, curses, or artifacts

This chunk answers:

Can a player gain stats from enemies?

Can a player copy a spell by seeing it?

Can a slime player absorb monsters?

Can a vampire grow by drinking blood?

Can a dragonkin awaken stronger dragon traits?

Can a player adapt to poison?

Can a player gain resistance after surviving fire?

Can a player devour 100 rats for infinite stats?

Can a player absorb a dragon core and become a dragon god?

The central rule is:

Anime-style growth is allowed, but it must be bounded, validated, source-based, and auditable.

Growth should feel exciting.

It should not become infinite free power.

⸻

2. Core philosophy

AIDRPG should support power fantasy.

Many players want systems inspired by:

* isekai
* cultivation
* monster evolution
* devourer builds
* skill-copy builds
* mimic powers
* adaptation powers
* reincarnation systems
* dungeon monster growth
* RPG status panels
* bloodline awakening
* soul absorption
* demon contracts
* divine blessings
* cursed evolution
* “gain stats by eating monsters”
* “gain resistance by surviving damage”
* “copy enemy skills after seeing them”
* “learn faster because of Quick Learner”
* “turn monster traits into abilities”

This is fun and should be supported.

But these systems are dangerous because they can easily break the game.

Without safeguards, a player can say:

“I absorb the rat and gain all its stats.”

“I copy the demon lord’s ultimate spell.”

“I adapt to all damage.”

“I eat 100 slimes and become immune to everything.”

“I devour the dragon and gain its full power.”

“I observe god once and learn creation magic.”

AIDRPG must prevent this from becoming uncontrolled.

The correct approach is:

Absorption usually gives progress, evidence, stat XP, traits, candidates, or temporary effects — not instant full mastery.

3. Ownership boundaries

AbilityReducer owns

* copy abilities
* mimic abilities
* absorption abilities
* adaptation abilities
* evolution abilities
* copied ability candidates
* stolen power records
* ability evidence
* ability rank changes
* passive/candidate creation
* unstable powers
* transformed ability variants

ProgressionReducer owns

* stat XP from absorption
* domain XP from copied learning
* level XP from meaningful absorption events
* anti-farm scaling
* stat increases
* source caps

BodyReducer owns

* mutation conditions
* corruption
* backlash injuries
* body instability
* poison/adaptation consequences
* transformation body effects
* HP/MP/EP costs
* compatibility sickness

PlayerReducer owns

* race/species changes
* ancestry/origin updates
* player sheet race/body identity

InventoryReducer owns

* monster cores
* consumed materials
* essence items
* blood samples
* body parts
* artifacts
* ownership/custody/access of absorbable items

ActorReducer / ScaleReducer owns

* defeated enemy records
* corpse/source availability
* group source counts
* unique enemy identity
* boss source state

CardSync owns

* generated ability cards after stabilization
* race/evolution card updates
* absorbed trait card updates if important
* avoiding duplicate ability cards

ContextBuilder owns

* current warnings about unstable absorbed powers
* candidate versus stable reminders
* active mutation/corruption warnings
* relevant limits in the current scene

No system should directly grant permanent power without going through validation and the correct owner.

4. Anime-style growth categories

AIDRPG should separate these systems clearly.

Stat Absorption

The player gains stat XP or rarely stat points from a source.

Example:

Absorbing a beast core grants ATK XP and Beast Adaptation progress.

Devour Growth

The player eats, consumes, digests, or assimilates a creature/material.

Example:

A slime devours a wolf corpse and gains minor predator-trait evidence.

Ability Copy

The player learns or imitates an observed ability.

Example:

The player sees Shadow Step and creates a Shadow Step candidate.

Skill Mimicry

The player imitates a mundane technique or combat style.

Example:

The player copies a boxer’s footwork after seeing it.

Power Theft

The player steals part of an enemy’s power.

Example:

A vampire drains blood to gain temporary strength.

Adaptation

The player becomes more resistant or responsive after exposure.

Example:

After surviving weak poison several times, the player gains Poison Tolerance candidate.

Evolution

The player’s body/species/build changes after enough progress.

Example:

Slime evolves into Acid Slime.

Bloodline Awakening

Inherited power activates through stress, ritual, age, or story event.

Example:

Dragonkin awakens Ember Breath.

Soul/Core Absorption

The player absorbs a soul, core, crystal, essence, or magic organ.

Example:

Absorbing a dragon core grants Dragon Essence progress, risk, and possible breath evolution.

Contract/Blessing/Curse Growth

Power is gained from outside authority.

Example:

Demon pact grants Hellfire candidate but creates debt and holy vulnerability.

Each category needs different rules.

6. Source availability rules

The system must verify that the source is actually available.

Valid source availability examples:

* enemy defeated and body remains
* monster core dropped and player has access
* blood is collected
* player is bitten/exposed
* enemy ability observed clearly
* teacher demonstrates technique
* artifact is in player custody
* spirit willingly grants pact
* ritual captures essence
* player consumes material
* player touches magic crystal
* player survives damage exposure

Invalid source examples:

* enemy escaped
* body destroyed
* source is only rumored
* source exists only in Story Card text
* source was mentioned in a dream
* source belongs to an absent scene
* source already consumed
* source is in someone else’s custody
* source is hypothetical
* player merely says they absorbed it
* source is debug/example text

No source, no growth.

⸻

7. Source uniqueness

Sources should be classified by uniqueness.

Source Uniqueness	Meaning
common	ordinary rat, goblin, wolf, basic herb
uncommon	magical beast, trained enemy, rare material
rare	special monster, named creature part, unusual core
unique	named boss, one-of-a-kind artifact, bloodline source
legendary	dragon heart, lich core, divine relic
mythic	god avatar fragment, world beast essence
cosmic	reality-scale source

Uniqueness affects:

* potential gain
* risk
* card eligibility
* anti-farm cap
* mutation chance
* story consequences

Common sources should not grant huge permanent growth.

Unique sources can grant major growth, but should also carry major risks and consequences.

8. Compatibility

Absorption and copying require compatibility.

Compatibility factors:

* race/species
* body type
* existing traits
* ability category
* domain knowledge
* stat support
* source element
* source tier
* player level
* current condition
* equipment/focus
* ritual support
* source consent or resistance
* corruption risk
* divine/infernal alignment
* biological possibility
* magical framework

Compatibility levels:

Compatibility	Meaning
incompatible	cannot absorb/copy normally
poor	possible only with major risk or special method
partial	limited gain, high failure chance
moderate	usable with cost/risk
strong	good fit
perfect	source matches build/body/ability very well

Example:

Human eats wolf meat:

* can gain food/energy
* maybe Survival/Body training
* no automatic wolf stats

Wolfkin absorbs wolf spirit:

* moderate to strong compatibility

Slime absorbs slime core:

* strong compatibility

Fire dragonkin absorbs fire drake core:

* strong compatibility, but still capped

Ice fairy absorbs fire demon heart:

* poor/incompatible, high backlash/corruption risk

9. Gain types

Absorption/copy events can grant different gain types.

Possible gains:

* Level XP
* stat XP
* domain XP
* ability evidence
* ability candidate
* passive candidate
* trait candidate
* temporary buff
* resistance progress
* mutation progress
* race evolution progress
* skill insight
* recipe/material insight
* condition
* corruption
* sickness
* backlash
* hunger/satiety
* relationship/reputation effect
* title progress
* no gain

Most absorption should grant progress, not instant permanent power.

Preferred gain hierarchy:

1. Evidence or progress
2. Stat XP / Domain XP
3. Temporary effect
4. Candidate ability/trait
5. Stable ability/trait only after enough evidence
6. Direct stat point only rarely
7. Race evolution only after major threshold/story event

10. Stat absorption rule

Stat absorption should usually grant stat XP, not raw stat points.

Default:

Absorption Gain = stat XP based on source tier, compatibility, uniqueness, and risk.

Direct stat points require:

* source is meaningful
* player has compatible absorption ability/body
* source is not trivial
* source is consumed or otherwise spent
* source has not been used before
* gain passes cap
* event is validated
* operation ledger records it
* risk/consequence considered

Example:

Absorbing a wolf essence at Level 10:

* ATK XP
* SPD XP
* Beast/Predator Domain XP
* possible Scent/Tracking candidate if compatible

Not:

* +20 ATK instantly
* full wolf transformation
* permanent fangs unless mutation/evolution supports it

⸻

11. Suggested stat XP absorption formula

Use source effective level and compatibility.

Base Absorption Stat XP = Source Effective Level × 5

Then apply modifiers:

Modifier	Multiplier
far weaker than player	×0 to ×0.1
weaker than player	×0.25
near player scale	×1
stronger than player	×1.5
much stronger	×2 to ×3, high risk
common source	×0.5
uncommon source	×1
rare source	×1.5
unique source	×2
legendary source	×3
mythic source	×5
poor compatibility	×0.25
partial compatibility	×0.5
moderate compatibility	×1
strong compatibility	×1.5
perfect compatibility	×2
safe/processed source	×0.75
dangerous raw source	×1.25 plus risk
repeated same source	decay applies

Example:

Level 20 slime absorbs Level 18 acid slime core.

Base:

18 × 5 = 90 stat XP

Near scale:

×1

Uncommon:

×1

Strong compatibility:

×1.5

Final:

135 stat XP distributed into relevant stats/traits

Distribution might be:

* DEF XP
* INT XP if magical control
* Slime Body/Acid trait evidence
* Acid Resistance progress

Not all into raw ATK.

⸻

12. Direct stat point rule

Direct stat points are rare.

Direct stat gain may happen from:

* major boss core
* divine blessing
* evolution milestone
* stat crystal
* system reward
* race transformation
* legendary training breakthrough
* unique artifact fusion
* high-risk sacrifice

Direct stat gain should be small relative to current scale.

Suggested direct stat gain cap:

Source Scale	Direct Stat Gain
common	none
uncommon	none or +1 if low-level and special
rare	+1 to one stat or stat XP
unique	+1–3 distributed
legendary	+3–10 distributed
mythic	+10–25 distributed, high risk
cosmic	special story-scale, not normal math

At Level 1000, +1 stat can still matter as permanent growth.

Do not casually grant +1000 stats from one monster unless campaign is intentionally absurd and the source is cosmic with severe consequences.

⸻

13. Stat absorption distribution

Absorbed stat XP should go to traits that make sense.

Examples:

Wolf:

* ATK XP
* SPD XP
* Survival/Tracking Domain XP
* Scent candidate if compatible

Ogre:

* ATK XP
* DEF XP
* HP/body progress
* Brute Force trait candidate

Fairy:

* MP/INT XP
* LCK XP
* Illusion/Glamour Domain XP
* tiny flight magic candidate if compatible

Dragon:

* ATK XP
* DEF XP
* MP XP through INT/magic domain
* Dragon Breath evidence
* elemental resistance progress
* dragon mutation risk

Slime:

* DEF/body XP
* cohesion trait progress
* acid/absorption candidate
* body instability risk

Construct:

* DEF XP
* crafting/engineering insight
* core energy candidate if compatible
* repair/upgrade material rather than biological absorption

Spirit:

* MP/INT/LCK XP
* Spirit Sight/possession candidate
* soul instability risk

Do not grant unrelated stats unless source supports it.

⸻

14. Devour growth

Devour growth is a subtype of absorption where the player physically consumes a source.

Valid devour sources:

* corpse
* core
* blood
* flesh
* essence crystal
* mana organ
* soul fragment
* plant material
* monster part
* artifact shard
* magical liquid

Devour requirements:

* source available
* player can physically or magically consume it
* body supports digestion/assimilation
* source not too dangerous without preparation
* source not already consumed
* ownership/custody/access checked
* contamination/corruption checked

Devour outcomes:

* food/nutrition
* stat XP
* domain XP
* ability evidence
* temporary buff
* mutation progress
* sickness
* poison
* corruption
* rejection/vomiting
* body instability
* hunger change
* social/reputation consequences if witnessed

Eating something should not automatically mean gaining its full power.

⸻

15. Devour compatibility by race/body

Human

Can eat normal food/meat.

Cannot normally digest monster cores into powers unless ability/system/magic supports it.

Slime

Often strong devour compatibility.

May absorb mass/material/traits, but needs caps.

Vampire

Blood devour compatibility.

Growth through blood, life force, or essence.

Must manage hunger, sunlight/holy risks, consent/social consequences.

Demon

May consume souls/corruption/contracts if setting supports.

High corruption/social/divine risk.

Undead

May consume death energy, souls, flesh, blood, or necromancy depending subtype.

Normal food may not help.

Construct

May assimilate parts, cores, mana batteries, upgrades.

Does not digest flesh normally.

Dragon

May consume elemental essence, hoard magic, powerful prey.

May have age/element constraints.

Spirit

May absorb emotion, memory, worship, spirit energy, essence.

Physical food irrelevant unless manifested.

Devour rules must follow body category.

⸻

16. Devour anti-farm

Devouring repeated weak sources decays quickly.

Rules:

* same species/source type decays
* far weaker sources give little/no gain
* common food gives nutrition, not stat gain
* self-created sources give little/no gain
* summoned creatures give no normal devour reward
* source must be spent
* scene/day caps apply
* compatibility does not bypass caps
* repeated consumption may cause sickness/corruption instead of growth

Example:

Slime devours 100 rats at Level 50.

Expected:

* maybe nutrition/mass recovery
* no meaningful stat gain after early decay
* possible disease/taint risk if rats are dirty
* no infinite growth

Example:

Level 5 slime devours first magic rat core.

Expected:

* small stat XP
* possible poison/taint clue
* maybe ability evidence if core special

⸻

17. Ability copy

Ability copying means learning from observation, contact, analysis, or absorption.

Copy methods:

* visual observation
* magical analysis
* mimic talent
* skill theft
* devour essence
* memory absorption
* soul reading
* teacher demonstration
* status/system scan
* artifact recording
* bloodline resonance

Copy result should usually be:

* observation evidence
* candidate ability
* unstable imitation
* weaker version
* temporary echo
* partial technique
* style insight
* domain XP
* no gain if incompatible

Not normally:

* full stable copied ability
* full enemy rank
* full enemy cost efficiency
* full passive immunity
* all variants

⸻

18. Copy requirements

To copy an ability, check:

* was ability observed clearly?
* did player understand it?
* does player have copy/mimic talent?
* does player have relevant body?
* does player have relevant stat/domain?
* does player have enough level/scale?
* is source ability too high-tier?
* is source ability unique/divine/racial?
* does it require equipment/body part?
* was there time to study?
* was player under pressure?
* did enemy hide the method?
* did source resist copying?
* is copy permanent or temporary?

AIDRPG should not allow copying impossible body-specific powers without adaptation.

Example:

Human observes dragon flying.

Can learn:

* dragon flight mechanics?
* air movement insight?
* maybe anti-dragon tactics

Cannot instantly grow wings.

Unless:

* transformation ability
* dragon bloodline
* magic flight candidate
* artifact support

19. Copy compatibility table

Source Ability Type	Copy Difficulty
mundane skill	easiest
weapon technique	easy if body/equipment compatible
martial art	moderate
spell	moderate to hard
racial natural weapon	hard unless body compatible
passive resistance	hard, usually adaptation/evolution
regeneration	hard, body/source required
divine ability	very hard, requires blessing/law
demonic ability	hard, corruption/contract risk
cosmic ability	usually impossible without story leverage
unique boss ability	candidate only, major risk
artifact ability	may require item, not copyable directly

Example:

Copying sword feint:

* possible with observation and Sword Domain

Copying vampire regeneration:

* not normally possible by watching

Copying dragon breath:

* requires draconic body, spell emulation, or absorbed source

Copying god’s creation word:

* impossible without divine/cosmic story support

20. Copied ability rank

Copied ability should start lower than source.

Suggested rule:

Copied Ability Starting Stage = min(Candidate or Rank 1, Source Rank - 3)

For most cases:

* observed once: candidate
* practiced with support: unstable Rank 1
* taught directly: Rank 1–2
* absorbed compatible source: candidate to Rank 2 depending evidence
* copied from much stronger enemy: candidate only with risk

Example:

Enemy uses Rank 7 Shadow Step.

Player observes once with mimic talent.

Expected:

* Shadow Step candidate
* not Rank 7

Player trains and partially manifests it.

* Rank 1 unstable

After repeated successful use:

* Rank 2–3

⸻

21. Temporary copied powers

Some copy abilities may create temporary echoes.

Temporary copied power rules:

* duration limited
* cost higher
* effect weaker
* source link required
* ability may vanish
* leaves evidence for later learning
* may cause backlash
* may not create card unless stabilized

Example:

Mirror Mimic lets player use a weaker copy of enemy firebolt once.

Result:

* temporary Firebolt Echo
* high MP cost
* evidence for Firebolt candidate
* no stable Firebolt yet

Temporary powers are useful and fun without breaking long-term progression.

⸻

22. Skill mimicry

Skill mimicry applies to mundane or semi-mundane techniques.

Examples:

* copying boxing footwork
* copying sword stance
* copying guard’s spear grip
* copying thief’s lockpick motion
* copying monk breathing
* copying smith hammer rhythm

Skill mimicry can grant:

* domain XP
* talent evidence
* technique candidate
* small stat XP
* improved next attempt
* partial technique

Skill mimicry should not require magic unless the skill is supernatural.

Example:

Player from Earth remembers boxing.

AIDRPG may create:

* Brawler’s Instinct talent candidate
* Brawling Domain XP
* better interpretation of punches/footwork

But:

Real-world memory alone does not make them a master fighter.

Training and combat evidence are still needed.

⸻

23. Power theft

Power theft means taking power from another being.

Examples:

* vampire blood drain
* soul steal
* mana drain
* demon contract
* skill theft
* stealing divine spark
* taking enemy core
* absorbing spell mid-cast
* draining life force

Power theft is more aggressive than copy.

Requirements:

* target available
* contact/range
* ability exists
* target resistance
* source tier check
* compatibility
* moral/social consequences
* corruption risk
* resource cost
* target consequence
* duplicate/source cap

Power theft outcomes:

* temporary buff
* resource restoration
* stat XP
* ability evidence
* stolen charge
* target weakened
* backlash
* corruption
* relationship/reputation consequence

Power theft from major beings is dangerous.

Stealing from a god should not be casual.

⸻

24. Mana/life drain rules

Drain abilities should define:

* what is drained
* how much
* from whom
* range/contact
* resistance
* conversion efficiency
* target harm
* user cap
* overflow risk
* moral/social consequences
* whether drained power is temporary or permanent

Example:

Mana Drain Rank 2:

Drains small MP from touched target.
Conversion: 50% efficiency.
Limit: cannot exceed user's max MP.
Resistance: target magic defense applies.
Risk: backlash if target is much stronger or corrupted.

Drain should not bypass max resources unless overcharge/storage ability exists.

25. Adaptation

Adaptation means the player becomes better at resisting or responding to repeated exposure.

Examples:

* poison tolerance
* heat tolerance
* cold tolerance
* pain tolerance
* fear resistance
* mana backlash resistance
* acid resistance
* curse resistance
* anti-magic adaptation
* monster trait adaptation
* damage type adaptation
* enemy style adaptation

Adaptation should usually progress through:

* exposure
* survival
* evidence
* cost/consequence
* recovery
* repeated meaningful challenge
* compatible body/ability
* rank-up

Adaptation should not grant instant immunity.

⸻

26. Adaptation lifecycle

Exposure

The player experiences a damage type or condition.

Example:

minor poison.

Survival

The player survives and recovers.

Tolerance candidate

The system tracks possible adaptation.

Example:

Poison Tolerance candidate.

Partial resistance

Future similar exposure is slightly less severe.

Stable resistance

A passive trait forms.

Improved resistance

Resistance grows through harder exposure.

Counter-resistance

Specific counter is learned.

Immunity

Only at high rank, narrow scope, and with strong support.

Immunity should be rare and specific.

Example:

“Resistant to common weak goblin venom” is reasonable.

“Immune to all poison, disease, curses, acid, divine toxins, and cosmic corruption” is not.

⸻

27. Adaptation gain rules

Adaptation requires meaningful exposure.

Valid exposure:

* real poison affected player
* fire burned player
* curse nearly took hold
* repeated cold travel caused hardship
* enemy style forced adaptation
* mana backlash occurred
* body processed toxin with cost
* training used controlled exposure

Invalid exposure:

* harmless tiny exposure far below player scale
* pretend exposure
* dream-only exposure
* repeated safe self-harm
* immunity already blocks everything
* source was not real
* card text says exposure happened

Adaptation gain should be reduced if:

* exposure is too weak
* repeated same exposure
* player is overleveled
* source is artificial/farmed
* no actual harm/risk occurred

⸻

28. Adaptation resistance levels

Use resistance levels:

Stage	Name	Meaning
0	none	no special resistance
1	familiarity	can recognize/handle minor exposure better
2	tolerance	mild reduction
3	resistance	clear reduction
4	strong resistance	major reduction
5	high resistance	severe exposure reduced
6	conditional immunity	immune to specific narrow source
7	broad resistance	applies to related sources
8	rare immunity	very high-rank and limited
9+	mythic adaptation	story-scale, still needs counters

Example:

Poison Tolerance Rank 3:

* reduces mundane poisons
* does not stop divine venom

Fire Resistance Rank 4:

* reduces normal fire
* dragon fire still dangerous

Adaptation should specify scope.

⸻

29. Adaptation counters

Every adaptation needs counters.

Examples:

Poison resistance counters:

* stronger poison
* magical poison
* soul venom
* unknown toxin
* overwhelming dose

Fire resistance counters:

* dragon fire
* holy fire
* hellfire
* starfire
* sustained exposure
* internal burning

Physical resistance counters:

* armor-piercing
* magic damage
* precision weak point
* crushing force
* soul damage

Curse resistance counters:

* stronger curse
* true name
* divine law
* consent/oath
* artifact curse

No resistance should become universal without explicit mythic design.

⸻

30. Evolution

Evolution is a major body/build/race change.

Evolution can be:

* race evolution
* class evolution
* ability evolution
* body mutation
* monster form evolution
* bloodline awakening
* divine/demonic transformation
* artifact fusion
* specialization evolution

Evolution should require:

* threshold progress
* strong source
* story event
* validation
* cost/risk
* new limits
* body/resource recalculation
* card/context updates
* audit record

Evolution should not happen from one vague phrase.

Example:

“You feel more monstrous.”

Not enough.

“The slime core fuses with yours, your status changes to Acid Slime, and your body begins secreting weak acid.”

Potentially enough if source is valid and transformation rules allow it.

⸻

31. Evolution progress tracks

AIDRPG can track evolution progress.

Examples:

Evolution Track: Acid Slime
Progress Sources:
- absorbed acid slime core
- survived acid exposure
- practiced acid secretion
- body tolerated acidic mana
Progress: Candidate / Unstable / Stabilizing / Complete
Risks:
- corrosion
- loss of fine control
- friendly equipment damage

Evolution tracks should be used for complex transformations.

Do not make a separate track for every tiny change.

Use when:

* race/body changes
* major passive develops
* monster evolution matters
* build identity shifts
* story card update needed

⸻

32. Evolution stages

Evolution stages:

Stage	Meaning
none	no evolution track
seeded	first compatible source
candidate	possible evolution path
unstable mutation	traits appear temporarily
developing	repeated evidence/progress
threshold ready	enough progress but trigger/cost needed
evolving	transformation active
stabilized	new form/trait confirmed
rejected	evolution fails
corrupted	evolution goes wrong
hybridized	partial blend
sealed	evolution suppressed

This prevents sudden unsupported transformation.

⸻

33. Evolution costs

Evolution may cost:

* HP
* MP
* EP
* time
* sleep/downtime
* cocoon/ritual state
* materials
* essence
* core
* sacrifice
* hunger
* vulnerability
* temporary weakness
* memory
* social consequence
* faction attention
* mutation risk
* trait loss
* body instability

Example:

Slime evolves into Acid Slime:

* consumes acid slime core
* body unstable for a scene
* equipment corrosion risk
* gains weak Acid Secretion candidate
* fire/drying vulnerability may worsen

Evolution should give and take.

34. Bloodline awakening

Bloodline awakening is a special evolution path.

Sources:

* inherited race/species
* ancestry
* prophecy
* stress
* ritual
* age
* teacher
* artifact
* bloodline source
* near-death event
* emotional trigger

Examples:

* dragonkin awakens Ember Breath
* angel-blooded player awakens Light Wings
* demon-blooded player awakens Hellfire
* beastkin awakens Beast Form
* royal blood awakens Command Aura
* ancient hero blood awakens Sword Memory

Awakening rules:

* ancestry must exist or be revealed with evidence
* awakening starts as candidate/unstable unless strong ritual/status confirms
* costs and weaknesses added
* not every bloodline trait awakens at once
* major awakening may change race/body/resources

⸻

35. Soul/core absorption

Soul/core absorption is high-risk.

Sources:

* monster core
* mana crystal
* demon soul
* spirit shard
* dragon heart
* lich phylactery fragment
* divine spark
* world seed

Rules:

* source must exist
* source must be accessible
* source may resist
* source may be contaminated
* source may contain memories/will
* source may be incompatible
* source may attract attention
* source may mutate body/soul
* source may create curse/debt
* source may require ritual processing
* source may be too strong to absorb safely

Soul/core absorption should not be casual inventory use.

38. Temporary versus permanent gains

Every absorption/copy outcome must define duration.

Duration types:

* instant
* one action
* one scene
* until rest
* one day
* until source burns out
* until condition removed
* permanent candidate
* permanent stat XP
* permanent trait after stabilization
* unstable permanent mutation
* sealed power
* recurring but conditional

Temporary buffs are easier to grant than permanent power.

Example:

Drinking ogre blood:

* temporary ATK buff
* nausea/corruption risk
* maybe minor stat XP if compatible

Permanent ogre strength:

* requires repeated source, compatible body, evolution path, or major ritual

⸻

39. Source caps

Each source can provide meaningful growth once.

Source cap examples:

* one monster core = one core absorption event
* one defeated wolf = one devour/essence event
* one observed spell = one observation evidence event
* one boss soul = one major absorption event
* one divine blessing = one blessing record
* one teacher lesson = one lesson reward

Repeated processing of same source should be rejected or give only minor leftover effects.

Operation keys must track source use.

Example:

absorb.core.acid_slime_17.turn_204
copy.observation.shadow_step.enemy_mage.turn_322
adapt.fire.exposure.dragon_breath.turn_811

⸻

40. Scene caps

A scene should not grant unlimited growth from repeated similar actions.

Suggested scene cap:

A scene can grant at most 1 major absorption/copy/adaptation reward per source type unless the danger escalates or a new unique source appears.

Examples:

* absorbing one slime core: valid
* absorbing ten identical weak slimes in same scene: decay heavily
* copying the same spell ten times in one fight: one observation plus maybe extra evidence if conditions change
* adapting to the same weak fire repeatedly: decay

Scene caps prevent spam.

⸻

41. Day/rest caps

For safe training and controlled absorption:

Safe controlled absorption/training should have a daily cap unless special story conditions exist.

This applies to:

* eating weak monsters
* controlled poison exposure
* repeated spell copying
* repeated self-harm adaptation
* training with same technique
* processing stored cores

A major dangerous ritual can bypass ordinary daily caps but adds high risk and story cost.

⸻

42. Level-band caps

Absorption should respect level scale.

Example level-band cap:

Player Level Band	Maximum Normal Permanent Gain From One Non-Unique Source
1–10	small stat XP, rare +1 stat
11–50	moderate stat XP, rare +1–2 stats
51–150	moderate stat XP, trait evidence
151–300	larger sources needed for meaningful gain
301–500	only rare/legendary sources matter
501–800	mythic sources required for major growth
801–1000	world/cosmic sources required for major growth

A Level 900 player should not gain meaningful permanent stats from basic goblins.

They might still gain story, reputation, or moral consequences.

43. Source-tier versus player-level rule

Compare source effective level to player effective level.

Source Compared to Player	Growth Result
far weaker	no meaningful growth, maybe nutrition/flavor
weaker	tiny stat/domain XP
near equal	meaningful stat/domain XP
stronger	strong progress, risk
much stronger	major progress, severe risk
impossible-scale	usually cannot absorb safely without story leverage

Example:

Level 10 player absorbs Tier 5 ogre core:

* strong potential
* high risk
* compatibility sickness likely

Level 500 player absorbs same ogre core:

* little permanent growth
* maybe material or flavor
* no major stat gain

44. Risk table

Absorption/copy/adaptation should often carry risk.

Risk level:

Risk	Meaning
none	safe, minor source
low	discomfort or small cost
moderate	condition/resource cost possible
high	injury, mutation, corruption, sickness
severe	major body/soul risk
catastrophic	death-boundary, possession, permanent transformation
forbidden	should hard-deny without major story support

Risk increases when:

* source is stronger
* compatibility is poor
* source is corrupted
* source is divine/demonic/cosmic
* player lacks ability
* player is injured/exhausted
* ritual is rushed
* source resists
* player overcharges
* source is unstable
* environment is hostile

High reward should usually mean high risk.

46. Adaptation versus immunity safeguard

The system must avoid universal immunity.

Bad:

After being burned once, player is immune to fire.

Good:

After surviving several low-grade fire exposures, player gains Fire Tolerance Rank 1, reducing minor mundane fire discomfort.

Resistance must specify:

* source type
* severity covered
* current rank
* exceptions
* counters
* growth path

Example:

Fire Tolerance Rank 2:
Reduces minor mundane flame and heat.
Does not protect against dragon fire, hellfire, divine flame, lava, or sustained burning.

⸻

47. Copy versus ownership safeguard

Copying an ability does not mean stealing it permanently from source unless the ability is power theft.

Observation copy:

* source keeps ability
* player gets candidate/evidence

Power theft:

* source may lose power temporarily/permanently
* requires stronger validation
* may create moral/social consequences
* target may resist
* may be illegal/evil/hostile depending world

Do not conflate copy and theft.

* long adaptation

It may grant:

* knowledge of regeneration
* clue about fire weakness

Mundane skills can be learned more normally.

* Sword Domain XP
* Counter technique candidate
* no instant master-level counter

Quick Learner may improve this, but anti-farm still applies.

⸻

50. Quick Learner and similar passives

Quick Learner-type abilities are important.

They may improve:

* evidence gain
* domain XP
* stat XP
* ability stabilization speed
* learning from failure
* copying mundane techniques
* understanding patterns

But Quick Learner must not:

* grant instant mastery
* bypass source requirements
* bypass anti-farm
* make impossible body powers possible
* remove costs
* ignore level/tier gaps
* create stable abilities from one weak hint

Suggested Quick Learner effect:

Increases learning rewards from meaningful new experiences by 10–50% depending rank.
Does not apply to trivial repetition.
Does not bypass compatibility.
Does not convert candidates into stable abilities without evidence.

At high rank, Quick Learner may allow faster stabilization, but still needs meaningful evidence.

Adaptive powers can be strong, but must be tracked.

62. Absorption validation outcomes

Accept

Valid source, compatible enough, gain committed.

Example:

Absorbed slime core grants Slime Body XP and Acid Secretion evidence.

Reject

Source or compatibility insufficient.

Example:

Human eats normal wolf meat and claims wolf stats.

Pending

Possible but not confirmed.

Example:

Strange core pulses; absorption may be possible after analysis.

Soft_fail

Partial gain with cost.

Example:

Dragon core rejects player but leaves Ember Breath candidate and severe fever.

Hard_deny

Unsafe/impossible/non-canon.

Examples:

* absorbing absent dragon
* copying god power from rumor
* gaining infinite stats from rats
* card text grants evolution
* dream devour affects real body without support

63. Absorption safety rules

63.1 No source, no gain

There must be a real source.

63.2 Source must be available

Defeated, accessible, observed, consumed, touched, contracted, or otherwise connected.

63.3 Source is spent or capped

Same source cannot be farmed repeatedly.

63.4 Weak sources decay

Far weaker enemies give little/no growth.

63.5 Absorption usually gives stat XP, not raw stats

Direct stat points are rare.

63.6 Copying gives candidates, not mastery

Observed powers start lower than source.

63.7 Passives are hard to copy

Body traits require body change, adaptation, ritual, or absorption.

63.8 Adaptation is specific

No universal immunity from one exposure.

63.9 Strong source means strong risk

Dragon/god/demon/cosmic sources are dangerous.

63.10 Compatibility matters

Wrong body/source can fail or corrupt.

63.11 Anti-farm always applies

Repeated safe actions decay.

63.12 Self-created source farming is blocked

Summons, illusions, or generated weak sources cannot produce full rewards.

63.13 Cards do not grant powers

Cards mirror confirmed state only.

63.14 Evolution requires thresholds

No race/body transformation from vague flavor.

63.15 Every gain must be auditable

The player should know source, gain, cost, and risk.

66. Final Chunk 26 doctrine

Anime-style growth is allowed.

Devourers can grow.

Mimics can copy.

Adapters can resist.

Bloodlines can awaken.

Slimes can assimilate.

Vampires can feed.

Constructs can upgrade.

Dragonkin can awaken breath.

Demons, angels, spirits, and gods can grant dangerous power.

But every gain needs a real source.

Every source needs availability.

Every source can be used only within caps.

Weak sources decay.

Compatibility matters.

Risk matters.

Most gains become stat XP, domain XP, ability evidence, candidates, temporary buffs, or evolution progress.

Direct stat points are rare.

Stable abilities require evidence.

Passives are hard to copy.

Adaptation is specific, not universal immunity.

Evolution requires thresholds and story support.

Divine, demonic, and cosmic sources are powerful but dangerous.

Story Cards do not grant powers.

AI narration does not bypass validation.

Quick Learner helps learning but does not erase limits.

The goal is to support exciting anime-style progression while keeping AIDRPG coherent, fair, auditable, and safe from infinite freeform power inflation.
