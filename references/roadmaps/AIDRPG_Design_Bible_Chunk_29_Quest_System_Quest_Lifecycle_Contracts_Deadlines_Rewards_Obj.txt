AIDRPG Design Bible — Chunk 29: Quest System, Quest Lifecycle, Contracts, Deadlines, Rewards, Objective Validation, and Quest Archive

1. Purpose of this chunk

Chunk 29 defines how AIDRPG handles quests, objectives, contracts, bounties, job requests, task chains, deadlines, quest rewards, failure states, and quest archives.

Earlier chunks defined:

* player scaling
* stats and resources
* XP and progression
* enemy scaling
* combat
* abilities
* absorption
* crafting
* economy
* labor and project costs

Chunk 29 answers:

How does a quest become real?

How does AIDRPG know what the player is trying to do?

How are quest objectives tracked?

How are partial objectives handled?

How are quest rewards validated?

How do contracts differ from quests?

How do deadlines work?

How does the system stop duplicate quest rewards?

How does it prevent fake quest completion?

How does it archive completed, failed, abandoned, or obsolete quests?

How does freeform AI Dungeon narration update quests without scrambling them?

The central rule is:

A quest is a structured promise, goal, problem, obligation, or opportunity that must be tracked through evidence.

Quests should guide gameplay without forcing the story into rigid rails.

⸻

2. Core philosophy

AI Dungeon often creates goals naturally.

The player may encounter:

* “Find my missing daughter.”
* “Kill the wolves near the mill.”
* “Deliver this letter.”
* “Investigate the murdered merchant.”
* “Repair the village wall.”
* “Gather ten slime cores.”
* “Escort the caravan.”
* “Pay back your debt by next week.”
* “Defeat Frostmaw.”
* “Explore the ruined tower.”
* “Craft a cure.”
* “Build a forge.”
* “Prove the guard captain is corrupt.”
* “Recover the stolen ring.”
* “Keep the workers paid.”
* “Destroy the demon seal.”
* “Protect the town until dawn.”
* “Join the tournament.”
* “Prepare for the dragon attack.”

AIDRPG must support these.

But it must avoid:

* accepting every vague sentence as a quest
* duplicating the same quest under different names
* marking quests complete without proof
* granting rewards twice
* losing quest progress
* inventing quest objectives from Story Cards
* letting rumors become confirmed objectives
* confusing contracts, quests, cases, domain projects, and personal goals
* failing to track deadlines
* leaving completed quests active forever
* letting AI Dungeon randomly say “quest complete” when the state does not support it

The system should track quests clearly but compactly.

4. Quest types

AIDRPG should classify quests.

Personal goal

A player-created goal.

Example:

“I want to become a master swordsman.”

Usually no formal reward unless the story creates one.

Request quest

An NPC asks the player for help.

Example:

“Please find my missing son.”

Contract quest

A formal agreement with payment, deadline, and terms.

Example:

“Escort the caravan to Greyford for 50 gold.”

Bounty

A reward for capturing, killing, proving, or recovering something.

Example:

“Bring proof that Frostmaw is dead.”

Investigation case

A mystery requiring clues and evidence.

Example:

“Find who murdered the merchant.”

Tracked mainly by InvestigationReducer but may have quest wrapper.

Delivery quest

Move item/message/person to destination.

Example:

“Deliver this sealed letter to Captain Mora.”

Collection quest

Gather specified materials/items.

Example:

“Bring 10 slime cores.”

Kill/defeat quest

Defeat, rout, capture, or neutralize target.

Example:

“Clear the wolves from the mill.”

Escort/protection quest

Keep person/group/object safe.

Example:

“Escort the caravan.”

Crafting quest

Make, repair, upgrade, or obtain an item.

Example:

“Craft a cure for the plague.”

Domain quest

Improve, defend, repair, or manage a base/holding.

Example:

“Repair the north wall before winter.”

Exploration quest

Find, map, scout, or enter a location.

Example:

“Explore the ruined tower.”

Social quest

Persuade, negotiate, prove, expose, reconcile, or gain trust.

Example:

“Convince the council to fund the defenses.”

Survival quest

Survive a condition or deadline.

Example:

“Hold the gate until dawn.”

Training quest

Reach a skill, domain, level, or ability milestone.

Example:

“Stabilize Mana Slash.”

Main quest / arc quest

Major story objective.

Example:

“Stop the demon seal from breaking.”

Quest type affects objectives and validation.

6. Quest status states

Quests should use clear statuses.

Status	Meaning
Rumored	heard about it, not accepted or confirmed
Offered	NPC/faction offered it
Available	known and can be accepted
Active	player accepted or is pursuing
In Progress	progress has been made
Blocked	cannot progress until blocker solved
Ready to Turn In	completion done, reward/closure pending
Completed	quest objective resolved
Rewarded	reward paid/applied
Failed	failure condition occurred
Abandoned	player dropped it
Expired	deadline passed
Superseded	replaced by newer quest/state
Cancelled	giver/conditions removed it
Archived	no longer active; stored for history

Important:

“Completed” and “Rewarded” are different.

A quest can be completed but not yet paid.

Example:

Player kills the wolves.

Quest is Ready to Turn In.

Reward occurs when Old Bren pays or otherwise confirms reward.

7. Quest priority

Quest priority helps ContextBuilder.

Suggested priorities:

Priority	Meaning
background	personal/long-term, rarely injected
low	optional or minor
normal	active ordinary quest
high	current important quest
urgent	deadline/danger immediate
main	major story arc
critical	immediate survival/world-state issue

Context should include:

* critical quests
* urgent deadlines
* current location-relevant quests
* current scene objectives
* quests with nearby target
* quests blocked by current action
* active turn-in opportunities

Do not inject every quest every turn.

8. Quest creation rules

A quest may be created when:

* NPC explicitly requests action
* player accepts a task
* official contract/bounty exists
* story establishes a clear objective
* player declares a personal goal
* system/scenario starts with active quest
* clue/case creates investigation objective
* domain project begins
* deadline/obligation is established
* tutorial/system objective appears

Quest should not be created from:

* random flavor text
* rumors without player interest
* Story Card text alone
* examples
* debug output
* command output
* dream-only content
* hypothetical plans
* vague mood statements
* every minor errand unless relevant

Example:

“The innkeeper mentions wolves near the mill.”

Create:

* rumor or opportunity, not active quest

“The innkeeper asks you to clear the wolves and promises 15 gold.”

Create:

* offered quest

Player says:

“I accept.”

Status:

* active

9. Quest acceptance

Acceptance can be explicit or implied.

Explicit acceptance:

* “I accept the job.”
* “I’ll do it.”
* signing contract
* taking quest item
* receiving advance payment
* agreeing to terms

Implied acceptance:

* player begins pursuing objective
* player takes delivery package
* player leaves with escort target
* player starts investigating at request
* player assigns workers to domain project

Do not assume acceptance from merely hearing about something.

Example:

NPC says:

“Someone should kill the rats.”

Player walks away.

No active quest.

Maybe rumored/available.

12. Count objectives

Collection and kill quests often require counts.

Count objective rules:

* required count must be explicit or inferred carefully
* current count updates only from validated events
* duplicate events do not increment twice
* source identity matters
* target type matters
* quality/condition may matter
* proof may matter
* partial counts should persist

Example:

Quest requires 10 slime cores.

Player has 4 validated slime cores.

Output repeats “you collect another slime core.”

If duplicate event:

* no second count

If new validated core:

* count becomes 5

If player has 10 but two are cracked and quest requires intact cores:

* valid count may be 8

13. Defeat objectives

Defeat does not always mean kill.

Defeat objective should define acceptable resolution.

Possible resolutions:

* killed
* captured
* routed
* banished
* sealed
* negotiated away
* scared off
* disarmed
* neutralized
* cured
* exorcised
* escaped by target but threat removed
* proven innocent/wrong target

Example:

“Clear the wolves from the mill.”

Acceptable:

* wolves killed
* wolves driven away
* alpha tamed/relocated
* food source restored so wolves leave

Not automatically required:

* kill every wolf

Example:

“Bring me Frostmaw’s head.”

Requires:

* specific kill/proof

Quest wording matters.

16. Investigation quest objectives

Investigation objectives require evidence.

Examples:

* find clue
* interview witness
* compare testimony
* prove suspect lied
* recover evidence
* identify culprit
* present proof
* solve case

Important:

Investigation truth should not be created by high INT alone.

Evidence must exist.

Quest completion requires:

* enough evidence
* correct conclusion
* accepted authority/giver response if needed
* optional arrest/trial resolution if part of quest

If player accuses culprit without proof:

* maybe social event
* not solved-case completion

InvestigationReducer owns clue/evidence truth.

QuestReducer tracks quest wrapper.

17. Crafting quest objectives

Crafting quests require item output validation.

Example:

“Craft a cure.”

Objectives may be:

1. Identify illness.
2. Find recipe.
3. Gather herbs.
4. Brew cure.
5. Test cure.
6. Deliver/administer cure.

Completion requires:

* cure item exists
* correct potency
* delivered to correct target
* effect works or accepted by quest terms

A failed or weak potion may create partial success, complication, or quest failure.

CraftingReducer and InventoryReducer own item truth.

QuestReducer owns objective status.

20. Contract acceptance

Contract acceptance should require stronger evidence than informal quests.

Valid acceptance:

* signature
* verbal agreement witnessed
* taking deposit
* receiving contract item
* explicit “I accept”
* magically binding oath
* starting work after agreeing terms

If terms are unclear, contract may be proposed but not active.

Example:

Merchant says:

“I could pay someone 50 gold to escort this caravan.”

This is offer, not active contract.

Player says:

“I accept and take the 10 gold deposit.”

Contract active.

Economy commits deposit.

Quest active.

21. Deposits and advances

Some quests/contracts pay upfront.

Advance payment rules:

* money/item is transferred immediately if validated
* contract obligation is created
* failure may require repayment
* advance cannot be claimed twice
* deposit may be escrow or retained
* reputation/legal consequences if player runs away

Example:

Player receives 10 gold advance.

If quest fails:

* may owe refund
* may lose reputation
* may face debt/contract breach

EconomyReducer owns payment.

ContractReducer owns obligation.

23. Reward promise versus reward payment

A quest may have promised rewards.

Example:

Old Bren promises 15 gold.

Quest record:

Promised Reward: 15 gold
Reward Status: promised

After completion and turn-in:

Reward Status: paid
Currency +15 gold

If giver refuses:

Reward Status: disputed

If giver dies before payment:

Reward Status: unpaid/lost/claimable from estate maybe

This avoids giving rewards before they happen.

⸻

24. Reward validation

Reward can be paid only when:

* quest completion is validated
* giver/source exists or reward container exists
* player is eligible
* reward is available
* no duplicate payment
* payment source can pay
* contract terms support payment
* inventory/currency transfer is valid

Reward should be rejected if:

* quest not complete
* wrong target
* no proof
* fake proof
* giver absent/dead without alternate payout
* already rewarded
* reward only rumored
* Story Card says reward but state does not
* AI output contradicts contract terms without support

⸻

25. Partial rewards

Partial success may yield partial rewards.

Examples:

Escort quest:

* all cargo delivered: full reward
* people safe but cargo lost: partial reward
* arrived late: reduced reward
* target injured: penalty

Collection quest:

* 7 of 10 herbs delivered: partial pay or no completion depending terms

Bounty:

* target dead but proof damaged: reduced/disputed reward

Investigation:

* culprit identified but escaped: partial reward, case not fully closed

Domain repair:

* wall stabilized but not fully repaired: partial funding

Partial rewards should be contract/quest-specific.

⸻

26. Quest failure

Quests can fail.

Failure conditions:

* deadline missed
* protected target dies
* quest item destroyed/lost
* wrong target killed
* giver dies/cancels
* player betrays contract
* enemy escapes permanently
* required proof destroyed
* domain project collapses
* case evidence lost
* faction relation makes quest impossible
* player abandons long enough
* world state changes
* objective becomes impossible

Failure should be validated.

Do not fail quest from vague “things look bad.”

Failure can create:

* reputation loss
* debt
* penalty
* new quest
* combat
* legal trouble
* grief
* reduced reward
* alternate branch

Failure should not always mean game over.

⸻

27. Quest abandonment

Player may abandon quests.

Abandonment can be:

* explicit
* implied by leaving for too long
* abandoning target
* refusing contract
* destroying quest item
* joining opposing faction

Abandonment effects depend on quest.

Minor personal goal:

* no penalty

Formal contract:

* breach penalty

Escort:

* target risk/reputation loss

Domain project:

* sunk costs/materials lost

Investigation:

* case remains unsolved

Abandoned quests should move to archive or inactive list unless still relevant.

29. Deadline warning thresholds

Context should warn when deadlines are relevant.

Suggested thresholds:

* deadline soon
* deadline today
* deadline in hours
* deadline imminent
* missed deadline

Example Context:

The caravan contract deadline is tonight; if you do not reach Greyford by then, payment may be reduced or disputed.

Do not inject every future deadline constantly.

Prioritize current and urgent deadlines.

32. Quest branching

Quests can branch.

Branch examples:

* kill wolves
* drive wolves away
* discover wolves are starving
* tame alpha
* expose miller attracting wolves
* negotiate with druid
* fail and village loses mill

Branching should be tracked without duplicating quest.

Use objectives/notes/branch field.

Example:

Branch: Nonlethal resolution
New objective: Find why wolves are attacking the mill.

Do not create entirely separate duplicate quests unless story truly splits.

⸻

33. Hidden objectives

Some objectives may be hidden.

Examples:

* protect evidence integrity
* avoid killing sacred animal
* discover true culprit
* keep seal intact
* maintain trust
* prevent witness death
* avoid alerting guards

Hidden objectives should not be shown as confirmed unless discovered.

They may affect rewards/outcome.

AIDRPG should be careful not to spoil hidden objectives through commands unless debug mode explicitly shows hidden state.

Player-facing /quests should normally hide them or show “unknown complications.”

40. Quest and combat connection

Combat can progress quests when linked.

Examples:

* defeat target
* survive attack
* protect NPC
* capture enemy
* clear route
* retrieve item after combat
* discover clue from enemy
* force enemy retreat

Combat alone should not update unrelated quests.

Example:

Killing random wolf does not complete Frostmaw quest.

Killing wolves near Old Mill may progress “Clear Mill Wolves.”

CombatResolver proposes quest progress.

QuestReducer validates objective link.

44. Quest and domain connection

Domain projects often have quest wrappers.

Examples:

* build wall before winter
* repair mine
* fortify base
* house refugees
* restore watermill
* expand workshop
* protect harvest

DomainReducer tracks project progress.

QuestReducer tracks objective and rewards.

A domain quest can fail if:

* project deadline missed
* funds exhausted
* workers flee
* attack destroys site
* materials unavailable
* political permission revoked

⸻

45. Quest and reputation connection

Quest outcomes can affect reputation.

Positive:

* completed as promised
* protected civilians
* delivered early
* solved case truthfully
* paid workers
* spared enemies
* returned stolen goods

Negative:

* failed contract
* lied about proof
* killed wrong target
* abandoned escort
* stole reward
* harmed civilians
* sold quest item
* missed deadline
* betrayed faction

Reputation changes should be scoped:

* local
* faction
* public
* secret
* rumor
* personal actor trust

⸻

46. Quest and XP connection

Quest completion may reward XP.

ProgressionReducer owns XP.

QuestReducer proposes:

* objective XP
* completion XP
* optional objective XP
* failure survival XP
* investigation XP
* social XP
* domain XP

XP should depend on:

* difficulty
* risk
* player level
* story importance
* anti-farm
* contribution
* objective completed
* duplicate checks

Do not grant XP twice for same quest.

Quest completion XP and combat XP may both apply, but must not double-count the same accomplishment excessively.

Rewards need operation keys.

quest.complete.clear_mill_wolves.turn_142

Separate keys for:

* objective progress
* quest completion
* reward payment
* XP reward
* item reward
* reputation change
* contract deposit
* contract final payment

This prevents duplication and makes audit clear.

⸻

48. Quest duplication prevention

Duplicate quest detection should compare:

* title
* quest giver
* target
* location
* objective
* related item
* related actor
* related contract
* quest type
* active status
* canonical key

Example duplicates:

* “Clear the Mill Wolves”
* “Wolves at the Old Mill”
* “Old Bren’s Wolf Problem”

These may be the same quest.

If duplicate detected:

* merge aliases
* update existing quest
* do not create new quest

If separate:

* track separately

Example:

“Clear wolves from Old Mill” and “Hunt Frostmaw” may be linked but not identical if Frostmaw is alpha.

⸻

49. Quest title naming rules

Quest names should be clear and specific.

Bad quest titles:

* Quest
* Task
* Help
* Wolves
* Go there
* Do the thing
* You must hurry
* Old text from AI
* Debug quest
* Story Card quest
* “Find out what happens next”

Good quest titles:

* Clear the Mill Wolves
* Deliver Bren’s Letter
* Investigate the Merchant’s Murder
* Repair the North Wall
* Gather Ten Slime Cores
* Escort the Greyford Caravan
* Craft the Fever Cure
* Recover the Stolen Ring
* Pay Mara’s Repair Debt

Titles should be concise and stable.

Aliases can preserve alternate names.

⸻

50. Quest card eligibility

Most quests should stay in protected Quest Log.

Generated quest cards should be created only for:

* major ongoing quests
* main story arcs
* complex multi-stage quests
* long-term contracts
* major domain quests
* important investigation arcs
* quests with many linked actors/places
* quests that AI Dungeon must remember reliably

Do not create generated cards for:

* every minor errand
* every completed quest
* every small collection task
* every temporary objective
* every rumor
* every failed minor task

Protected Quest Log should handle most quest state.

⸻

Archive entries should include:

⸻

55. Quest archive use

Archive is used for:

* remembering past accomplishments
* preventing duplicate quest rewards
* title/reputation validation
* actor relationship context
* world-state continuity
* future callbacks
* avoiding re-offering same completed quest
* tracking unpaid/disputed rewards

Archive should not be injected every turn.

Only relevant archived quests should appear in Context.

Example:

If player returns to Old Mill, archived wolf quest may be relevant.

If player meets Old Bren, prior quest may be relevant.

⸻

56. Quest reactivation

Some archived quests can reactivate.

Examples:

* wolves return
* unpaid reward disputed
* solved case reopened
* old enemy escaped
* contract breach resurfaces
* repaired wall damaged again
* cult returns
* missing person found alive
* delivered item was fake

Reactivation should create:

* new quest linked to archive
* or reopen old quest with status superseded/reactivated

Do not erase old outcome.

Example:

Old quest:

Clear the Mill Wolves — completed.

New quest:

Investigate the Wolves’ Return — linked sequel.

⸻

57. Quest uncertainty

Some quest facts may be uncertain.

Uncertain quest facts:

* rumor source
* target identity
* true culprit
* real reward
* hidden deadline
* item authenticity
* employer honesty
* objective morality
* whether target is guilty
* whether creature is actually threat
* whether contract terms are fair

Quest records should distinguish:

* confirmed
* claimed
* rumored
* suspected
* false
* unknown

Example:

Claimed:

“Bren says wolves killed his sheep.”

Confirmed:

“Player found wolf tracks and sheep remains.”

Suspected:

“Tracks may be unusually large.”

This avoids treating NPC statements as absolute truth.

⸻

58. Quest truth validation

Quest updates need evidence.

Valid evidence:

* direct narration
* player action confirmed by AI output
* command/manual override
* inventory state
* actor dialogue if treated as claim
* discovered clue
* completed combat event
* completed crafting project
* completed domain progress
* contract record
* economy transaction
* time/deadline state

Invalid evidence:

* Story Card text alone
* Context text
* debug output
* examples
* player wish without result
* rumor as confirmed fact
* dream-only event
* duplicate output
* hallucinated “quest complete”
* unsupported AI status panel

Example:

AI says:

“Quest complete!”

But objective state shows target escaped and proof missing.

Expected:

* reject or mark disputed/pending
* no reward

⸻

59. Quest failure versus complication

Not every setback is failure.

Examples:

Setback:

* target escaped but can be tracked
* item stolen but recoverable
* witness refuses to talk
* deadline extended
* potion failed but recipe improved
* wall project delayed

Failure:

* target dies when protection objective required survival
* item destroyed beyond recovery
* deadline passes with stated failure
* giver cancels and reward impossible
* quest target permanently leaves
* evidence destroyed and case cannot proceed
* contract breached

Use complication when quest can continue.

Use failure when completion condition becomes impossible or contract defines failure.

⸻

60. Quest moral ambiguity

Some quests may be morally complex.

Examples:

* kill “monster” that is intelligent or innocent
* recover stolen item that was stolen back from tyrant
* expose culprit but harm victim’s family
* protect caravan carrying slaves
* repair wall for oppressive lord
* hunt goblins defending home
* cure plague caused by employer

AIDRPG should not force simple success/failure.

Quest branches can include:

* refuse quest
* investigate truth
* side with target
* renegotiate
* expose giver
* complete differently
* accept lower reward
* gain/lose reputation with different factions

Moral ambiguity is story state, not system confusion.

⸻

61. Quest and player agency

Quest system should guide, not railroad.

Player can:

* accept
* refuse
* ignore
* abandon
* negotiate
* betray
* solve differently
* ask for more reward
* investigate giver
* change objective
* complete nonlethally
* outsource
* pay others
* report to authority
* fail and continue

QuestReducer should track consequences of player choices.

It should not force “intended” route if freeform story validates another route.

⸻

62. Quest outsourcing

Players may delegate quest work.

Examples:

* hire guards to clear wolves
* pay workers to repair wall
* send companion to deliver letter
* ask alchemist to brew cure
* hire investigator
* assign foreman
* summon scout

Outsourcing requires:

* delegate exists
* delegate is willing/paid/commanded
* capability appropriate
* time passes
* risk to delegate
* success not guaranteed
* reward/contribution adjusted
* quest terms allow it

Player may receive less XP if they did not participate directly.

But quest completion/reputation/payment may still occur.

Contracts may forbid subcontracting.

⸻

63. Quest reward distribution

If party or workers help, rewards may be split.

Reward split depends on:

* contract terms
* party agreement
* wages
* shares
* ownership
* contribution
* betrayal
* social expectations

Examples:

* mercenaries paid wages, player keeps bounty
* adventuring party splits reward evenly
* companion refuses payment
* guild takes tax
* foreman and workers paid separately

AIDRPG should not assume player keeps 100% of all rewards if agreement says otherwise.

Context should include quest info only when useful.

Include:

* current active objective
* immediate deadline
* relevant quest item/custody
* nearby target/place
* current blocker
* ready turn-in
* contract obligation
* failure risk

Do not include:

* every old quest
* every archived quest
* every promised reward
* every minor background goal

Good Context:

You are currently pursuing Clear the Mill Wolves. Two of five wolves are confirmed defeated, and the alpha is still active near the Old Mill.

Good Context:

Bad Context:

Inject all 27 quest records, including completed and abandoned errands.

71. Quest acceptance tests

Test 1 — rumor does not become active quest

Given:

NPC mentions wolves near the mill.

Expected:

* rumor/opportunity created at most
* no active quest until request/acceptance

Test 2 — offered quest becomes active after acceptance

Given:

Old Bren asks player to clear wolves for 15 gold.

Player says “I accept.”

Expected:

* quest active
* promised reward recorded
* no payment yet

Test 3 — completion and reward are separate

Given:

Player defeats wolves.

Expected:

* quest ready to turn in or completed
* reward unpaid until Bren pays

Test 4 — reward paid once

Given:

Bren pays 15 gold.

Same output repeats.

Expected:

* gold added once
* reward status paid
* duplicate skipped

Test 5 — wrong target does not complete quest

Given:

Quest is to kill Frostmaw.

Player kills ordinary wolf.

Expected:

* no Frostmaw completion
* maybe unrelated wolf event

Test 6 — count objective tracks correctly

Given:

Quest requires 10 slime cores.

Player collects 4.

Expected:

* count 4/10
* duplicate collection does not increase count twice

Test 7 — damaged quest items count differently

Given:

Quest requires 10 intact slime cores.

Player has 8 intact and 2 cracked.

Expected:

* valid count 8/10 unless giver accepts cracked cores

Test 8 — delivery requires custody

Given:

Player no longer has sealed letter.

Input says player delivers it.

Expected:

* reject unless letter returned or alternate delivery validated

Test 9 — tampered delivery affects reward

Given:

Player opens sealed letter before delivery.

Expected:

* delivery may still complete
* reward/reputation/contract affected

Test 10 — escort target death fails quest

Given:

Escort target dies and quest required survival.

Expected:

* quest failed or severe branch
* reward reduced/void
* reputation/contract consequences possible

Test 11 — escort injury is partial success, not failure

Given:

Escort target arrives injured but alive.

Expected:

* completion possible
* partial reward/penalty possible

Test 12 — investigation needs evidence

Given:

Player guesses culprit correctly but has no evidence.

Expected:

* no solved-case completion
* possible theory noted

Test 13 — crafting quest needs valid item

Given:

Player needs cure but crafts weak tonic.

Expected:

* partial progress or failed attempt
* quest not complete unless cure works/accepted

Test 14 — domain quest needs project progress

Given:

Player says wall is repaired without materials/workers/time.

Expected:

* reject completion
* requirements/blockers remain

Test 15 — contract deposit creates obligation

Given:

Player accepts caravan contract and receives 10 gold deposit.

Expected:

* money added once
* contract active
* obligation and deadline recorded

Test 16 — missed deadline

Given:

Caravan arrives after deadline.

Expected:

* contract/quest outcome adjusted
* reward may reduce/dispute
* not automatic full reward

Test 17 — abandoned contract has penalty

Given:

Player abandons active paid contract.

Expected:

* contract breach
* debt/reputation/penalty possible

Test 18 — quest duplicate merges

Given:

AI creates “Wolves at Old Mill” after “Clear the Mill Wolves” already exists.

Expected:

* alias/merge
* no duplicate active quest

Test 19 — hidden objective not spoiled

Given:

Quest has hidden objective “do not kill sacred wolf.”

Player uses /quests.

Expected:

* hidden objective not revealed unless discovered/debug mode

Test 20 — archive prevents repeat reward

Given:

Completed quest is offered again due to AI drift.

Expected:

* archive detects completed state
* no duplicate quest/reward unless new sequel/reactivation is valid

72. Quest safeguards

72.1 Quest creation requires a real source

Do not create active quests from random flavor or card text.

72.2 Acceptance matters

Hearing about a task is not accepting it.

72.3 Objectives must be explicit and trackable

Vague goals should become personal goals or notes, not rigid quests.

72.4 Completion requires evidence

Do not accept “quest complete” without objective support.

72.5 Rewards are separate from promises

Promised reward is not paid reward.

72.6 Rewards pay once

OperationLedger blocks duplicates.

72.7 Quest items need custody tracking

Delivery and proof require actual item access.

72.8 Counts must be deduped

Collection/kill/proof counts update once per validated source.

72.9 Contracts track obligations

Deposits, deadlines, penalties, and payment terms matter.

72.10 Deadlines use Time system

No vague missed deadline unless time supports it.

72.11 Failure should be validated

Setbacks are not always failure.

72.12 Quest branches are allowed

Player can solve creatively if story supports it.

72.13 Hidden objectives should not be spoiled

Unless debug/manual mode.

72.14 Quests should not overfill Context

Inject only relevant quest info.

72.15 Archive completed/failed quests

Do not leave old quests active forever.

72.16 Archive prevents duplicate rewards

Past outcomes matter.

72.17 Social consequences need awareness

Reputation changes require witnesses, evidence, or rumor spread.

72.18 Outsourcing is allowed but tracked

Delegates need time, skill, risk, and payment.

72.19 Quest cards mirror state only

Cards do not create quest truth.

72.20 Every quest update should be auditable

The player should understand why progress, completion, failure, or reward happened.

⸻

73. Final Chunk 29 doctrine

Quests are structured goals.

Contracts are structured obligations.

Cases are evidence-driven investigations.

Domain quests are project-linked objectives.

Bounties require proof.

Delivery quests require custody.

Escort quests require protected target state.

Crafting quests require valid crafted outputs.

Collection quests require counted items.

Defeat quests require correct target and acceptable resolution.

Rewards must be promised, earned, and paid separately.

Quest completion requires evidence.

Quest rewards must never duplicate.

Deadlines matter.

Failure must be validated.

Setbacks can become complications instead of failure.

Players can solve quests creatively.

Quests can branch, be abandoned, expire, reactivate, or archive.

Quest logs should guide the player without railroading them.

Story Cards do not create quest truth.

AI Dungeon narration must be validated before it changes quest state.

The goal is a quest system that supports freeform AI Dungeon play while keeping objectives, rewards, contracts, deadlines, proof, and consequences coherent across long campaigns.
